\begin{abstract}

Reusable procedural memory can spare a bounded solver repeated derivation,
but storing a correct procedure does not ensure its applicable use,
improve an implemented system, or recover its lifecycle costs. We present
the \emph{Courtroom loop} as a theory-guided synthesis of versioned
procedural assurance, conditional analyses of reuse, and prospective
evidence collection. Admission-time certification concerns a reusable
procedure or relation within its declared scope; runtime validation
concerns a particular application. Complete system releases and resource
ledgers retain successful, failed, rejected, and unused work. A broader
aim is to motivate a procedural knowledge commons of scoped, versioned
procedures and evidence-bearing relationships.

The theory distinguishes representation, bounded policy-family
accessibility, and realized performance. Standard representation results
and a Shannon--Kullback--Leibler decomposition distinguish conditional
uncertainty from a restricted approximation gap. On FormulaReasoning, the complete question
determines the canonical target, so support can improve computation
without adding conditional Shannon information. Interactive
log-loss/error calibrations require law- and budget-preserving closures.
A separate stage model gives sufficient conditions for useful coverage
to improve full-solution accuracy, explains cross-stage complementarity
and admission-order effects on cumulative performance, and supports
conditional lifecycle break-even calculations.

An evidence framework distinguishes support availability, particular
use, comparative effect, and lifecycle value. Availability and assessed
valid use do not by themselves establish comparative effect or lifecycle
value. We instantiate it in a proposed FormulaReasoning
feasibility protocol with $D=8$ sequential development questions, an
isolated frozen-baseline shadow, sealed replays, and a matched-budget
comparison on $H=4$ prospectively untouched terminal questions. Exact
canonical numerical scores and fallible retained-derivation assessments,
including unassessable judgments and explicitly pending assessments,
remain separate. The protocol is
designed to document one development trajectory and its costs, not to
estimate family optima, identify component effects, or establish
population superiority. No prospective outcomes are reported.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A formula, transformation, or solution strategy can be constructed and
checked once, then instantiated on later questions. Reusable procedural
memory offers a way to replace repeated, fallible reconstruction with
scoped support. The central question is when that support improves
bounded reasoning enough to justify construction, assurance, retrieval,
execution, and maintenance.

A broader motivation is a procedural knowledge commons: a shared,
versioned graph of scoped procedures and relationships, including
dependencies and composition claims. Both nodes and relationships would
carry provenance and assurance evidence. Such a commons could spread
acquisition and assurance costs across uses and contributors, allowing
procedural knowledge to accumulate beyond a single solver or development
trajectory.

Accumulation alone does not answer this question. A correct procedure can
be selected for the wrong target, applied outside its scope, bound to
incorrect inputs, or composed through an invalid relation. Retrieval and
checking consume resources needed elsewhere. A successful answer may
retain an invalid derivation, while an assessed valid application does
not reveal what would have happened without it. These distinctions
motivate the \emph{Courtroom loop}: a theory-guided synthesis connecting
procedural assurance, conditional models of useful reuse, and an
auditable development trajectory.

\paragraph{Information, accessibility, and realization.}

The equations below state distinct conditional claims about information,
useful reuse, and lifecycle value; the information decomposition does not
prove the reuse mechanism. The theory applies to its declared ensembles,
policy families, and comparisons independently of the proposed pilot.
Representation concerns which task-relevant distinctions survive under a
declared source ensemble. Bounded accessibility concerns what a registered
policy family can attain with its observations, tools, and budget.
Realization concerns the particular implemented policy. For a discrete
target $Z$ observed through $X$, fix a common predictor-independent law
$P$ and a nonempty registered family $\mathcal V$ of measurable
probability kernels. Best expected log loss decomposes as
\[
L_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Z\mid X)
=H_P(Z\mid X)+\Gamma_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Z\mid X),
\]
Here $L_{\mathcal V}^{P}$ is the infimum of expected log loss over the
registered predictive family $\mathcal V$, $H_P$ is conditional Shannon
entropy, and $\Gamma_{\mathcal V}^{P}$ is the family's best expected
Kullback--Leibler approximation gap relative to the true conditional
distribution. The decomposition separates uncertainty in the observations
from restrictions on prediction; it is not an implemented solver's gain.
Standard information--distortion and packing--Fano converses
describe representation limits under a specified source--representation
ensemble and decoder-visible state. Observed solver errors alone do not
establish a positive Shannon residual.

FormulaReasoning \citep{li2026formulareasoning} has a canonical target
determined by the complete question. With verified reference function
$f_{\mathrm{FR}}$ and registered canonicalizer $c_Q$,
\begin{equation}
Z=c_Q\!\left(f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q)\right),
\qquad H(Z\mid Q)=0.
\label{eq:intro-formulareasoning-determinacy}
\end{equation}
Determination does not guarantee reliable computation within budget.
Stored procedures can externalize derivations, constrain bindings, or make
compositions executable without adding Shannon information conditional on
the complete question. The counterfactual representation analysis asks a
different question under its own source ensemble.

Interactive policies induce different histories and laws. The
accessibility analysis fixes a common task law and frozen interaction
environment, evaluates each policy under its own law, and takes a family
infimum. General acceptance-set and finite-canonical calibrations connect
best-family log loss and decision error under their respective law- and
budget-preserving closure premises
(Sections~\ref{subsubsec:acceptance-set-accessibility}
and~\ref{subsubsec:formula-reasoning-finite-specialization}). These
conditional connections establish neither a positive accessibility
residual nor improvement by an implemented solver. Realization accounting
keeps family-optimal risk separate from the risk of the chosen policy;
the pilot does not estimate that decomposition.

\paragraph{A conditional mechanism for useful reuse.}

A separate, idealized stage model addresses an implemented mechanism.
Fix a task law, common budget, and decomposition into $m$ semantic stages.
Let $a_{i,t}(q)$ be the conditional probability of choosing applicable
verified support at stage $i$ under store version $t$, and $p_i(q)$ the
baseline route's success probability when selected. The specified
kernels hold at every registered valid history, with $p_i(q)$ common to
the compared controllers. Routing precedes execution and does not require
detecting that the baseline would fail.

Assume sound procedures and relations, correct applicability, binding and
execution, stable stage interfaces, and preserved conditional fallback
behavior and continuation resources. Then
\begin{equation}
s_{i,t}(q)=a_{i,t}(q)+\bigl(1-a_{i,t}(q)\bigr)p_i(q),
\label{eq:intro-stage-reliability}
\end{equation}
and the full-solution success probability and expected accuracy are
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Succ}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t(q)
=\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i,t}(q),
\qquad
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\operatorname{Succ}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t(Q)\right].
\label{eq:intro-solution-accuracy}
\end{equation}
The product follows conditional probabilities along valid prefixes,
without assuming independent stage errors. Useful coverage is a routing
probability, not store size or a count of observed uses.

Nondecreasing useful coverage at every stage for almost every question
gives nondecreasing expected full-solution accuracy under these premises.
If a region of task probability at least $\alpha>0$ has coverage gain at
least $r>0$ at one fixed stage, baseline failure probability at least
$b>0$ there, and pre-update success product at least $\gamma>0$ over the
remaining stages, then
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
\geq\alpha rb\gamma.
\label{eq:intro-verified-reuse-gain}
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{subsec:verified-procedural-growth} derives the bound, its
induced-distribution log-loss interpretation, and an imperfect-use variant.
Finite admission tests and runtime checks can provide evidence relevant
to the assumptions without establishing sound use or stable continuation
uniformly over future questions.

Under the fixed-kernel model, feasible coverage improvements at different
stages have nonnegative mixed accuracy gains. Their marginal benefits can
increase when their useful scopes overlap and other stages can succeed;
alternatives at one stage may instead substitute for each other. Equal
final coverage and conditional mechanisms give equal endpoint accuracy,
but admission orders can yield different intermediate coverage and
cumulative benefit. Section~\ref{subsubsec:reuse-complementarity} gives an
example in which the largest immediate gain is not optimal for cumulative
accuracy, even with equal admission costs. These are conditional
comparisons, not consequences of observing co-use or one admission path.

\paragraph{Lifecycle value.}

Let $g:=\alpha rb\gamma$ be a maintained lower bound on expected
full-solution accuracy gain between the specified releases. In a declared
deployment scenario, suppose incremental expected
cost through $N$ uses is at most $F+Nc_{\mathrm{use}}$, with upfront bound
$F\geq0$, recurring-cost bound $c_{\mathrm{use}}$, and
value $v\geq0$ per additional fully correct solution in the same units.
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\operatorname{NetValue}_N]
\geq N\bigl(vg-c_{\mathrm{use}}\bigr)-F.
\label{eq:intro-reuse-net-value}
\end{equation}
For $vg>c_{\mathrm{use}}$, an integer $N\geq1$ with
$N\geq F/(vg-c_{\mathrm{use}})$ suffices for nonnegative expected net
value. Linearity of expectation requires no independence across uses;
a negative lower bound does not establish unprofitability.
Section~\ref{subsubsec:reuse-lifecycle-value} also treats differing gains
and costs along admission paths.

Resource ledgers retain native quantities, human effort, failed, rejected,
and unused work by phase; unrecorded historical work remains unknown.
Observed expenditure does not establish future benefit: lifecycle
projections separately declare their comparator, valuation, horizon, and
assumptions about gains and costs.

\paragraph{Assurance across a versioned lifecycle.}

Courtroom separates \emph{admission-time certification} of a reusable
motif or relation from \emph{runtime validation} of a particular
application and from external assessment of the submitted solution.
Motifs carry semantic roles, units, applicability conditions, execution
contracts, provenance, and assurance evidence. A relation carries its own
scoped composition claim; admitting two motifs does not certify a link
between them. Certification records the claim, scope, evidence, and
decision rather than conferring unrestricted correctness. Formal
assurance, when available, remains conditional on its specification,
premises, and trusted checking machinery.

The inner loop executes a frozen question-specific policy. The outer
loop examines authorized sealed records, tests general revisions, and
publishes the next release. The complete release $S_t$ includes prompts,
code, tools, retrieval, validators, stores, and resource settings. Store
growth is one part of this whole-system process. Admissions,
replacements, revocations, unused support, unsuccessful revisions, and
human work remain in the record. This makes the realized path
inspectable without treating its bundled changes as identified component
effects.

The distinction between answer and reasoning quality has prior art.
ReCEval evaluates reasoning chains through step correctness and
informativeness, using natural-language-inference and
$\mathcal V$-information-based metrics \citep{prasad2023receval}.
Courtroom's FormulaReasoning protocol instead registers a numerical
contract and a separate assessment of the submitted mathematical
derivation. That assessment is fallible: assessed validity is not a proof
of validity, and unassessable evidence remains explicit. We do not assume
that one reasoning metric or assurance standard transfers unchanged
across domains.

\paragraph{Availability, use, effect, and value.}

Availability asks what support a frozen release contains; use asks what
was selected, bound, executed, and retained; effect asks how performance
differs under a specified task law, endpoint, and budget; value asks
whether future gains justify lifecycle costs under declared premises.
These are distinct questions, not an implication chain.

Application validity concerns compliance with a declared contract;
attribution concerns the difference made by a specified change, which a
trace alone cannot establish.

\paragraph{A prospective feasibility protocol.}

The framework permits registered positive development and evaluation sizes
$D$ and $H$. Section~\ref{sec:pilot-design} proposes a FormulaReasoning
instantiation with $D=8$ sequential development questions and $H=4$
prospectively untouched terminal questions. Registration freezes $S_0$,
selection, ordering, permissions, budgets, evaluation, and stopping rules.
Known historical uses and registered source, generated, or template
siblings are excluded. An isolated frozen-$S_0$ shadow is scheduled beside
each adaptive first contact. Both records seal before permitted
current-reference release to development; shadow outcomes remain unavailable during
adaptation and confirmatory execution.

Official same-item replays measure exposed-item fit. After $S_D$ and the
adaptation ledger freeze, final-development replays and the
$S_0$--$S_D$ terminal comparison proceed without revision. Both batches use
the same registered outer resource cap $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$, applied
field by field; frozen endpoint policies retain their internal stopping
rules. Development budgets may differ by release, so the earlier
trajectory is not a matched-budget component experiment. Confirmatory
archives remain mutually embargoed until all scheduled records seal or
the registered abort-sealing rule closes the partial record. Exact
numerical comparison follows reference-blind normalization and
six-significant-digit canonicalization on a bounded finite grid, with
independently recomputed references. Separate retained-derivation
assessments report assessed validity, invalidity, and unassessable
judgments, with pending work and its process state explicit at each
reporting cutoff. Noncompletion, numerical failure, and assessment coverage
remain distinct records with explicit denominators.

The proposed pilot has one human--API development path, bundled revisions,
and one execution per scheduled cell. Four paired terminal items provide
a small descriptive endpoint comparison. The records can document
applications, retained defects, regressions, revisions, and costs, but do
not identify component effects, population superiority, family optima,
complementarity, or optimal admission order. No prospective results are
claimed here. The theory motivates what to examine without acquiring its
premises from this small study.

\paragraph{Positioning and longer-term motivation.}

Reusable procedural support has a longer history than current LLM
systems. In explanation-based learning, Minton's PRODIGY/EBL evaluated
learned search-control rules by savings when applicable, applicability
frequency, and matching cost; added knowledge could slow problem solving
\citep[Sections~1 and~4.3]{minton1988utility}. This utility problem motivates
charging selection and application work rather than equating store growth
with useful reuse. More recently, Voyager accumulates and retrieves
executable, compositional skills \citep{wang2024voyager}.
ACE and Evo-Memory study evolving context or memory; Skill-Pro, human-guided
systems, and code-evolution approaches develop reusable skills or revise
implementations
\citep{ace2026,wei2025evomemory,mi2026skillpro,aria2025,darwingodel2026}.

Admission and version management also have close precedents. AlphaOPT
builds an experience library for optimization-program formulation, tests
new insights through retrieval and execution replay on their source tasks,
and refines applicability conditions using cross-task evidence
\citep{kong2026alphaopt}. This is a close precedent for scoped, checked
reuse, not a proof of general semantic validity. AlphaOPT v4 also reports
phase-specific token, monetary, and runtime costs for library learning,
diagnosis, refinement, and testing, and discusses amortizing construction
cost over subsequent reuse \citep[Appendix~B.4 and Table~5]{kong2026alphaopt}.
Janus selects
between old and proposed memories; matched-rollout validation estimates a
candidate skill's marginal utility before storage
\citep{chen2026janus,zhang2026coevolving}.
Anything2Skill manages evidence-bearing skill contracts in a versioned bank,
and ChipMEM conditions procedural storage on tool-based verification
\citep{pan2026anything2skill,alrabah2026chipmem}.
Zep Agent Skills compiles verified traces into versioned contracts with
field-level event citations and admission gates, while SkillSentry monitors
and guides skill execution at runtime
\citep{chalef2026learningbydoing,skillsentry2026}.
Procedural Graphs evolves relations among procedures using validation-gated
edits; SPEM stores atomic skills and reusable skill-DAG compositions
\citep{lu2026proceduralgraphs,zhou2026spem}.
These recent reports include preprints, notably ChipMEM, Zep Agent Skills,
Procedural Graphs, and SPEM. Li's lifecycle survey already treats evolving skill libraries in terms of
admission, retrieval/composition, maintenance, lineage, and governance,
and calls for trajectory-aware evaluation \citep{li2026dynamicskills}.
Courtroom specializes that perspective into the assurance distinctions,
conditional calculations, and prospective protocol below; lifecycle
management itself is not a new contribution.

SkillsBench supplies paired no-skill and curated-skill evaluations;
SkillEvolBench and AFTER examine skill evolution and transfer
\citep{li2026skillsbench,skillevolbench2026,belikova2026after}.
Skill Coverage distinguishes exercised behavioral constraints from task
outcomes, while differential analyses document functional and efficiency
regressions, including excessive verification
\citep{tan2026skillcoverage,dong2026skillharm}.
Analyses of memory retrieval address additional resource constraints
\citep{xmemory2026}. Colaco and Lahjouji formulate memory compaction as
a rate--distortion tradeoff between retained information and downstream
utility under a resource budget \citep{colaco2026whattokeep}. Their
compaction perspective is complementary to, not a substitute for, the
distinctions among information availability, bounded accessibility,
particular-application validity, and lifecycle cost here.
Courtroom's contribution is the connection among scoped reusable-object
assurance, particular-application validity, whole-system development,
conditional reuse and lifecycle calculations, and a sealed prospective
protocol. It does not originate reusable stores, admission gates,
versioning, or procedural composition. The pilot does not isolate
certification's incremental value over runtime validation.

Collective skill evolution and reusable skill knowledge bases also
motivate pooling support across contributors
\citep{skillclaw2026,skillx2026}. For such a commons, governance,
maintenance, retrieval interference, correlated failures, and renewed
validation remain concerns. The conditional
calculations identify what would need to hold for such a commons to be
worthwhile; cross-user applicability and lifecycle value need evidence
beyond this pilot.

\paragraph{Contributions and roadmap.}

The paper combines a versioned assurance and accounting architecture, a
layered information framework, conditional reuse and lifecycle
calculations, and a prospective FormulaReasoning protocol. These
components serve different purposes: the architecture specifies authority
and records; the theory states conditional relationships; the protocol
defines observations and their limits.
Section~\ref{sec:courtroom-orientation} gives a short orientation to the
system and its assurance distinctions. Section~\ref{sec:theory} develops
representation, accessibility, realization, reuse, complementarity,
order, and lifecycle value; Section~\ref{subsec:identification-pilot} maps
theoretical quantities to observable evidence.
Section~\ref{sec:the-courtroom-loop} then presents the detailed architecture
and a brief hypothetical example. Section~\ref{sec:pilot-design} specifies the proposed
study; the remaining appendices detail execution and isolation, trace
coding, ledgers, and registration.



\section{Courtroom Orientation}
\label{sec:courtroom-orientation}

The Courtroom loop separates question-specific execution from the
construction and release of reusable procedural support. A stored
\emph{motif} is a scoped procedure with declared input/output roles,
applicability conditions, provenance, and assurance evidence. Relations
carry dependency or composition claims with their own scope and evidence.
Admission certification concerns these reusable claims; runtime validation
concerns a particular selection, binding, composition, or execution.
Neither finite admission tests nor a runtime recommendation are assumed
to be infallible.

Let $S_t$ denote the complete release after development episode $t$, with
$S_0$ the starting release and $M_t:=\operatorname{mem}(S_t)$ its active
procedural store. The release includes the controller, prompts, tools,
retrieval and validation routes, resource limits, and store, rather than
memory alone. The \emph{inner loop} answers a question using one frozen
release, retaining its proposed derivation and sealing its terminal
record. The \emph{outer loop} uses authorized post-seal evidence to propose
and check revisions, admit or reject reusable objects, and freeze the
next release. Thus development question $Q_t$ uses $S_{t-1}$, while a
published revision becomes available only in $S_t$. Empirical releases
may revoke objects as well as add them. Construction, rejected work,
retrieval, checking, execution, and maintenance all retain their costs.

The following section develops the conditional analyses before the
operational detail. It distinguishes information in a declared ensemble,
what a bounded policy family can attain, and what an implemented policy
realizes. Its separate reuse model asks when increased useful coverage
improves full-solution accuracy under explicit sound-use, fallback,
interface, and budget premises. The detailed architecture in
Section~\ref{sec:the-courtroom-loop} specifies the authority and records
that make those premises and their possible failures inspectable; the
architecture does not establish the premises by itself.

\section{Layered Theory of Representation, Accessibility, and Realization}
\label{sec:theory}

The theory gives separate analyses of representation, bounded
accessibility, and implemented realization. These are not successive steps
in a proof that an available procedure improves a solver. Representation
asks what can be recovered from a declared source and representation;
accessibility asks what a registered policy family can attain within
budget; realization concerns the policy actually implemented. Standard
representation converses and the Shannon--KL decomposition supply the
information-theoretic background. Law- and budget-preserving terminal
closures connect optimal log loss with optimal decision error for
interactive policy families.

A separate stage model supplies a sufficient mechanism for useful reuse:
increased useful coverage can improve expected full-solution accuracy when
its local sound-use, fallback, and continuation premises hold. We derive
gain and complementarity calculations, distinguish endpoint from cumulative
performance, and connect justified gains to conditional lifecycle value.
The family-level calibration does not establish the stage-model premises,
and the gain calculation does not establish future costs or value.

These results are self-contained conditional analyses, not quantities
estimated by the proposed pilot. The trajectory definitions specify which
outcomes and resources a prospective study can report. The final
subsection connects each theoretical claim to its premises and evidential
limits, complementing the introduction's availability--use--effect--value
map.

\paragraph{Conventions and common acceptance rule.}

All relevant spaces are standard Borel, and all kernels and evaluation
maps are measurable. Each declared model has its own probability space;
no joint law between the operational FormulaReasoning model and the
counterfactual representation ensemble is assumed. Logarithms, entropies,
and KL divergences use base~2, with $-\log_2 0:=+\infty$. Policy families
are nonempty. Displayed differences of entropies, cross-entropies, or
risks require finite components unless extended values are explicitly
allowed.

Let $d_{\mathsf T}:\mathcal Q\times\mathcal Y\times\widehat{\mathcal Y}\to[0,\infty]$ be a registered, measurable, query-dependent distortion or discrepancy rule. For tolerance $\tau\geq0$, define

\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(q,y)
:=
\left\{
\widehat y\in\widehat{\mathcal Y}:
d_{\mathsf T}(q,y,\widehat y)\leq\tau
\right\},
\qquad
\ell_{\mathsf T,\tau}(q,y,\widehat y)
:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\widehat y\notin\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(q,y)
\right\}.
\label{eq:theory-common-acceptance-rule}
\end{equation}

We assume that the acceptance relation in Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-common-acceptance-rule} is measurable and that the accepted set is nonempty on the registered task support. The rule need not be a metric ball, and accepted sets for different targets may overlap. We abbreviate a registered instance as $\mathcal A_\tau$ and $\ell_\tau$ when the task is clear.

FormulaReasoning has an open-ended structured answer space $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$. Let $\varnothing$ denote the registered semantic no-answer disposition for a launched policy and define the disjoint extension

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}
:=
\widehat{\mathcal Y}\sqcup\{\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}

For the FormulaReasoning specialization, the general adjudicated output space in Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-common-acceptance-rule} is instantiated by $\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}$.

The primary numerical endpoint uses exact canonical equality; the
repository metric is a sealed secondary comparison. A registered jointly
measurable renderer $\jmath_q:\mathcal Y\to\widehat{\mathcal Y}$ places
each verified reference in the raw schema, and $c_q(y)$ abbreviates
$c_q(\jmath_q(y))$. Fix a deterministic,
jointly measurable canonicalizer

\begin{equation}
c_q:
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal Z_{\bot},
\qquad
\mathcal Z_{\bot}
:=
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{FR}}\cup\{\bot\},
\qquad
2\leq K:=|\mathcal Z_{\bot}|<\infty,
\qquad
c_q(\varnothing):=\bot.
\label{eq:theory-canonicalizer}
\end{equation}

Question dependence is restricted to a frozen, jointly measurable public
metadata projection $u:\mathcal Q\to\mathcal U$, with a globally finite
vocabulary for quantity kind, dimension, canonical unit, bare-number and
missing-unit rules, and percentage--ratio scale. Set
$c_q=\bar c_{u(q)}$. The projection is fixed by preregistered deterministic
extraction or locked reference-blind annotation before reference access.
Neither map may inspect hidden reference fields, execute task formulas,
or select target-specific precision, exponent bounds, or plausibility
ranges. Explicit task-rounding directives belong to the reference
function before canonicalization. These restrictions prevent the
question-dependent evaluator from acting as an answer oracle.

The canonicalizer parses one unambiguous answer field and applies the
registered exact unit and scale conversions. The accounting seal fixes a
positive integer precision $p_{\mathrm{FR}}$ and global integer exponents
$e_{\min}\leq e_{\max}$ before reference inspection; these choices remain
common to all items. The proposed pilot sets $p_{\mathrm{FR}}=6$. Nonzero
values are rounded once in normalized decimal scientific form to
$p_{\mathrm{FR}}$ significant decimal digits, using round-to-nearest,
ties-to-even; a significand carry is
renormalized before the exponent check. Decimal literals and exact
conversion factors use exact rational or decimal arithmetic, not binary
floating-point adjudication. Signed zeros share one label. Malformed,
ambiguous, incompatible, nonfinite, and out-of-range outputs map to
$\bot$; out-of-range nonzero values are neither clipped nor replaced by
zero. The complete parsing, unit, affine-conversion, and conformance
rules are specified in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-question-sets}.
The finite numerical grid is

\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{FR}}
:=
\{0\}
\cup
\left\{
\sigma\,n\,10^{e-(p_{\mathrm{FR}}-1)}:
\sigma\in\{-1,+1\},\ 
n\in\{10^{p_{\mathrm{FR}}-1},\ldots,10^{p_{\mathrm{FR}}}-1\},\ 
e\in\{e_{\min},\ldots,e_{\max}\}
\right\}.
\end{equation}

Let $L:=e_{\max}-e_{\min}+1$. For the full signed
$p_{\mathrm{FR}}$-digit grid,

\begin{equation}
K=|\mathcal Z_{\bot}|=2+18\cdot10^{p_{\mathrm{FR}}-1}L,
\label{eq:theory-canonical-alphabet-size}
\end{equation}

where the additional labels are zero and $\bot$. Both exponent bounds
are necessary: fixed significant digits and an upper magnitude bound
alone permit arbitrarily small nonzero labels. The manifest records the
bounds, precision, $K$, and $\log_2(K-1)$; item-specific ranges cannot shrink this
common alphabet.

The variable $Y$ denotes the independently audited recomputed reference value before canonicalization; the published answer remains a separate reconciliation field rather than the value silently substituted for $Y$. The verified reference computation and the solver output pass through the same canonicalizer. Define the canonical target

\begin{equation}
Z:=c_Q(Y),
\qquad
\Pr\{Z=\bot\}=0.
\label{eq:theory-canonical-target}
\end{equation}

The reference contract treats displayed numerals as exact task constants
and recomputes $Y$ using exact rational or decimal arithmetic, or
certified intervals sufficient to identify a unique canonical cell or
an exact tie. It independently reconciles the formula, inputs, units,
quantity kind, and published answer. A coarse published value is not
zero-padded: recomputation must agree with its implied rounding cell
under the frozen preallocation rule. Reference and solver output use the
same canonicalizer, with no tolerance between distinct labels:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_0(q,y)
:=
c_q^{-1}\!\left(\{c_q(y)\}\right),
\qquad
\ell_0(q,y,\widehat y)
:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{c_q(\widehat y)\neq c_q(y)\right\}.
\label{eq:theory-canonical-acceptance}
\end{equation}

Several raw expressions can share one canonical label. Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-canonical-acceptance}
is an instance of Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-common-acceptance-rule} with
discrepancy equal to the indicator of unequal labels and $\tau=0$.

Every finite-alphabet Fano or confidence-completion statement below is invoked only for an explicitly registered finite target or finite reduction. Neither the set of answers observed after an experiment nor an output-dependent precision choice may be used post hoc as the alphabet.

\paragraph{Complete solutions and retained mathematical validity.}

Canonical numerical correctness is one endpoint. The procedural-growth
result below uses a second endpoint that also requires a valid retained
derivation. Fix a standard-Borel space $\mathcal O$ of complete terminal
solution records, including the registered failure and no-answer
dispositions. A measurable extraction map
\[
\operatorname{ans}:\mathcal O\longrightarrow
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}
\]
returns the released semantic answer. Let
$\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)\in\{0,1\}$ be a registered measurable predicate
for mathematical validity of the submitted derivation and all its
retained dependencies. It checks the stated claims, bindings, units,
inferences, and applicability conditions against the question. It does
not require matching a unique reference proof.

For the deterministic FormulaReasoning task, define
\begin{align}
\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}(q,y)
&:=
\left\{o\in\mathcal O:
\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)=1,\ 
c_q(\operatorname{ans}(o))=c_q(y)\right\},
\label{eq:theory-full-solution-acceptance}\\
\ell^{\mathrm{sol}}(q,y,o)
&:=
1-\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)\,
\mathbf 1\!\left\{c_q(\operatorname{ans}(o))=c_q(y)\right\}.
\label{eq:theory-full-solution-loss}
\end{align}
We assume that the full-solution acceptance graph is measurable and
that an accepted solution exists on the registered task support. This
does not assert that a policy can find one within its budget. A
numerically correct answer supported by an invalid retained derivation
has full-solution loss one. Discarded exploratory errors do not by
themselves invalidate a repaired solution, provided the submitted
derivation and every retained dependency are valid. A failed or
no-answer disposition cannot satisfy the canonical equality and hence
also has full-solution loss one.

The predicate $\operatorname{Valid}$ specifies the mathematical
endpoint. Admission tests, runtime validators, and empirical
adjudicators provide evidence about that endpoint; they are not
identified with infallible access to it. The study records assessed
valid, assessed invalid, and unassessable derivations under a frozen
rubric. Their relation to the mathematical loss and the treatment of
unresolved assessments are stated in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:full-solution-assessments}.

The general acceptance-set theory can use $\mathcal O$ and
$\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}$ when its own closure and coverage premises
hold. The finite numerical alphabet alone does not provide a fallback
over valid derivations. We therefore distinguish the finite canonical
calibration from the idealized full-solution reuse model.

\subsection{Task Determinacy Is Not Solver Accessibility}
\label{subsec:task-determinacy}

Let $Q$ include the complete FormulaReasoning question and the evidence supplied with it, and let $Y$ be the verified reference value before canonicalization. The benchmark is deductive: for some measurable task map $f_{\mathrm{FR}}$,

\begin{equation}
Y=f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q)
\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\label{eq:fixed-benchmark-determinacy}
\end{equation}

Because $c_q$ is deterministic, the operational target $Z=c_Q(Y)$ is also measurable with respect to $Q$. Since $Z$ has the registered finite alphabet $\mathcal Z_{\bot}$,

\begin{equation}
H(Z\mid Q)=0.
\label{eq:fixed-benchmark-zero-entropy}
\end{equation}

This determinacy does not supply a budget-feasible computation of the
target. Stochastic decoding makes the solver's output random, not $Z$
conditional on $Q$. Throughout, solver-relative limitations are modeled
by a declared restricted family of procedures.

A positive conditional-entropy residual instead requires a genuinely
restricted decoder-visible observation. Because $Z$ is determined by
$Q$,

\begin{equation}
H(Z\mid Q,C)=0
\label{eq:answer-determined-with-side-state}
\end{equation}

for every additional state $C$. A nontrivial Fano bound based on
$H(Z\mid C_0)$ requires $C_0$ to be the decoder's complete answer-relevant
observation, apart from independent randomization. If the decoder can
reread $Q$, the residual is zero. Multiple valid derivations do not
alter this determinacy of the numerical target.

\subsection{Standard Conditional Representation Background}
\label{subsec:representation-converses}

A nontrivial Shannon representation question requires a counterfactual ensemble beyond the fixed benchmark. Fix a registered joint law $\widetilde P_{U,\widetilde Q}$ over task-relevant source states and supported queries. Let

\begin{equation}
\widetilde Y
=
\widetilde f(U,\widetilde Q),
\qquad
\Theta\sim P_{\Theta\mid U},
\qquad
\Theta\perp\widetilde Q\mid U,
\label{eq:source-representation-ensemble}
\end{equation}

where $\Theta$ is a random representation produced from the source. This upper-case random variable is not the same object as fixed realized model weights $\theta_\star$ used in the bounded-family analysis below. Unlike the operational FormulaReasoning target $Z$, the counterfactual target $\widetilde Y$ need not be measurable with respect to $\widetilde Q$. The pair $(\widetilde Q,\Theta)$ is the closed baseline state for this theoretical ensemble: the decoder may use this pair, but it may not bypass the representation by rereading $U$.

The counterfactual theory deliberately permits arbitrary target and output spaces. Let $\mathcal T_\Theta$ denote the standard-Borel alphabet of $\Theta$. For a FormulaReasoning-like counterfactual ensemble supported on the registered canonicalizer's query domain, one may either retain the raw target $\widetilde Y$ and use the canonical-preimage acceptance rule or form the finite canonical target $\widetilde Z:=c_{\widetilde Q}(\widetilde Y)$. This is an instantiation of the general theory, not a restriction imposed on other tasks.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}[Task sufficiency]
\label{def:task-sufficiency}

For exact recovery, $\Theta$ is \emph{exactly task-sufficient} if there is a measurable target-valued decoder $g_{\mathcal Y}:\widetilde{\mathcal Q}\times\mathcal T_\Theta\to\mathcal Y$ such that

\begin{equation}
\widetilde Y=g_{\mathcal Y}(\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\label{eq:task-sufficient-decoder}
\end{equation}

For arbitrary target and output spaces under the acceptance rule in Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-common-acceptance-rule}, it is \emph{$\tau$-task-sufficient} if there is a measurable output-valued decoder $g:\widetilde{\mathcal Q}\times\mathcal T_\Theta\to\widehat{\mathcal Y}$ such that

\begin{equation}
g(\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\in
\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y)
\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\label{eq:tolerance-task-sufficient-decoder}
\end{equation}

When $\widetilde Y$ has a finite alphabet whose labels are admissible outputs, exact task sufficiency is equivalent to

\begin{equation}
H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)=0.
\end{equation}

In that finite setting, define the representation residual

\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{rep}}
:=
H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta).
\label{eq:representation-residual}
\end{equation}

\end{definition}

Task sufficiency concerns recoverability, not verbatim storage. A representation may omit an explicit formula while preserving every distinction needed to derive an accepted answer; conversely, it is insufficient if it merges source states that require acceptance-incompatible outputs to the same supported query.

For every integer $m\geq2$, define

\begin{equation}
\phi_m(e)
:=
h_2(e)+e\log_2(m-1),
\qquad
0\leq e\leq1,
\label{eq:fano-curve}
\end{equation}

where $h_2$ is binary entropy. For each $m$, the function $\phi_m$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ and strictly increasing from $0$ to $\log_2m$ on the Fano branch $[0,1-1/m]$. Whenever $\phi_m^{-1}$ appears in a Fano statement, it denotes the inverse of that increasing branch.

For finite exact prediction, let

\begin{equation}
e_{\Theta}^{\star,01}
:=
\inf_{g:\widetilde{\mathcal Q}\times\mathcal T_\Theta\to\mathcal Y}
\Pr\!\left\{
g(\widetilde Q,\Theta)\neq\widetilde Y
\right\}.
\label{eq:representation-bayes-error}
\end{equation}

For arbitrary outputs and the registered tolerance rule, let

\begin{equation}
R_{\Theta}^{\star,\tau}
:=
\inf_g
\Pr\!\left\{
g(\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\notin
\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y)
\right\},
\label{eq:representation-tolerance-risk}
\end{equation}

where the exact infimum is over measurable target-valued decoders and the tolerance infimum is over measurable decoders into $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$, each using only the displayed closed observation.

For the task $\mathsf T=(\widetilde P_{U,\widetilde Q},\widetilde f,d_{\mathsf T})$, define the task-specific information--distortion curve

\begin{equation}
D_{\mathsf T}(R)
:=
\inf_{\substack{
 P_{\Xi\mid U}:\\
\Xi\perp\widetilde Q\mid U,\\
I(U;\Xi\mid\widetilde Q)\leq R
}}
\;
\inf_g
\mathbb E\!\left[
d_{\mathsf T}\!\left(
\widetilde Q,
\widetilde Y,
g(\widetilde Q,\Xi)
\right)
\right],
\qquad R\geq0.
\label{eq:task-information-distortion}
\end{equation}

The first infimum ranges over standard-Borel auxiliary alphabets for $\Xi$ and measurable channels satisfying the displayed Markov and rate conditions; for each such choice, the second ranges over measurable decoders from $\widetilde{\mathcal Q}$ times the chosen auxiliary alphabet into $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$. The restriction $\Xi\perp\widetilde Q\mid U$ is substantive: the representation channel does not condition directly on the realized query beyond information about it already contained in $U$.

\paragraph{Standard representation bounds.}

The information--distortion comparison below follows directly from its
definition. The remaining bounds apply standard conditional Fano and data
processing \citep[Sections~2.8 and~2.10]{cover2006elements}, with the explicit
acceptance-separated reduction in part~(iii). They apply under the
ensemble in Equation~\eqref{eq:source-representation-ensemble}:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]

\item If the actual representation channel satisfies

\begin{equation}
I(U;\Theta\mid\widetilde Q)\leq R,
\label{eq:representation-rate-premise}
\end{equation}

then every decoder $g$ of $(\widetilde Q,\Theta)$ satisfies

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\!\left[
d_{\mathsf T}\!\left(
\widetilde Q,
\widetilde Y,
g(\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\right)
\right]
\geq
D_{\mathsf T}(R).
\label{eq:representation-distortion-converse}
\end{equation}

\item If $\widetilde Y$ has a finite alphabet of size $M\geq2$, then

\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{rep}}
\leq
\phi_M\!\left(e_{\Theta}^{\star,01}\right),
\qquad
e_{\Theta}^{\star,01}
\geq
\phi_M^{-1}(h_{\mathrm{rep}}).
\label{eq:conditional-fano-converse}
\end{equation}

Under Equation~\eqref{eq:representation-rate-premise}, this further implies

\begin{equation}
e_{\Theta}^{\star,01}
\geq
\phi_M^{-1}\!\left(
\left[
H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q)-R
\right]_+
\right),
\qquad
[x]_+:=\max\{x,0\}.
\label{eq:rate-fano-converse}
\end{equation}

\item Let $M_{\mathrm{pack}}\geq2$, and suppose there are jointly measurable maps, written in section notation as

\begin{equation}
\alpha_q:\mathcal Y\to\{1,\ldots,M_{\mathrm{pack}}\},
\qquad
\kappa_q:\widehat{\mathcal Y}\to\{1,\ldots,M_{\mathrm{pack}}\}
\label{eq:representation-packing-maps}
\end{equation}

meaning that $(q,y)\mapsto\alpha_q(y)$ and $(q,\widehat y)\mapsto\kappa_q(\widehat y)$ are measurable, such that

\begin{equation}
\widehat y\in\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(q,y)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\kappa_q(\widehat y)=\alpha_q(y)
\label{eq:acceptance-separated-reduction}
\end{equation}

for $\widetilde P_{\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y}$-almost every $(q,y)$ and every $\widehat y\in\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(q,y)$. Set $J:=\alpha_{\widetilde Q}(\widetilde Y)$. Then

\begin{equation}
R_{\Theta}^{\star,\tau}
\geq
\phi_{M_{\mathrm{pack}}}^{-1}\!\left(
H(J\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\right).
\label{eq:packing-fano-residual-converse}
\end{equation}

Under Equation~\eqref{eq:representation-rate-premise},

\begin{equation}
R_{\Theta}^{\star,\tau}
\geq
\phi_{M_{\mathrm{pack}}}^{-1}\!\left(
\left[
H(J\mid\widetilde Q)-R
\right]_+
\right).
\label{eq:packing-rate-fano-converse}
\end{equation}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Derivation.}
For part~(i), the actual channel $P_{\Theta\mid U}$ is feasible in
Equation~\eqref{eq:task-information-distortion}, so its best decoder and
every particular decoder have distortion at least $D_{\mathsf T}(R)$.
For part~(ii), a finite-label Bayes decoder exists and has error at most
$1-1/M$. Conditional Fano therefore applies on the declared increasing
branch. Conditional data processing gives
\[
I(\widetilde Y;\Theta\mid\widetilde Q)
\leq I(U;\Theta\mid\widetilde Q)\leq R,
\qquad
h_{\mathrm{rep}}
\geq[H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q)-R]_+.
\]
For part~(iii), $\widehat J_g:=\kappa_{\widetilde Q}
(g(\widetilde Q,\Theta))$ satisfies
\[
\{\widehat J_g\neq J\}
\subseteq
\{g(\widetilde Q,\Theta)\notin
\mathcal A_{\mathsf T,\tau}(\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y)\}.
\]
The tolerance risk is consequently at least the unrestricted Bayes error
for $J$. Apply finite-label Fano and
$I(J;\Theta\mid\widetilde Q)\leq I(U;\Theta\mid\widetilde Q)$.
The conditional Markov chains used here follow from
Equation~\eqref{eq:source-representation-ensemble} and the deterministic
target and packing maps.

The rate premise requires independent justification; parameter count,
response variability, and benchmark error do not establish it. For
operational FormulaReasoning with canonical zero--one loss, $Q$ already
determines $Z$ and every registered reduction of it. An unrestricted
decoder knowing the task map needs only a constant representation, so
these representation converses are vacuous. Extending that conclusion
to another raw distortion requires an explicit zero-distortion renderer;
nonempty accepted sets alone do not guarantee one.

\subsection{External Support: Shannon Information and Bounded Accessibility}
\label{subsec:external-support-accessibility}

External support raises two distinct questions. In a declared source
ensemble, an exposed packet may change the information available to a
decoder. In a bounded computation, a procedure may change what a policy can
execute even when the complete question already determines the answer.
We first state the Shannon comparison, then develop the fixed-law and
interactive-family accessibility analyses without identifying these roles.

\subsubsection{The counterfactual Shannon layer}

Extend the counterfactual ensemble to a declared joint law of $(U,\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y,\Theta,\mathsf M)$, permitting the specified dependencies of the external store---including dependence on $\Theta$---while preserving the previously declared marginal law of $(U,\widetilde Q,\widetilde Y,\Theta)$ and its Markov condition. Let

\begin{equation}
\widetilde E
:=
\mathsf{Ret}(\widetilde Q,\mathsf M)
\label{eq:retrieved-packet}
\end{equation}

be the packet exposed by a specified deterministic measurable retrieval map. A randomized retriever can instead be modeled by adjoining an explicitly declared independent retrieval seed. The raw store $\mathsf M$ and the exposed packet $\widetilde E$ are not interchangeable.

When $\widetilde Y$ is finite, define

\begin{equation}
h_0
:=
H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta),
\qquad
h_1
:=
H(\widetilde Y\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta,\widetilde E).
\label{eq:external-support-residuals}
\end{equation}

For an acceptance-separated finite reduction $J=\alpha_{\widetilde Q}(\widetilde Y)$ satisfying Equation~\eqref{eq:acceptance-separated-reduction}, define analogously

\begin{equation}
h_0^J
:=
H(J\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta),
\qquad
h_1^J
:=
H(J\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta,\widetilde E).
\label{eq:external-support-packed-residuals}
\end{equation}

The chain rule \citep[Section~2.5]{cover2006elements} identifies the
change in Shannon residual:
\begin{equation}
h_0-h_1
=I(\widetilde Y;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta).
\label{eq:external-support-identity}
\end{equation}
If the finite target alphabet has size $M\geq2$ and a target-valued
decoder based on $(\widetilde Q,\Theta,\widetilde E)$ has error at most
$\epsilon\leq1-1/M$, conditional Fano gives
$h_1\leq\phi_M(\epsilon)$. Substitution and nonnegativity of mutual
information yield
\begin{equation}
I(\widetilde Y;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\geq\left[h_0-\phi_M(\epsilon)\right]_+.
\label{eq:external-support-fano-necessity}
\end{equation}

For an acceptance-separated reduction $J$ with $M_{\mathrm{pack}}\geq2$
labels, every tolerance-correct output identifies the correct packed
label. A supported tolerance decoder with error at most
$\epsilon\leq1-1/M_{\mathrm{pack}}$ therefore induces a packed-label
decoder with error at most $\epsilon$. Applying the same chain rule and
conditional Fano gives
\begin{equation}
h_0^J-h_1^J
=I(J;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\geq\left[h_0^J-\phi_{M_{\mathrm{pack}}}(\epsilon)\right]_+.
\label{eq:external-support-packing-necessity}
\end{equation}

Mutual information does not encode correctness, certification, or
successful use: even a systematically wrong signal can be informative.
Certification changes the composition and reliability of a store; its
transcript is side information only when the registered decoder sees it.

Positive conditional mutual information requires answer-relevant
dependence in the declared ensemble. If $\mathsf M=m$ is fixed and
retrieval is deterministic, $\widetilde E$ is a function of
$\widetilde Q$, giving

\begin{equation}
I(\widetilde Y;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)=0,
\qquad
I(J;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)=0.
\label{eq:fixed-store-zero-information}
\end{equation}

If these relations are instantiated directly on FormulaReasoning, where $Z=c_Q(f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q))$, the exact and packed residuals are zero after conditioning on $Q$. A fixed formula or motif can nevertheless help a bounded system by externalizing a derivation, constraining semantic roles, or making a difficult composition executable within budget. That is an accessibility or realization effect, not new Shannon information conditional on the complete question.

\subsubsection{Discrete singleton-label log-loss background}
\label{subsubsec:discrete-v-background}

First consider terminal prediction from one covariate $X$ under a common
predictor-independent law $P_{X,Y}$. This fixed-law comparison differs
from the policy-induced experiments defined below.

In this subsection only, $Y$ denotes a generic discrete exact label and $\mathcal V$ is a nonempty class of measurable probability mass kernels $v(\cdot\mid X)$. Define the best fixed-law exact-label log loss

\begin{equation}
L_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Y\mid X)
:=
\inf_{v\in\mathcal V}
\mathbb E_{P}\!\left[
-\log_2 v(Y\mid X)
\right].
\label{eq:v-entropy}
\end{equation}

When a class satisfies the predictive-family conditions of
\citet[Definitions~1--2]{xu2020usable}, the corresponding quantity is
commonly called predictive $\mathcal V$-entropy. We use $L_{\mathcal V}^{P}$ because the identity below needs only fixed-law conditional cross-entropy algebra, not those additional family axioms. Define the restricted KL approximation gap

\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Y\mid X)
:=
\inf_{v\in\mathcal V}
\mathbb E_{P}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
P_{Y\mid X}(\cdot\mid X)
\,\middle\|\,
v(\cdot\mid X)
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:accessibility-gap}
\end{equation}

The standard relative-entropy identity
\citep[Sections~2.3--2.4]{cover2006elements} gives, under the fixed law
$P_{X,Y}$,

\begin{equation}
L_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Y\mid X)
=
H_P(Y\mid X)
+
\Gamma_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Y\mid X).
\label{eq:v-cross-entropy-decomposition}
\end{equation}

For each $v$, conditional cross-entropy equals conditional entropy plus
expected conditional KL divergence, including extended values. Taking
the infimum leaves the common entropy term unchanged.

For a single fixed FormulaReasoning covariate $X$ containing $Q$, substitute the canonical target $Z=c_Q(Y)$. Because $Z=c_Q(f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q))$ is deterministic given $Q$,

\begin{equation}
H_P(Z\mid X)=0.
\end{equation}

Thus fixed-law canonical log loss equals the restricted KL approximation
gap. When the Shannon term is positive, the decomposition distinguishes
uncertainty in the observation from the additional loss imposed by the
predictor family. When it vanishes, all remaining fixed-law log loss is
an approximation gap.

In broader policy-induced experiments, observations may reveal hidden
source uncertainty and the corresponding entropy terms need not be
common; a family infimum then cannot generally be separated into two
infima. This caveat does not apply to fixed finite task-derived targets
under the observed-history response kernels specified in
Equation~\eqref{eq:observed-history-response-kernel} below.

Ordinary predictive $\mathcal V$-entropy cannot be inserted into classical Fano for an arbitrary family. For example, let $Y=X\in\{0,1\}$ and suppose a restricted family contains only a predictor assigning probability $0.51$ to the correct semantic answer and $0.49$ to the other answer. Its argmax is always correct, while its log loss is positive. The family has excluded every confidence sharpening of an already perfect decision rule.

On a finite alphabet, arbitrary positive scaling of scores drives log
loss to zero if the correct label is already the unique maximizer
almost surely and one starting scale has integrable log loss: the losses
decrease pointwise to zero and are dominated by that starting loss.
This requires scaling closure and cannot repair an incorrect ranking.
The completion below instead starts from any registered decision rule.

Nor does testing one predictor estimate a fixed-law family optimum. For any tested $v\in\mathcal V$,

\begin{equation}
L_{\mathcal V}^{P}(Y\mid X)
\leq
\mathbb E_P[-\log_2v(Y\mid X)].
\label{eq:single-predictor-upper-bound}
\end{equation}

A sample estimate needs uncertainty analysis. Estimating the family
optimum additionally requires a specified, optimizable family and
probabilities aggregated over complete canonical preimages; one emitted
string's token probability is insufficient. The pilot supplies neither
such optimization nor canonical probability reports.

\subsubsection{General evaluator-aligned acceptance-set accessibility}
\label{subsubsec:acceptance-set-accessibility}

We now return to an arbitrary standard-Borel raw output space $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$ and a general registered acceptance rule $\mathcal A_\tau$. We assume that its acceptance graph

\begin{equation}
\mathfrak A_\tau
:=
\left\{
(q,y,\widehat y):
\widehat y\in\mathcal A_\tau(q,y)
\right\}
\end{equation}

is measurable. This ensures that the accepted-set probabilities scored below are measurable random variables.

Fix a realized system state $s$, a resource specification $B$, a common task law $P_{Q,Y}$, and a frozen interaction environment $\mathsf E_s$. For the theoretical estimand, the environment contains posited model and provider response kernels together with the registered retrieval interfaces, tools, stores, validators, and reset or isolation rules. This mathematical specification does not imply that an opaque hosted-provider kernel is empirically known or constant across executions. An admissible policy begins after $(Q,Y)$ is drawn, observes $Q$ and only the permitted runtime fields, and chooses prompts, retrievals, model calls, tool invocations, validation steps, repairs, stopping actions, and terminal behavior nonanticipatingly within $B$. It never receives the hidden reference or adjudication record as an oracle.

Here $s$ fixes the release and active store for the within-question
experiment. The outer development process may later construct,
validate, and freeze a new store and release. A more general
stateful environment can model store writes as actions with registered
state-transition kernels, but the study's within-question policies
have read-only access to their frozen release. The policy's law is
therefore state- and environment-dependent even when those indices are
suppressed in $P^\pi$.

Formally, after adjoining cemetery symbols to represent stopping, a policy consists of history-adapted stochastic action kernels

\begin{equation}
\pi_k(da_k\mid h_k),
\end{equation}

an almost-surely finite stopping time $T_\pi$, and a terminal kernel. The frozen environment supplies response kernels

\begin{equation}
\mathsf K_{s,k}(do_k\mid h_k,a_k).
\label{eq:observed-history-response-kernel}
\end{equation}

Here $a_k$ is the selected action and $o_k$ is the environment
observation returned in response. The kernel describes the law of that
observation given the observed history and action at stage $k$.
This is a generative specification: conditional also on $(Q,Y)$, the
action and response laws remain $\pi_k(\cdot\mid h_k)$ and
$\mathsf K_{s,k}(\cdot\mid h_k,a_k)$, respectively. Any initial permitted
runtime fields are conditionally independent of $Y$ given $Q$, and
private policy randomness is independent of task and environment
randomness. Thus actions and responses use the observed history, which
includes $Q$, not an additional hidden-target input. The resulting factorization gives $Y\perp H_\pi\mid Q$ and, for
any fixed measurable finite target $J=j(Q,Y)$,
\[
H_{P^\pi}(J\mid H_\pi)=H_P(J\mid Q)
\qquad\text{for every policy }\pi.
\]
This statement is limited to fixed task-derived targets. A broader
experiment in which observations reveal hidden target or source
uncertainty instead requires a correspondingly target-dependent response
kernel, or a declared latent-state kernel with its joint task law; the
conditional independence and common entropy above need not hold there.

Private policy randomization is represented inside the action kernels. Budget admissibility requires the registered cumulative resource vector through $T_\pi$, including terminal operations, to satisfy $B$. The task law, policy kernels, and environment kernels induce the
policy-specific law $P^\pi$ by the Ionescu--Tulcea construction
\citep[Chapter~8]{kallenberg2021foundations}. Stage-specific action, observation, and history spaces may be represented by their standard-Borel disjoint unions. This kernel formulation is what ``nonanticipating end-to-end policy'' means below.

An explicit terminal-reserve convention chooses a solving allowance $B_{\mathrm{solve}}$ and a reserve $b_{\mathrm{term}}$ for the registered terminal path before defining the policy families, with declared total $B=B_{\mathrm{solve}}+b_{\mathrm{term}}$ (componentwise for a resource vector). The solving allowance is not the total budget; all charged terminal operations must also be accounted for.

Let $\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ be a nonempty registered family of end-to-end decision policies. Each $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ induces its own complete source-policy history $H_\pi$ through selection of the raw output $\widehat Y_\pi$, together with a joint law $P^\pi$. The history includes $Q$ and $\widehat Y_\pi$ before adjudication, so $\widehat Y_\pi$ is measurable from $H_\pi$. Every policy shares the same task marginal,

\begin{equation}
P^\pi_{Q,Y}=P_{Q,Y},
\end{equation}

but different policies may induce different histories, history spaces, and joint laws. Define

\begin{equation}
e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi)
:=
P^\pi\!\left\{
\widehat Y_\pi
\notin
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\right\},
\qquad
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}
:=
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}}
e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi).
\label{eq:acceptance-decision-risk}
\end{equation}

Let $\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}$ be a nonempty registered family of end-to-end probabilistic policies. Each $\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}$ induces its own complete source-policy history $H_\rho$ through the point at which it reports a measurable terminal kernel, together with a joint law $P^\rho$ satisfying $P^\rho_{Q,Y}=P_{Q,Y}$. Write that kernel as

\begin{equation}
V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)
\end{equation}

on $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$. Write

\begin{equation}
p_\rho
:=
V_\rho\!\left(
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\,\middle|\,
H_\rho
\right)
\end{equation}

and define the policy loss and family acceptance-set log-loss residual by

\begin{equation}
L_{s,B}^{\tau}(\rho)
:=
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
-\log_2 p_\rho
\right],
\qquad
A_{s,B}^{\tau}
:=
\inf_{\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}}
L_{s,B}^{\tau}(\rho).
\label{eq:acceptance-set-residual}
\end{equation}

The scored probability is the total mass assigned to every raw output accepted by the evaluator, not the mass assigned to one canonical string. With non-singleton or overlapping accepted sets, $A_{s,B}^{\tau}$ is not generally Shannon conditional entropy, predictive $\mathcal V$-entropy, or a strictly proper score for the full target distribution. In particular, no common-history Shannon-plus-KL decomposition is assumed. It is a restricted, evaluator-aligned set-log functional.

Both $\pi$ and $\rho$ are complete policies with registered
history-dependent action and stopping rules. Either may randomize
during interaction. Their distinction is the terminal object: a
decision policy produces one output, whereas a predictive policy
reports a distribution over outputs. The rules are registered before
execution; their realized actions and probability reports depend on
the history actually encountered.

Throughout, ``budget-preserving'' means that the source policy and its terminal wrapper jointly satisfy $B$. The registered manifest may use the reserve convention above or assign the registered terminal operations zero incremental cost. A reserve provides accounting headroom, but does not itself establish family membership, law-preserving closure, or that every terminal path required by the stated closures fits the reserve; inherited or composed terminal operations must also be counted. If a source policy can exhaust $B$ and the wrapper has positive cost, same-$B$ closure fails. One could instead formulate a comparison from $B$ to $B+b_{\mathrm{wrap}}$, but the results below use the stated same-$B$ closure premise.

The first calibration direction uses a law-preserving sampling closure. We say that $(\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}},\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}})$ is \emph{budget-preserving sampling-closed} if, for every $\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}$, there is a decision policy

\begin{equation}
S(\rho)\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}
\end{equation}

that first executes $\rho$'s complete preterminal interaction unchanged, preserving the joint $P^\rho$-law of

\begin{equation}
\left(
Q,Y,H_\rho,
V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)
\right),
\end{equation}

and then samples exactly from $V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)$ using a fresh terminal seed $\xi_{\mathrm{term}}\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]$ independent of that tuple. Equivalently, under the preserved coupling,

\begin{equation}
\mathcal L\!\left(
\widehat Y_{S(\rho)}
\,\middle|\,
Q,Y,H_\rho
\right)
=
V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho).
\end{equation}

Under the registered coupling, $H_{S(\rho)}$ extends the preserved copy of $H_\rho$ by the fresh seed and sampled output; the output is deterministic given that seed and the preserved history. The displays identify the preserved copy with $H_\rho$. The sampling wrapper receives no additional answer-relevant observation and remains within $B$. Consequently,

\begin{equation}
e_{s,B}^{\tau}\!\left(S(\rho)\right)
=
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[1-p_\rho\right].
\label{eq:sampling-closure-risk-form}
\end{equation}

A measurable representation of a kernel by an independent uniform seed
exists on standard-Borel spaces
\citep[Chapter~4]{kallenberg2021foundations}; the closure additionally
requires the registered sampler to be operationally available within $B$.

For the reverse direction, fix a registered measurable fallback kernel

\begin{equation}
(q,a)\longmapsto\nu_{q,a}
\end{equation}

on $\widehat{\mathcal Y}$. It may depend on the public query $q$ and proposed output $a$, but not on the hidden reference or adjudication record. Because the FormulaReasoning target is mathematically determined by $q$, absence of a separate $y$ argument is not by itself an operational safeguard: constructing, evaluating, sampling, and implementing $\nu$ must be registered budget-preserving mechanical operations and may not invoke an uncharged substantive solver on $q$.

For $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ and $0<\delta<1$, define the terminal completion kernel

\begin{equation}
V_{\pi,\delta}(C\mid H_\pi)
:=
(1-\delta)
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\widehat Y_\pi\in C
\right\}
+
\delta\,
\nu_{Q,\widehat Y_\pi}(C)
\label{eq:open-confidence-completion}
\end{equation}

for every measurable $C\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal Y}$. We say that the policy families are \emph{budget-preserving confidence-complete relative to $\nu$} if, for every $\pi$ and $\delta$, there is a predictor policy

\begin{equation}
C_\delta(\pi)\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}
\end{equation}

that first executes $\pi$'s complete preterminal interaction unchanged, preserves the joint $P^\pi$-law of

\begin{equation}
(Q,Y,H_\pi,\widehat Y_\pi),
\end{equation}

and then reports the kernel in Equation~\eqref{eq:open-confidence-completion}. Under its registered coupling, $H_{C_\delta(\pi)}$ consists of the preserved copy of $H_\pi$ together with its deterministic terminal extension; the displays identify that preserved copy with $H_\pi$. In particular,

\begin{equation}
L_{s,B}^{\tau}\!\left(C_\delta(\pi)\right)
=
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}\!\left[
-\log_2
V_{\pi,\delta}\!\left(
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\,\middle|\,
H_\pi
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}

The wrapper obtains no new answer-relevant information and remains within $B$; every substantive computation used to produce $\widehat Y_\pi$ is inherited unchanged from the source decision policy.

For each decision policy, define its error event

\begin{equation}
F_\pi
:=
\left\{
\widehat Y_\pi
\notin
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\right\}
\label{eq:completion-error-event}
\end{equation}

and its error-event fallback code cost

\begin{equation}
G_{\tau,\pi}
:=
\begin{cases}
-\log_2
\nu_{Q,\widehat Y_\pi}\!\left(
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\right),
&\text{on }F_\pi,
\\[3pt]
0,
&\text{on }F_\pi^c.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fallback-code-length}
\end{equation}

The cost may be infinite on a failure unless the fallback assigns positive mass to the accepted set. Defining it as zero off the error event avoids an undefined $0\cdot\infty$ product.

A registered nondecreasing function

\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\tau,s,B}:[0,1]\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
\end{equation}

is a \emph{fallback-tail modulus} if the same function controls every decision policy under that policy's own induced law:

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}\!\left[
G_{\tau,\pi}
\right]
\leq
\Omega_{\tau,s,B}\!\left(
e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi)
\right)
\qquad
\text{for every }
\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\label{eq:fallback-tail-modulus}
\end{equation}

Define its right-continuous envelope by

\begin{equation}
\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(r)
:=
\inf_{\epsilon>0}
\Omega_{\tau,s,B}\!\left(
\min\{r+\epsilon,1\}
\right).
\label{eq:right-continuous-tail-envelope}
\end{equation}

For a nondecreasing modulus, this is its right limit at $r<1$ and equals $\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(1)$ at $r=1$. The right envelope is needed because the family-optimal error is an infimum and need not be attained.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Acceptance-set accessibility calibration]
\label{prop:tolerance-accessibility-calibration}

Under the definitions above:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]

\item If the policy families are budget-preserving sampling-closed, then

\begin{equation}
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}
\leq
(\ln 2)A_{s,B}^{\tau},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\frac{R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}}{\ln 2}
\leq
A_{s,B}^{\tau}.
\label{eq:sampling-accessibility-bound}
\end{equation}

\item If the policy families are budget-preserving confidence-complete and Equation~\eqref{eq:fallback-tail-modulus} holds, then

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\tau}
\leq
h_2\!\left(R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}\right)
+
\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}\!\left(
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}
\right).
\label{eq:completion-accessibility-bound}
\end{equation}

\item Suppose confidence completion, Equation~\eqref{eq:fallback-tail-modulus}, and

\begin{equation}
\lim_{r\downarrow0}
\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(r)
=
0.
\label{eq:vanishing-fallback-tail}
\end{equation}

Then

\begin{equation}
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_{s,B}^{\tau}=0.
\end{equation}

Define

\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\tau,s,B}(c)
:=
\inf\!\left\{
r\in[0,1]:
h_2(r)
+
\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(r)
\geq c
\right\}.
\label{eq:accessibility-inverse-modulus}
\end{equation}

For every $c>0$ for which the set in Equation~\eqref{eq:accessibility-inverse-modulus} is nonempty,

\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\tau,s,B}(c)>0,
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\tau}\geq c
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}
\geq
\Psi_{\tau,s,B}(c)
>0.
\label{eq:open-accessibility-error-floor}
\end{equation}

\item If sampling closure also holds under the premises of part~(iii), then

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\tau}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}=0.
\label{eq:zero-accessibility-equivalence}
\end{equation}

\end{enumerate}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

For part~(i), sampling closure gives
$e_{s,B}^{\tau}(S(\rho))=\mathbb E_{P^\rho}[1-p_\rho]$.
For $p\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
1-p\leq-\ln p=(\ln2)(-\log_2p),
\label{eq:log-dominates-error}
\end{equation}
including $p=0$ in the extended sense. Thus
$R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}\leq(\ln2)L_{s,B}^{\tau}(\rho)$ for every
$\rho$; taking the infimum proves the sampling bound.

For part~(ii), fix $\pi$ and write $e=e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi)$.
On success the completion assigns accepted mass at least $1-\delta$;
on failure that mass is exactly
$\delta\nu_{Q,\widehat Y_\pi}(\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y))$.
Law preservation therefore yields
\begin{align}
A_{s,B}^{\tau}
&\leq L_{s,B}^{\tau}(C_\delta(\pi))\notag\\
&\leq(1-e)[-\log_2(1-\delta)]
 +e[-\log_2\delta]+\mathbb E_{P^\pi}[G_{\tau,\pi}].
\label{eq:fixed-decision-open-completion-loss}
\end{align}
Choose $\delta=e$ for $0<e<1$ and take the one-sided infimum at
$e=0$ or $1$. This uses available completion weights, without adding
endpoint weights to the family, and gives
\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\tau}
\leq h_2(e)+\mathbb E_{P^\pi}[G_{\tau,\pi}]
\leq h_2(e)+\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(e).
\label{eq:fixed-decision-tail-bound}
\end{equation}
For $r^\star=R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}$, choose errors
$e_n\in[r^\star,\min\{r^\star+1/n,1\}]$ from admissible policies.
Monotonicity of $\Omega$ gives
\[
A_{s,B}^{\tau}\leq h_2(e_n)
+\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(\min\{r^\star+1/n,1\}).
\]
Continuity of $h_2$ and the right-envelope definition prove
Equation~\eqref{eq:completion-accessibility-bound}, even when the
infimum is unattained or the modulus takes infinite values.

For part~(iii), the vanishing-tail premise gives
$\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(0)=0$ and
$h_2(r)+\overline\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(r)\to0$ as $r\downarrow0$.
Hence zero optimal error forces zero optimal log loss. For $c>0$, the
calibration curve stays below $c$ on some interval $[0,\eta_c)$.
Whenever its superlevel set is nonempty, its infimum is at least
$\eta_c>0$. If $A_{s,B}^{\tau}\geq c$, the completion bound places
$R_{s,B}^{\star,\tau}$ in that set, proving the error floor.
Part~(iv) combines this implication with the sampling bound.

\end{proof}

The completion bound makes the output geometry explicit through the fallback code length. Suppose that, for some $\beta_\tau\in(0,1]$,

\begin{equation}
\nu_{Q,\widehat Y_\pi}\!\left(
\mathcal A_\tau(Q,Y)
\right)
\geq
\beta_\tau
\qquad
P^\pi\text{-almost surely on }F_\pi
\label{eq:uniform-fallback-small-ball}
\end{equation}

for every $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$. Then

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}\!\left[
G_{\tau,\pi}
\right]
\leq
e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi)
\log_2\frac{1}{\beta_\tau},
\end{equation}

so a valid modulus is

\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\tau,s,B}(r)
=
r\log_2\frac{1}{\beta_\tau}.
\label{eq:uniform-small-ball-modulus}
\end{equation}

Such a uniform lower bound may be unavailable on an unbounded output space. A moment condition is more flexible. If, uniformly over $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$,

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}\!\left[
G_{\tau,\pi}^{\,p}
\right]
\leq
M_p
\qquad
\text{for some }p>1\text{ and }0\leq M_p<\infty,
\label{eq:fallback-moment-condition}
\end{equation}

then H\"older's inequality gives

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}\!\left[
G_{\tau,\pi}
\right]
\leq
M_p^{1/p}
e_{s,B}^{\tau}(\pi)^{1-1/p},
\label{eq:fallback-moment-modulus}
\end{equation}

which supplies a vanishing modulus. These premises must be instantiated for the registered output normalization, evaluator, environment, and policy family. Merely asserting that a fallback exists does not make the resulting error floor quantitatively useful.

The former fixed-observation formulation is recovered when all admissible policies are defined on a common transcript space and induce the same joint law of $(Q,Y,W)$, differing only through terminal postprocessing.

\subsubsection{FormulaReasoning finite exact specialization}
\label{subsubsec:formula-reasoning-finite-specialization}

For this specialization, the raw terminal interface used when the policy
families are registered is the already declared disjoint extension
$\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}$, with $c_q(\varnothing)=\bot$. We use
$\mathcal A_0$ without changing notation for the corresponding preimage in
$\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}$.  Because the verified-reference
contract requires $\Pr\{Z=\bot\}=0$, the no-answer disposition is never
correct under the primary loss.  The decision- and predictor-policy families
in this specialization have this terminal codomain from the outset; neither
the sentinel nor any policy is added after outcomes are observed.

For $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$, define its canonical terminal output by

\begin{equation}
\widehat Z_\pi
:=
c_Q\!\left(\widehat Y_\pi\right)
\in\mathcal Z_{\bot}.
\label{eq:canonical-terminal-rule}
\end{equation}

For every $\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}$, push its raw terminal kernel
through the same canonicalizer:

\begin{equation}
\overline V_\rho(z\mid H_\rho)
:=
V_\rho\!\left(
c_Q^{-1}(\{z\})
\,\middle|\,
H_\rho
\right),
\qquad
z\in\mathcal Z_{\bot}.
\label{eq:canonical-kernel-pushforward}
\end{equation}

Because $Q$ is measurable from $H_\rho$ and $(q,\widehat y)\mapsto
c_q(\widehat y)$ is jointly measurable, $\overline V_\rho$ is a measurable
probability kernel.  Define

\begin{align}
e_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}(\pi)
&:=
P^\pi\!\left\{\widehat Z_\pi\neq Z\right\},
\notag\\
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}
&:=
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}}
e_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}(\pi),
\label{eq:canonical-family-risk}
\\
A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}
&:=
\inf_{\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}}
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
-\log_2\overline V_\rho(Z\mid H_\rho)
\right].
\label{eq:canonical-family-residual}
\end{align}

For every decision policy $\pi$ and predictor policy $\rho$, respectively,

\begin{align}
P^\pi\!\left\{
\widehat Y_\pi\notin\mathcal A_0(Q,Y)
\right\}
&=
P^\pi\!\left\{
\widehat Z_\pi\neq Z
\right\},
\notag\\
V_\rho\!\left(
\mathcal A_0(Q,Y)\mid H_\rho
\right)
&=
\overline V_\rho(Z\mid H_\rho)
\qquad P^\rho\text{-almost surely}.
\label{eq:canonical-preimage-identities}
\end{align}

Consequently, Equations~\eqref{eq:canonical-family-risk} and
\eqref{eq:canonical-family-residual} are exactly the general policy-indexed
quantities $R_{s,B}^{\star,0}$ and $A_{s,B}^{0}$ for the raw canonical-preimage
acceptance rule in Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-canonical-acceptance};
canonicalization does not introduce a second evaluator.

The raw interface also requires a registered representative renderer for
canonical labels. Fix a jointly measurable deterministic map
$\overline\iota$ and define

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\overline\iota:
\mathcal U\times\mathcal Z_{\bot}
&\longrightarrow
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing},
\\
\iota(q,z)
&:=
\overline\iota\!\left(u(q),z\right),
\\
c_q\!\left(\iota(q,z)\right)
&=z,
\qquad
\iota(q,\bot)=\varnothing
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:canonical-representatives}
\end{equation}

for every registered $q$ and $z$.  Thus every numeric grid label has a
permitted structured rendering under every registered metadata type, using
only the frozen public projection $u(q)$ and a deterministic numeric
constructor.  Each $c_q$ is therefore surjective onto the common alphabet.
Item-specific plausibility filters may not remove alternatives, and the renderer
may not use the reference, an adjudication record, a reference program, or a
hidden target-specific field.  Joint measurability and the right-inverse
identity are established by construction and checked by a preregistered
conformance suite.  If this renderer is unavailable, the finite raw-interface
completion below cannot be invoked.

For $a\in\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}$, define the target-independent
raw fallback

\begin{equation}
\nu^{\mathrm{can}}_{q,a}(C)
:=
\frac{1}{K-1}
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathcal Z_{\bot}:\\
z\neq c_q(a)}}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{\iota(q,z)\in C\right\},
\qquad
C\in\mathcal B(\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}).
\end{equation}

The fallback is uniform over the $K-1$ canonical labels other than the
proposed label and emits the registered representative of the sampled label.
We say that the policy families are \emph{budget-preserving finite-confidence
complete} if, for every $\pi\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ and every
$0<\delta<1$, there exists a predictor wrapper
$C_\delta^{\mathrm{can}}(\pi)\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}$ that preserves the
complete joint law of $(Q,Y,H_\pi,\widehat Y_\pi)$ and adds only the terminal
kernel

\begin{align}
V_{\pi,\delta}^{\mathrm{can}}(C\mid H_\pi)
&:=
(1-\delta)
\mathbf 1\!\left\{\widehat Y_\pi\in C\right\}
\notag\\
&\quad+
\frac{\delta}{K-1}
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathcal Z_{\bot}:\\
z\neq\widehat Z_\pi}}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{\iota(Q,z)\in C\right\}.
\label{eq:raw-canonical-confidence-completion}
\end{align}

The copied wrapper history is identified with $H_\pi$ in this display.  The
pushforward of the completed kernel is

\begin{equation}
\overline V_{\pi,\delta}^{\mathrm{can}}(z\mid H_\pi)
=
(1-\delta)
\mathbf 1\!\left\{z=\widehat Z_\pi\right\}
+
\frac{\delta}{K-1}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{z\neq\widehat Z_\pi\right\}.
\label{eq:canonical-confidence-completion}
\end{equation}

This wrapper copies the source policy's entire interaction experiment; it
does not rerun the question under a different prompt, retrieval path, model
call, tool sequence, or provider draw.  It uses $Q$ only through the public
metadata $u(Q)$, together with the already selected output, and receives no
new answer-relevant observation.  Computing the proposed canonical label,
representing the uniform alternative distribution, evaluating or sampling it,
and rendering the selected representative must be registered mechanical
operations within $B$, not an uncharged second solver.  A compact indexed
implementation may be used; the condition does not require materializing all
$K-1$ alternatives.  Closure for every $0<\delta<1$ is a substantive
family-level premise and is not established by the feasibility study.

For the registered finite fallback, the accepted mass is $1/(K-1)$ on
every canonical error. Thus the error-event code cost is
$\log_2(K-1)$ on that event and zero off it, giving the continuous,
vanishing fallback-tail modulus
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P^\pi}[G_{0,\pi}]
=e_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}(\pi)\log_2(K-1),
\qquad
\Omega_{0,s,B}(r)=\overline\Omega_{0,s,B}(r)
=r\log_2(K-1).
\end{equation}
For a decision policy with error $e=e_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}(\pi)$, the
corresponding completion loss is
\begin{equation}
(1-e)[-\log_2(1-\delta)]
+e\log_2\!\left(\frac{K-1}{\delta}\right),
\qquad 0<\delta<1.
\label{eq:fixed-canonical-completion-loss}
\end{equation}
The general completion curve is therefore exactly $\phi_K$. These facts
supply the modulus, not the separate law-preserving, same-budget closure
premises.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}[FormulaReasoning finite canonical calibration]
\label{cor:formula-reasoning-canonical-calibration}

For the registered finite alphabet with $2\leq K<\infty$, the canonical
preimage acceptance rule, and the representative renderer above, the
following hold. Under budget-preserving finite confidence completion,

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}
\leq
\phi_K\!\left(
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}
\right).
\label{eq:canonical-completion-bound}
\end{equation}

Under budget-preserving law-preserving sampling closure,

\begin{equation}
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}
\leq
(\ln2)A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}.
\label{eq:canonical-sampling-bound}
\end{equation}

If both closures hold, then

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}=0.
\label{eq:canonical-zero-equivalence}
\end{equation}

For $0<c\leq\log_2K$, define

\begin{equation}
\Psi_K(c)
:=
\inf\!\left\{
r\in[0,1]:
\phi_K(r)\geq c
\right\}.
\label{eq:canonical-inverse-calibration}
\end{equation}

Then $\Psi_K(c)>0$, it equals the inverse of the increasing branch of
$\phi_K$ on $[0,1-1/K]$, and finite confidence completion alone gives

\begin{equation}
A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}\geq c
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}
\geq
\Psi_K(c)
>0.
\label{eq:canonical-accessibility-floor}
\end{equation}

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under the respective stated closure premises, apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:tolerance-accessibility-calibration}(ii)--(iv) with
the displayed modulus and the canonical-preimage identities; part~(i)
gives the sampling bound. The proposition already handles
unattained policy infima and error endpoints $0$ and $1$ using only
$0<\delta<1$. This is an existential family comparison, not a requirement
to estimate a policy's error during execution.
The first crossing of $c\in(0,\log_2K]$ by $\phi_K$ is its positive
inverse on $[0,1-1/K]$. Its global maximum is $\log_2K$, and the admissible
weight $\delta=1-1/K$ gives the uniform canonical kernel.
\end{proof}

The finite construction resolves the continuous-singleton problem by
assigning an atom to each canonical representative. Uniformity is a
chosen terminal wrapper, not an assumption about an LLM's native answers.
The construction applies also to $K=2$, when the fallback penalty is zero;
$K=1$ is excluded. Finiteness and the registered renderer supply the
modulus; law preservation, same-budget implementation, and closure for
every $0<\delta<1$ remain separate premises. The manifest must report the
$K$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-canonical-alphabet-size}, its fallback
penalty, and $\Psi_K$ before claiming quantitative instantiation. Finer
precision or wider range can weaken the resulting bound.

Apply the same conditional cross-entropy identity policy by policy to
any finite target $J$ and terminal report $v_\rho$ on its alphabet in a
declared policy-induced experiment:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}[-\log_2v_\rho(J\mid H_\rho)]
={}&H_{P^\rho}(J\mid H_\rho)\\
&+\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
P^\rho_{J\mid H_\rho}\,\middle\|\,v_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)
\right)\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:policywise-entropy-kl}
\end{equation}
As in Equation~\eqref{eq:v-cross-entropy-decomposition}, the identity
includes extended values. For any fixed finite task-derived target $J=j(Q,Y)$ under
Equation~\eqref{eq:observed-history-response-kernel}, the entropy term is
$H_P(J\mid Q)$ for every policy and can be pulled outside the family
infimum, even though the transcript laws differ. In a broader experiment
with hidden-source-revealing observations and correspondingly extended
response kernels, entropy may instead depend on the policy; an infimum
of these sums then cannot generally be separated into two infima.
No common-entropy claim is made for an arbitrary policy-specific $J$.

Because every $H_\rho$ contains $Q$ and $Z$ is determined by $Q$, its
conditional law is $\delta_Z$ and its conditional entropy is zero.
Equation~\eqref{eq:policywise-entropy-kl} therefore gives

\begin{align}
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
-\log_2\overline V_\rho(Z\mid H_\rho)
\right]
&=
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\delta_Z
\,\middle\|\,
\overline V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)
\right)
\right],
\notag\\
A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}
&=
\inf_{\rho\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{pred}}}
\mathbb E_{P^\rho}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\delta_Z
\,\middle\|\,
\overline V_\rho(\cdot\mid H_\rho)
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:canonical-accessibility-gap}
\end{align}

This is an infimum of policy-specific KL approximation risks.  It is not a
single Shannon-plus-KL or predictive-$\mathcal V$ decomposition under a common
transcript law.

The relations are family-level converses, not empirical estimators.  For any
implemented $\pi_{\mathrm{impl}}\in\Pi_{s,B}^{\mathrm{dec}}$,

\begin{equation}
R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}
\leq
P^{\pi_{\mathrm{impl}}}\!\left\{
\widehat Z_{\pi_{\mathrm{impl}}}\neq Z
\right\}.
\label{eq:implemented-risk-upper-bounds-optimum}
\end{equation}

Consequently, positive error by a tested policy cannot establish a
positive family optimum or an accessibility lower bound. Selective
improvement after relevant support is introduced can be circumstantial
evidence for an accessibility explanation, subject to the scope in
Section~\ref{subsec:identification-pilot}; it does not supply the premise
$A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}\geq c$.

\subsection{An Idealized Model of Procedural Reuse}
\label{subsec:verified-procedural-growth}
% Legacy label retained while surrounding sections are revised.
% References should name this subsection, not a proposition.
\label{prop:verified-procedural-growth}

The family-level calibration does not, by itself, order the risks of two
implemented systems. The following stage model describes a sufficient
mechanism for improvement: increased useful coverage replaces some
fallible baseline executions while preserving the other stage
mechanisms. Here coverage concerns conditional use of the supported route
at a valid interface, not inventory size or a count of recorded uses;
route reliability is specified separately. The comparison uses full-solution loss from
Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-full-solution-loss}, a common future-task law,
and a common runtime budget. Its conclusions are elementary consequences
of explicit local conditions, whose applicability is the substantive
question for a deployed system. The model is therefore a conditional
comparison of controllers, not a conclusion drawn from an admission record
or successful trace.

\paragraph{Stores, stages, and valid interfaces.}

Fix a realized sequence of active stores
\[
M_0\subseteq M_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_T
\]
and their registered controllers. This nested comparison is an idealized
case; empirical releases may also revoke objects or change unrelated
components. For each store, evaluate a new question under the same law
$P_Q$, with $Y=f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q)$. Equivalently, condition on an already
realized development history and specify a common future-question law.
The questions used to construct the stores are not thereby fresh
evaluation draws.

Fix a finite number $m\geq1$ of semantic stages and a registered
decomposition for each question. A stage can include several model calls,
mechanical operations, and bounded local repairs. It ends with a committed
stage output or a failure disposition. Let $E_{i,t}$ be the event that
stage $i$ under store $M_t$ commits a mathematically valid output at its
registered interface. After early termination, remaining stages are
padded with failure dispositions. Set
\[
C_{0,t}:=\Omega_{\mathrm{prob}},
\qquad
C_{i,t}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}E_{j,t},
\]
where $\Omega_{\mathrm{prob}}$ denotes the entire probability space.
The last stage includes correct final-answer rendering and
canonicalization. The decomposition must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\{O_t\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}(Q,Y)\}=C_{m,t}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\label{eq:reuse-solution-stage-equivalence}
\end{equation}
Thus every retained dependency is covered by a stage contract. A
discarded intermediate attempt is not itself an $E_{i,t}$ failure when
the stage completes a valid repair before commitment.

Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-solution-stage-equivalence} is a substantive
interface requirement, not a consequence of listing the architecture's
seven operational functions. If a controller can revise an earlier
commitment after later stages, the affected computation must be modeled
by a stage or block whose output incorporates that repair. The simple
comparison below does not automatically cover arbitrary backtracking or
changing decompositions.

\paragraph{Registered conditional kernels.}

Write $P_t$ for the complete law induced by the specified controller,
store, and interaction environment. Let $\mathcal H_{i-1,t}$ be its
history before the stage-$i$ route is chosen, including $Q$. On a valid
prefix, let $A_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate use of the admitted-procedure
route; $A_{i,t}=0$ invokes the registered baseline fallback. Routing
occurs before stage execution using the observed history and permitted
applicability checks. It does not require detecting an incorrect
baseline answer before selecting stored support. Any bounded repair
included in a stage is part of the registered route's behavior.
This procedural fallback is distinct from the terminal probability
kernel used in confidence completion.

The following assumptions define the idealized comparison.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]

\item \textbf{Sound initial store and sound admission.}
Every active object in $M_0$ satisfies its declared contract. Every
subsequently admitted procedure and relation satisfies its declared
contract, including scope and composition claims. Admission never
silently changes an earlier object's meaning. These are assumptions of
mathematical soundness; a finite test suite or an empirical
\textsc{Accept} verdict alone does not establish them.

\item \textbf{Correct supported use.}
On every registered valid stage interface, the supported route selects
an applicable object, supplies valid semantic bindings, respects its
dependencies, and executes it correctly. Its final stage output is
therefore valid:
\begin{equation}
P_t(E_{i,t}\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t},A_{i,t}=1)=1
\quad\text{on }C_{i-1,t}.
\label{eq:reuse-supported-soundness}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Stable fallback and continuation interfaces.}
There are measurable functions $p_i:\mathcal Q\to[0,1]$, common to all
store versions, such that
\begin{equation}
P_t(E_{i,t}\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t},A_{i,t}=0)=p_i(Q)
\quad\text{on }C_{i-1,t}.
\label{eq:reuse-stable-fallback}
\end{equation}
Valid outputs are normalized to the registered interfaces used by later
stages. The comparison preserves the conditional stage mechanisms and
remaining resources needed for all the displayed equalities. In
particular, changing the route cannot reduce a later fallback's
reliability through altered prompts, state, provider behavior, or
displaced computation. Merely giving two intermediate values the same
name or type does not establish this condition.

\item \textbf{Monotone useful coverage.}
There are measurable $a_{i,t}:\mathcal Q\to[0,1]$ such that
\begin{equation}
P_t(A_{i,t}=1\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t})=a_{i,t}(Q)
\quad\text{on }C_{i-1,t},
\qquad
a_{i,t+1}(q)\geq a_{i,t}(q)
\label{eq:reuse-monotone-coverage}
\end{equation}
for $P_Q$-almost every $q$, every stage, and every compared update.
Coverage concerns the probability of using the supported route at a
valid interface. It is not the number of stored or retrieved objects.

\item \textbf{Measurability, termination, and budget.}
The stage kernels and registered interfaces are measurable, the
controllers terminate almost surely, and their complete executions
remain within the same resource specification $B$. Retrieval,
applicability checking, validation, repair, execution, fallback, and
terminal operations are charged. The comparison does not obtain the
preserved fallback and continuation by adding uncharged work.

\end{enumerate}

The conditional displays are kernel requirements on registered valid
interfaces. Conditional behavior for a route assigned probability zero
can be specified by its registered stage kernel without conditioning on
a positive-probability event. Likewise, kernels may be defined at valid
interfaces that a particular controller never reaches. The derivation below
uses recursions and does not divide by a zero prefix probability.
Conditioning on a valid prefix in the analysis does not give the
controller a runtime oracle for whether that prefix is valid.

The history conditions in
Equations~\eqref{eq:reuse-stable-fallback}--\eqref{eq:reuse-monotone-coverage}
are stronger than comparing unconditional stage success rates from two
logs. They prevent a change in the mixture of histories or fallback
inputs from being hidden inside a supposedly unchanged $p_i(q)$.
No independence of stage errors is separately assumed.

\paragraph{Stage reliability and full-solution accuracy.}

Soundness of active stores follows by induction from the initial-store
and admission assumptions. Correct supported use then gives a valid
stage output on the supported route. Conditional total probability over
$A_{i,t}$ gives stage reliability
\begin{equation}
s_{i,t}(q):=p_i(q)+a_{i,t}(q)\bigl(1-p_i(q)\bigr).
\label{eq:reuse-stage-reliability}
\end{equation}
Specifically, on $C_{i-1,t}$,
$P_t(E_{i,t}\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t})=s_{i,t}(Q)$.
Because the valid-prefix event and $Q$ are measurable from the recorded
history, the tower property gives
\begin{align*}
P_t(C_{i,t}\mid Q)
&=\mathbb E_{P_t}\!\left[
\mathbf 1_{C_{i-1,t}}
P_t(E_{i,t}\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t})\mid Q\right]\\
&=s_{i,t}(Q)P_t(C_{i-1,t}\mid Q).
\end{align*}
Starting from $P_t(C_{0,t}\mid Q)=1$ and iterating proves
\begin{align}
S_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(q)
&:=P_t\{O_t\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}(Q,Y)\mid Q=q\}
=\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i,t}(q),
\label{eq:reuse-success-product}\\
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
&:=P_t\{O_t\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}(Q,Y)\}
=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i,t}(Q)\right].
\label{eq:reuse-population-accuracy}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-solution-stage-equivalence} identifies the final
valid-prefix event with full-solution success. The recursion also covers
zero-probability prefixes and does not require independent stage errors.

\paragraph{Coverage gains.}

Write $\Delta a_{i,t}(q):=a_{i,t+1}(q)-a_{i,t}(q)$. Under monotone
useful coverage,
\[
s_{i,t+1}(q)-s_{i,t}(q)
=\Delta a_{i,t}(q)\bigl(1-p_i(q)\bigr)\geq0.
\]
Every factor of the success product lies in $[0,1]$, so the product
is nondecreasing pointwise. Hence
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
\geq\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t.
\label{eq:reuse-accuracy-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
For any fixed stage $j$, increasing the other factors can only increase
the new product, giving
\[
\prod_i s_{i,t+1}(q)-\prod_i s_{i,t}(q)
\geq\Delta a_{j,t}(q)\bigl(1-p_j(q)\bigr)
\prod_{i\neq j}s_{i,t}(q).
\]
Integration yields the quantitative comparison
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
\geq\mathbb E_Q\!\left[
\Delta a_{j,t}(Q)\bigl(1-p_j(Q)\bigr)
\prod_{i\neq j}s_{i,t}(Q)\right].
\label{eq:reuse-single-stage-gain}
\end{equation}
Equality holds if only stage $j$ changes coverage. The gain is strictly
positive whenever the nonnegative integrand is positive on a set of
positive $P_Q$-probability.

In particular, suppose a measurable region $\mathcal Q_+\subseteq\mathcal Q$ and
constants $\alpha,r,b,\gamma>0$ satisfy $P_Q(\mathcal Q_+)\geq\alpha$ and, for
almost every $q\in\mathcal Q_+$,
\[
\Delta a_{j,t}(q)\geq r,\qquad
1-p_j(q)\geq b,\qquad
\prod_{i\neq j}s_{i,t}(q)\geq\gamma.
\]
The integrand on that region is at least $rb\gamma$, so
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
\geq\alpha r b\gamma>0.
\label{eq:reuse-quantitative-gain}
\end{equation}
The empty product is one when $m=1$.

\paragraph{Scale and interpretation.}

For a hypothetical four-stage task, suppose every fallback has
conditional success probability $0.95$ and initial supported coverage is
zero. Then full-solution accuracy is $0.95^4=0.81450625$. Suppose an
update supplies a perfectly reliable supported route at one stage with
probability $0.8$ on a region containing $40\%$ of questions, leaving
coverage elsewhere and all other mechanisms unchanged. The affected stage has success
probability $0.8+0.2(0.95)=0.99$ on that region. The population gain is
\[
0.4(0.8)(0.05)(0.95^3)=0.013718,
\]
or approximately $1.37$ percentage points, yielding accuracy
$0.82822425$. These are illustrative parameters, not estimates from
FormulaReasoning. In an empirical application, scope, useful coverage,
fallback vulnerability, and reliability of the other stages each need
evidence.

The model does not assume an improvement in end-to-end accuracy.
It derives that conclusion from local reliability, coverage, and
preservation conditions. It also identifies when the guarantee is zero:
the new object may never be used, its fallback may already be perfect,
or another indispensable stage may never succeed.

\paragraph{Imperfect supported use.}
The product and gain calculations also apply when the supported route
has a registered measurable success kernel $w_i:\mathcal Q\to[0,1]$
instead of certainty:
\[
P_t(E_{i,t}\mid\mathcal H_{i-1,t},A_{i,t}=1)=w_i(Q)
\quad\text{on }C_{i-1,t}.
\]
This replaces the perfect-supported-route premise; the valid interfaces,
fallback, routing, continuation, and budget conditions remain. Require
the same $w_i$ at every registered valid history across the compared
controllers. Then
\begin{equation}
s_{i,t}(q)=p_i(q)+a_{i,t}(q)\bigl(w_i(q)-p_i(q)\bigr).
\label{eq:reuse-imperfect-stage-reliability}
\end{equation}
If $w_i(q)\geq p_i(q)$, increasing coverage is beneficial in the same
sense, with $w_j-p_j$ replacing $1-p_j$ in the gain formula. Where
$w_i<p_i$, additional supported use can be harmful. Admission tests
alone do not establish this comparison of conditional kernels.

\paragraph{Expected accuracy along the idealized sequence.}

Suppose the model's premises hold for countably many releases on the
same task law, with the same finite stage decomposition and fallback
functions. Each $a_{i,t}(q)$ is bounded and nondecreasing in $t$, so it
has a measurable limit $a_{i,\infty}(q)$ almost everywhere. The finite
success product converges pointwise and stays in $[0,1]$. Dominated
convergence therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
\uparrow\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{\infty}
:=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[
\prod_{i=1}^{m}
\left(p_i(Q)+a_{i,\infty}(Q)(1-p_i(Q))\right)\right].
\label{eq:reuse-accuracy-limit}
\end{equation}
The gains telescope:
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\left(\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t\right)
=\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{\infty}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_0
\leq1-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_0.
\]
The limiting accuracy need not be one.

This is expected accuracy under one task law, not a monotone sequence of
binary outcomes on different questions. The same limit argument applies
to stable imperfect-use kernels $w_i\geq p_i$, replacing $1-p_i$ by
$w_i-p_i$. Adaptive changes to the kernels or stage decomposition are
outside this comparison.

\subsubsection{Complementarity of stage coverage}
\label{subsubsec:reuse-complementarity}

Consider the imperfect-use model, including perfect use as $w_i=1$.
Fix $m\geq2$, the common task law, and all valid-interface kernels
$p_i,w_i$ with $w_i\geq p_i$. For a measurable coverage profile
$a=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$, write
\[
s_i^a(q):=p_i(q)+a_i(q)(w_i(q)-p_i(q)),
\qquad
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a]
:=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m}s_k^a(Q)\right].
\]
Choose distinct stages $i,j$ and measurable increments
$0\leq\Delta a_i\leq1-a_i$, $0\leq\Delta a_j\leq1-a_j$.
Let $a^{(i)}$, $a^{(j)}$, and $a^{(ij)}$ increase only coordinate $i$,
only $j$, or both. All four profiles must be realized by controllers
within the same $B$, with the displayed conditional kernels and
continuation mechanisms preserved. Define
\[
d_i(q):=\Delta a_i(q)(w_i(q)-p_i(q)),
\qquad
d_j(q):=\Delta a_j(q)(w_j(q)-p_j(q)).
\]
The exact finite-difference identity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a^{(ij)}]
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a^{(i)}]
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a^{(j)}]
+\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a]\\
&\qquad=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[
d_i(Q)d_j(Q)\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}s_k^a(Q)\right]\geq0.
\end{split}
\label{eq:reuse-coverage-complementarity}
\end{equation}
For each question, expand the four products and cancel their common
terms; the remaining factor is
$d_i(q)d_j(q)\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}s_k^a(q)$. Integration proves the
identity. The inequality is strict exactly when this integrand is
positive on a set of positive task probability; an empty product is one.
As with the stage-product derivation, independent stage errors are not
required.

Thus the marginal accuracy gain from improving stage $i$ is no smaller
after stage $j$ has improved. Accuracy is affine in each coverage
function separately, with the others fixed. For scalar coverage
coordinates on a feasible product domain, the nonnegative mixed
differences are the usual supermodularity property
\citep[Section~2.6.1]{topkis1998supermodularity}. The finite-difference
form accommodates query-dependent coverage directly.

This complementarity concerns different stages with overlapping useful
scope and viable remaining stages. It need not hold between arbitrary
motifs: two motifs covering the same stage can be substitutes, and
disjoint question scopes can give zero cross-effect. Changes that alter
fallback reliability, retrieval, supported kernels, or resources do not
automatically satisfy the four-profile comparison.

\paragraph{Admission order and cumulative accuracy.}
If different admission orders produce the same final coverage and
conditional mechanisms, their final accuracy is equal by the product
formula. For an order $\sigma$, let $a^{\sigma,t}$ be its profile after
admission $t$ and let $n_t$ questions be answered under that profile.
If each question has the declared task and execution marginals, the
expected total number of fully correct solutions is
\begin{equation}
J(\sigma):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}n_t
\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}[a^{\sigma,t}].
\label{eq:reuse-order-cumulative-accuracy}
\end{equation}
Linearity of expectation suffices; no independence across these uses is
needed. Equal final accuracy does not determine $J(\sigma)$.

For a counterexample to immediate-gain greedy admission, let half the
questions have two fallible stages with baseline success $0.5$ each.
Motifs $A$ and $B$ fully cover the respective stages and raise each
supported success probability to $0.9$. The other half have one fallible
stage with baseline success $0.65$, raised to $0.9$ by motif $C$.
All other stages succeed surely, so both question types fit one common
decomposition. Assume equal admission costs and durations, no changes
to the specified kernels, and 100 future questions after each admission.
Baseline accuracy is $0.45$. Alone, $A$ or $B$ adds $0.10$, whereas
$C$ adds $0.125$, so greedy admission chooses $C$ first.
\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Order & After 1 & After 2 & After 3 & Expected correct / 300\\
\midrule
$C,A,B$ & $0.575$ & $0.675$ & $0.855$ & $210.5$\\
$A,B,C$ & $0.550$ & $0.730$ & $0.855$ & $213.5$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Illustrative admission paths with the same endpoint accuracy.
Every entry follows from the specified conditional stage model.}
\label{tab:reuse-admission-order-example}
\end{table}
Interchanging $A$ and $B$ gives the same respective totals. Both greedy
tie-breakings after $C$ therefore yield fewer expected correct solutions
than admitting $A,B$ first. This establishes that immediate-gain greedy
admission is not generally optimal for cumulative accuracy, even with
equal costs. The example is not an estimate from FormulaReasoning.
General adaptive order dependence is treated in
Section~\ref{subsec:adaptive-path-dependence}; cumulative gains and costs
meet in Equation~\eqref{eq:order-cumulative-net-value}.

\subsubsection{The induced solution-distribution log-loss interpretation}
\label{subsubsec:reuse-log-loss}

Let $\mathsf K_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(do\mid q)$ be the output kernel induced by the complete
implemented controller under store $M_t$, after integrating over its
internal interaction and randomization. This kernel describes the
distribution of complete submitted solution records. It need not be a
probability report produced by that controller.

For deterministic FormulaReasoning targets,
\[
S_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(q)
=\mathsf K_t^{\mathrm{sol}}\!\left(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}
(q,f_{\mathrm{FR}}(q))\mid q\right).
\]
Define its accepted-solution log loss by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}
:=
\mathbb E_Q[-\log_2 S_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(Q)]
\in[0,\infty].
\label{eq:reuse-induced-log-loss}
\end{equation}
This scores total probability of all accepted complete solutions; it
does not select one reference derivation as the unique correct string.

Under the stage model, taking the negative logarithm of
Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-success-product} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}
=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}-\log_2s_{i,t}(Q)\right],
\qquad
\mathcal L_{t+1}^{\mathrm{sol}}\leq\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}.
\label{eq:reuse-log-loss-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
These relations hold in the extended reals, including $+\infty$ when a
factor is zero. Pointwise success is nondecreasing, so its negative
logarithm is nonincreasing and expectation preserves the order. If
$\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}<\infty$ and success increases on a set of
positive probability, the integrable loss difference is nonnegative
everywhere and positive on that set; the expected log-loss decrease is
then strict.

An analytical pushforward expresses this loss in KL notation. Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_q(o)&:=
\begin{cases}
c_q(\operatorname{ans}(o)),&\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)=1,\\
\bot,&\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)=0,
\end{cases}\\
v_t(z\mid q)&:=\mathsf K_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(\chi_q^{-1}(\{z\})\mid q).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reuse-validity-pushforward}
\end{equation}
Measurability of validity, answer extraction, and canonicalization makes
this a measurable evaluation map and $v_t$ a probability kernel. For
$P_Q$-almost every $q$, the true label
$z(q):=c_q(f_{\mathrm{FR}}(q))$ is different from $\bot$, and
\[
\chi_q(o)=z(q)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
o\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{sol}}(q,f_{\mathrm{FR}}(q)).
\]
Thus $v_t(z(q)\mid q)=S_t^{\mathrm{sol}}(q)$. Applying the same
conditional cross-entropy identity to the deterministic target $z(Q)$
under this implemented-controller law gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}
=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\delta_{c_Q(f_{\mathrm{FR}}(Q))}
\,\middle\|\,v_t(\cdot\mid Q)\right)\right].
\label{eq:reuse-log-loss-kl}
\end{equation}

The validity map $\chi_q$ is analytical and can require substantive
mathematical checking. Neither it nor $v_t$ is assumed computable within
$B$ or reported by the solver. Accordingly,
$\mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{sol}}$ is not identified with the predictive-family
optimum $A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}$. A full-solution completion would need
its own fallback over accepted derivations and law- and budget-preserving
closure; numerical representatives alone do not supply them.

Pointwise success improvement proves both the accuracy and log-loss
comparisons. Average log loss alone does not generally order average
accuracy. Full-solution improvement also need not improve answer-only
accuracy, since numerical successes produced by invalid derivations can
be lost. The experiment therefore retains both endpoints.

\subsection{Restricted Risk for Versioned Systems}
\label{subsec:restricted-risk}

Real releases can change more than the useful coverage isolated above.
We therefore define whole-system risk and its family-optimum accounting
without assuming that successive releases improve. This separates a
specified performance comparison from the architectural record of what
support was available and used.

The operational comparison fixes a common evaluation law $P_{Q,Y}$,
the registered canonicalizer, and the terminal solution-record space
$\mathcal O$ defined above. The canonical numerical loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}(q,y,o)
:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
c_q\!\left(\operatorname{ans}(o)\right)\neq c_q(y)
\right\}.
\label{eq:versioned-canonical-loss}
\end{equation}
The full-solution loss is
Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-full-solution-loss}. Throughout this
subsection, $\eta\in\{\mathrm{can},\mathrm{sol}\}$ selects the endpoint.
Pointwise, $\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\leq\ell^{\mathrm{sol}}$. The same
inequality holds between their risks for one policy, but it does not
order their changes between two policies.

Terminal actions mapped to $\bot$ fail both endpoints. The theoretical
policies terminate with records almost surely; empirical nonlaunch or
catastrophic incompleteness without a record supplies no fabricated
policy output. Empirical inclusion rules are fixed prospectively;
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes} specifies them for the pilot.

For release $S_t$ and execution regime $\gamma$, fix the realized state
$s_t$, budget $B_{t,\gamma}$, and interaction environment
$\mathsf E_{t,\gamma}$. Development uses $B_t$; the confirmatory
comparison of $S_0,S_D$ uses $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$. Environments retain
any branch-, call-, or time-specific kernels; one common
$\mathsf E_{\mathrm{eval}}$ denotes a separately standardized estimand.
This indexing does not assume empirical knowledge or stability of an
opaque provider's kernels.

Let
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{t,\gamma}^{\mathrm{dec}}
:=
\Pi_{s_t,B_{t,\gamma},\mathsf E_{t,\gamma},P_{Q,Y}}^{\mathrm{dec}}
\end{equation}
be the registered nonempty family of admissible end-to-end decision
policies. The branch- and phase-specific execution manifest is part
of the regime. Every action and terminal operation is charged.
Each $\pi$ induces its own history $H_{t,\gamma,\pi}$, terminal record
$O_{t,\gamma,\pi}$, and law $P_{t,\gamma}^{\pi}$, with
\[
\left(P_{t,\gamma}^{\pi}\right)_{Q,Y}=P_{Q,Y}.
\]
The terminal record is measurable from that policy's complete history.
Histories and their distributions need not be common across policies.

Define
\begin{align}
r_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}(\pi)
&:=
\mathbb E_{P_{t,\gamma}^{\pi}}\!
\left[\ell^\eta(Q,Y,O_{t,\gamma,\pi})\right],
\notag\\
R_{t,\gamma}^{\star,\eta}
&:=
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{t,\gamma}^{\mathrm{dec}}}
r_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}(\pi).
\label{eq:restricted-optimal-risk}
\end{align}
Let
$\pi_{t,\gamma}^{\mathrm{impl}}\in\Pi_{t,\gamma}^{\mathrm{dec}}$
be the policy fixed by the release and execution manifest. Put
\begin{equation}
R_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}
:=r_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}(\pi_{t,\gamma}^{\mathrm{impl}}),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}
:=R_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}-R_{t,\gamma}^{\star,\eta}\geq0.
\label{eq:version-realization-gap}
\end{equation}
For compatibility with the canonical empirical summaries below,
unadorned $R_{t,\gamma}^{\star}$, $R_{t,\gamma}$, and
$\varepsilon_{t,\gamma}$ abbreviate their $\eta=\mathrm{can}$
instances. The canonical optimum is the regime-specific
$R_{s,B}^{\star,\mathrm{can}}$ above. The full-solution optimum uses
its own loss and does not inherit the finite numerical calibration
without the additional full-solution closure premises.

When the common task law is denoted by $\mathcal P$, we may write
$R_{\mathcal P,\gamma}^{\eta}(S_t):=R_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}$.
If the same release is executed under branch-, episode-, time-, or
provider-specific kernels, those indices must be retained. A release
label alone does not determine a population risk.

For either endpoint and any two releases evaluated under the common
task law and the same registered regime, substituting
$R_{u,\gamma}^{\eta}=R_{u,\gamma}^{\star,\eta}
+\varepsilon_{u,\gamma}^{\eta}$ for $u\in\{j,t\}$ gives
\begin{equation}
R_{j,\gamma}^{\eta}-R_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}
=
\left(R_{j,\gamma}^{\star,\eta}
-R_{t,\gamma}^{\star,\eta}\right)
+
\left(\varepsilon_{j,\gamma}^{\eta}
-\varepsilon_{t,\gamma}^{\eta}\right).
\label{eq:versioned-risk-accounting}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:versioned-risk-accounting} is an accounting identity,
not an identified causal decomposition. If interaction environments
differ, the optimal-risk difference is indexed by both family and
environment. A release-only interpretation requires a standardized
environment or an identifying design. If task laws, budgets, or
endpoint definitions change, separately indexed arithmetic identities
still hold, but they no longer compare the same evaluation problem.

The reuse model in Section~\ref{subsec:verified-procedural-growth} supplies a different
claim: its local assumptions directly order the full-solution risks of
the specified implemented controllers. It does not obtain that ordering
by decomposing or estimating a family optimum. Conversely, improving a
family optimum does not ensure that an implementation realizes the
improvement. Retrieval interference, incorrect binding, or a harmful
revision can increase its realization gap.

The empirical summaries below concern realized records. They do not
separate implementation risk, family-optimal risk, or the realization
gap in Equation~\eqref{eq:versioned-risk-accounting}.

\subsection{Prospective Development-Trajectory Estimands}
\label{subsec:adaptive-estimands}

We next distinguish summaries of a recorded development path from
population targets under declared task and execution laws. This provides
an evaluation language without treating procedural-use evidence as an
identified performance effect.

Fix arbitrary positive integers $D$ and $H$, denoting the registered number
of development episodes and terminal questions. The design has an isolated
frozen-$S_0$ shadow, exposed-item replays, and an endpoint comparison of
$S_0$ with $S_D$. The equations below define its descriptive summaries and
conditional population targets; they do not depend on $D=8$ or $H=4$.
There are $2D$ planned first-contact cells and $D+2H$ planned confirmatory
records ($D$ final-development and $2H$ terminal), before accounting for
nonlaunch or other noncompletion.
Section~\ref{sec:pilot-design} specifies that concrete pilot instantiation.
Its execution, disclosure, and abort gates are given in
Sections~\ref{subsec:pilot-development-episode}
and~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory}. Other sizes require their own
prospectively registered operational rules; the pilot sections do not
supply an arbitrary-size protocol. No registered gate is relaxed by
taking a statistical summary.

Every denominator below contains only launched, analysis-valid,
target-loss-eligible cells with sealed terminal actions. A sealed
no-answer or handled-failure action remains an error. Nonlaunch and
catastrophic incompleteness without a terminal action supply no ordinary
loss. Planned-cell noncompletion sensitivities remain separately
registered; Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes} gives the pilot's
specification. All means are undefined for empty
denominators, and all inclusion and exclusion counts remain reported.

For $b\in\{\mathrm{adapt},0\}$, let
$\mathcal T_b^{\mathrm{valid}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,D\}$ be that branch's
included first contacts, and let
$\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\mathrm{valid}}
:=\mathcal T_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{\mathrm{valid}}
\cap\mathcal T_0^{\mathrm{valid}}$.
Their terminal actions are $\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}$ under
$(S_{t-1},B_{t-1})$ and $\widehat O_t^0$ under $(S_0,B_0)$.
Using $\ell^{\mathrm{can}}$ from
Equation~\eqref{eq:versioned-canonical-loss}, define

\begin{align}
\widehat R_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{\mathrm{preq}}
&:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{\mathrm{valid}}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{\mathrm{valid}}}
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}\right),
\label{eq:adaptive-prequential-risk}
\\
\widehat R_0^{\mathrm{shadow}}
&:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_0^{\mathrm{valid}}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_0^{\mathrm{valid}}}
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^0\right),
\label{eq:shadow-risk}
\\
\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{preq}}
&:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\mathrm{valid}}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\mathrm{valid}}}
\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^0\right)
-
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}\right)
\right].
\label{eq:prequential-paired-contrast}
\end{align}

A positive paired contrast means lower realized adaptive loss on the
included pairs. It equals the difference of branch means when both
branches have the same included set, in particular when all $D$ pairs
are included. With unequal denominators, that equality need not hold.
The outcome matrix, paired transitions, dispositions, and resources
accompany the means.

Let $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair},2{:}D}^{\mathrm{valid}}
:=\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\mathrm{valid}}
\cap\{t\in\mathbb N:2\leq t\leq D\}$ exclude the duplicate starting-release
pair. When $D=1$, this set is empty and its contrast is undefined.
When nonempty, report

\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{preq}}^{2{:}D}
:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair},2{:}D}^{\mathrm{valid}}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pair},2{:}D}^{\mathrm{valid}}}
\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^0)
-
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}})
\right].
\label{eq:prequential-paired-contrast-2-D}
\end{equation}

These are descriptive contrasts, released only after the required
sealing point. At $t=1$ both branches run $S_0$; that pair is reported
separately as duplicate same-release executions. Pairing holds item
identity fixed but does not identify an adaptation effect: releases are
history-dependent, not randomized, and provider responses may vary.

The following population comparison requires a declared stream law,
sampling from that law, and the specified conditional execution kernels.
It concerns policies that launch, remain analysis-valid, and are eligible
almost surely. Conditioning a complete-case pilot summary on realized
inclusion does not establish these premises. An unconditional
planned-cell estimand would additionally model launch and outcome or
specify its own intention-to-treat coding rule.

For comparison, let $\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{dev}}$ contain the complete record available before first exposure to $Q_t$, and require $S_{t-1}$ to be $\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{dev}}$-measurable. Suppose that the latent pair $(Q_t,Y_t)\sim\mathcal P$ independently of $\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{dev}}$; only $Q_t$, not the sealed target, is exposed to either branch. For branch $b\in\{\mathrm{shadow},\mathrm{adapt}\}$, episode $t$, and archived release $S$, let $\pi_{t,b}^{\mathrm{impl}}(S)$ denote the implemented end-to-end policy executed in the registered branch-specific environment, and let $P_{t,b}^{\pi(S)}$ be its induced law. Conditional on $\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{dev}}$, fresh environment responses and policy randomization are assumed to follow the indexed kernels defining $P_{t,b}^{\pi(S)}$ and to depend on prior history only through the displayed release, branch, episode, and environment indices. Define

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathcal P,t}^{b,\mathrm{can}}(S)
:=
\mathbb E_{P_{t,b}^{\pi(S)}}\!\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q,Y,O_{t,b}^{\pi(S)}
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}

Conditional on the registered branch environments and call kernels,

\begin{align}
&\mathbb E\!\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^0\right)
-
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(Q_t,Y_t,\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}\right)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{dev}}
\right]
\notag\\
&\qquad=
R_{\mathcal P,t}^{\mathrm{shadow},\mathrm{can}}(S_0)
-
R_{\mathcal P,t}^{\mathrm{adapt},\mathrm{can}}(S_{t-1}).
\label{eq:conditional-first-contact-target}
\end{align}

Only if both branches use the same release-conditional implemented policy and execution environment may the right side be written with one common $R_{\mathcal P,t}^{\mathrm{can}}(S)$. If that policy and environment are also stable across episodes, the time index may be dropped. Without these assumptions, the contrast is branch- and episode-conditional and must not be rewritten as a difference of two time-invariant release risks.

For a fully specified update policy $\mathfrak U$, a future replicated study could target

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{online}}^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathfrak U,D)
:=
\frac1D
\sum_{t=1}^{D}
\mathbb E_{\mathfrak U}\!\left[
R_{\mathcal P,t}^{\mathrm{shadow},\mathrm{can}}(S_0)
-
R_{\mathcal P,t}^{\mathrm{adapt},\mathrm{can}}(S_{t-1})
\right],
\label{eq:population-online-adaptation-contrast}
\end{equation}

The expectation includes streams, API randomness, environments, and the
specified distribution of revisions. Branch indices can be dropped only
under a common execution kernel. The human-guided pilot does not specify
this updater on all histories and does not identify this expectation.

After authorized adaptive feedback, an accepted change schedules one
official replay under $(S_t,B_t)$; a no-change episode is structurally
inapplicable. Replay and freeze permissions follow the prospectively
registered protocol; Section~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory} gives
the pilot's concrete gates. A replay on the exposed
item records fit; an incorrect-to-correct pair is an observed transition,
not evidence identifying its cause or recurrence.

If $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{acc}}$ denotes the accepted bundle of proposed changes, the realized transition is recorded as

\begin{equation}
S_t
=
\operatorname{Freeze}\!\left(
S_{t-1}\oplus\Delta_t^{\mathrm{acc}}
\right).
\label{eq:version-transition}
\end{equation}

This is a versioned historical transition, not an assertion that the human revision process is autonomous, stationary, or reproducible as an algorithm.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The final development replay applies frozen $S_D$ under
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ to the $D$ exposed questions. Let
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{valid}}^{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}$ be its
included indices under the common rules above, with terminal actions
$\widehat O_{D,t}^{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}$. Define

\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}^{\mathrm{can}}(S_D)
:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal D_{\mathrm{valid}}^{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{valid}}^{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}}
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q_t,
Y_t,
\widehat O_{D,t}^{\mathrm{final\mbox{-}dev}}
\right).
\label{eq:regression-risk}
\end{equation}

This is retained development fit, not fresh generalization evidence.
Official within-episode replays use $B_t$, whereas this batch uses
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$; observed differences are not automatically
budget-equivalent or evidence identifying forgetting or interference.

The untouched terminal panel compares $\mathcal J=\{0,D\}$ on $H$
questions under matched $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ and the registered
client-visible configuration. Internal policies and actual provider
kernels may differ. Batch sealing and the prohibition on feedback into
a later release follow the registered protocol;
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-final-replay-terminal} specifies the pilot's
concrete schedule.

For $j\in\mathcal J$, let $\mathcal I_{j}^{\mathrm{valid}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,H\}$ be the terminal questions on which the scheduled branch actually launches, the execution is analysis-valid, and the item remains eligible for the registered target-loss analysis, and let $\widehat O_{j,h}^{\mathrm{term}}$ be its terminal action for $h\in\mathcal I_j^{\mathrm{valid}}$. Provided $\mathcal I_j^{\mathrm{valid}}$ is nonempty, define the release-specific analysis-valid executed-cell canonical-loss mean

\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{\mathrm{term}}^{\mathrm{can}}(S_j)
:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_j^{\mathrm{valid}}|}
\sum_{h\in\mathcal I_j^{\mathrm{valid}}}
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q_h^{\mathrm{term}},
Y_h^{\mathrm{term}},
\widehat O_{j,h}^{\mathrm{term}}
\right),
\label{eq:terminal-risk}
\end{equation}

Let $\mathcal I_{0,D}^{\mathrm{pair}}:=\mathcal I_0^{\mathrm{valid}}\cap\mathcal I_D^{\mathrm{valid}}$. When this set is nonempty, define the paired endpoint contrast

\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{term}}^{0\to D,\mathrm{can}}
:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_{0,D}^{\mathrm{pair}}|}
\sum_{h\in\mathcal I_{0,D}^{\mathrm{pair}}}
\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q_h^{\mathrm{term}},Y_h^{\mathrm{term}},
\widehat O_{0,h}^{\mathrm{term}}
\right)
-
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q_h^{\mathrm{term}},Y_h^{\mathrm{term}},
\widehat O_{D,h}^{\mathrm{term}}
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:terminal-paired-contrast}
\end{equation}

With all $H$ pairs included, the paired contrast equals the difference
of release means, and each binary outcome changes a release's proportion
by $1/H$. Report the raw $2\times H$ matrix, actual branch and paired
denominators, and the registered noncompletion sensitivities. There are
$H$ paired item units, not $2H$ independent questions. In the $H=4$ pilot,
each included outcome changes a complete-panel release proportion by
25 percentage points.

Prospective nonexposure does not establish absence from model pretraining
or undocumented historical work. Population interpretation requires
probability sampling from a declared population and suitable execution
assumptions. These definitions do not supply those premises; the four-item
pilot remains descriptive and imprecise. Pairing does not remove provider
variability or selection through noncompletion.

For a population endpoint, first fix the common standardized confirmatory interaction environment $\mathsf E_{\mathrm{eval}}$ and matched outer budget $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$. For each archived release $S$, let $\pi_{\mathrm{eval}}^{\mathrm{impl}}(S)$ be the implemented end-to-end policy determined by its release and evaluation manifests, let $P_{\mathrm{eval}}^{\pi(S)}$ be the induced law with task marginal $\mathcal P$, and define

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathcal P,\mathrm{eval}}^{\mathrm{can}}(S)
:=
\mathbb E_{P_{\mathrm{eval}}^{\pi(S)}}\!\left[
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(
Q,Y,O_{\mathrm{eval}}^{\pi(S)}
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}

Conditional on the particular archived releases produced by this development history, the corresponding standardized population contrast is

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathcal P}^{0\to D,\mathrm{can}}(S_0,S_D)
:=
R_{\mathcal P,\mathrm{eval}}^{\mathrm{can}}(S_0)
-
R_{\mathcal P,\mathrm{eval}}^{\mathrm{can}}(S_D).
\label{eq:conditional-terminal-population-contrast}
\end{equation}

This estimand conditions on the archived releases, standardized
environment, and matched budget. If actual kernels differ, retain their
indices; a release-only effect does not follow. An updater-level target
would also average over development streams and revision behavior.
The proposed $D=8$, $H=4$ pilot does not identify either expectation.
Larger registered sizes alone would not supply a sampling law, standardized
execution kernels, or a specified updater.

For any nonempty evaluated set $\mathcal I$ of launched, analysis-valid, target-loss-eligible cells and version $S_j$, let $O_{i,j}$ denote the sealed terminal action and define

\begin{equation}
\widehat Y_{i,j}
:=
\operatorname{ans}(O_{i,j})
\in
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing},
\qquad
I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}}
:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{\widehat Y_{i,j}\neq\varnothing\right\}.
\label{eq:empirical-answer-presence}
\end{equation}

Thus $I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}}$ indicates that the terminal action contains a semantic answer, while a launched no-answer disposition has $\widehat Y_{i,j}=\varnothing$ and $I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}}=0$. Define

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(O_{i,j})
:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
c_{Q_i}\!\left(\operatorname{ans}(O_{i,j})\right)
=
c_{Q_i}(Y_i)
\right\}
=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\operatorname{ans}(O_{i,j})\in\mathcal A_0(Q_i,Y_i)
\right\}
\label{eq:empirical-acceptance-flag}
\end{equation}

using the common exact-canonical contract. Define

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}_{\mathcal I}(S_j)
:=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal I|}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}},
\label{eq:empirical-coverage}
\end{equation}

and, when at least one semantic answer is released,

\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{accepted}}(S_j)
:=
\frac{
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}}
\left[1-\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(O_{i,j})\right]
}{
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}I_{i,j}^{\mathrm{ans}}
}.
\label{eq:empirical-accepted-risk}
\end{equation}

Answer-release coverage differs from useful procedural coverage
$a_{i,t}(q)$. Accepted-answer risk is undefined if no answer is released.
A no-answer action remains in the end-to-end denominator with loss one;
these supplementary quantities cannot turn abstention into an accuracy
gain. The common noncompletion rules above still apply.

\subsubsection{Full-solution assessments and unresolved judgments}
\label{subsubsec:full-solution-assessments}

For each launched, analysis-valid, target-loss-eligible record, the
study separately assesses the retained derivation under the frozen
rubric. A completed assessment is one of \emph{valid}, \emph{invalid}, or
\emph{unassessable}. At a declared reporting cutoff, separately retain
whether checking is not started, in progress, completed, or has a failed
attempt. A pending report marker is not a fourth completed judgment and
does not fabricate work. It covers assessment still permitted under the
original gates, cap, and retry rules, including work awaiting its gate.
Failed attempts retain their reasons and resources. If no supported
completed judgment is available and no permitted checking remains, the
registered procedure records unassessable unless an established defect
supports invalidity; cap exhaustion and deviation
dispositions cannot be bypassed by leaving work pending. A validity
judgment requires support for the
required retained claims and dependencies; the absence of a detected
defect is insufficient. The sealed submission cannot be repaired during
adjudication. Assessments use the same phase-specific isolation and
release gates as other outcome-bearing evaluation material.

Let $\mathcal C_g$ be the eligible executed cells for a specified
branch and phase $g$, with $n_g:=|\mathcal C_g|>0$. Write
$k_c\in\{0,1\}$ for canonical correctness and $\widetilde d_c$ for
the derivation reporting value at that cutoff: \emph{invalid} takes
precedence whenever a supported retained defect has been established
under the registered rubric and access gates; otherwise use the supported
completed judgment when available, and \emph{pending} while permitted
assessment work remains.
An incomplete assessment cannot support a valid verdict. Partition this
denominator into
\begin{align}
\mathcal P_g^{\mathrm{sol}}
&:=\{c\in\mathcal C_g:k_c=1,\ \widetilde d_c=\mathrm{valid}\},
\notag\\
\mathcal F_g^{\mathrm{sol}}
&:=\{c\in\mathcal C_g:k_c=0
\ \text{or}\ \widetilde d_c=\mathrm{invalid}\},
\notag\\
\mathcal U_g^{\mathrm{sol}}
&:=\{c\in\mathcal C_g:k_c=1,\ \widetilde d_c\in\{\mathrm{unassessable},\mathrm{pending}\}\}.
\label{eq:solution-assessment-partition}
\end{align}
These are assessed passes, assessed failures, and unresolved
full-solution outcomes. In particular, canonical failure settles
full-solution failure even when the derivation is unassessable or pending.

Report all three counts, their common denominator, and the assessed
pass fraction $|\mathcal P_g^{\mathrm{sol}}|/n_g$, with pending and
completed-unassessable counts separate inside $\mathcal U_g^{\mathrm{sol}}$.
Neither pending work nor unassessability overrides an established
canonical failure or retained defect. The possible empirical
error fractions obtained by completing only the unresolved judgments
lie in
\begin{equation}
\left[
\frac{|\mathcal F_g^{\mathrm{sol}}|}{n_g},
\frac{|\mathcal F_g^{\mathrm{sol}}|
+|\mathcal U_g^{\mathrm{sol}}|}{n_g}
\right].
\label{eq:solution-assessment-range}
\end{equation}
This is an unresolved-assessment range conditional on accepting the
resolved judgments. It is not a confidence interval, a correction for
adjudicator error, or a guarantee about mathematical validity. A
mistaken resolved judgment can place the true full-solution error
outside the range. The counts and supporting audit records therefore
remain visible.

For paired contrasts, retain the common eligible executed-cell
intersection used by the corresponding canonical comparison; do not
silently discard a pair because one derivation is unassessable or pending. Define
for each cell
\[
\underline\ell_c:=\mathbf 1\{c\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{sol}}\},
\qquad
\overline\ell_c:=1-\mathbf 1\{c\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{sol}}\}.
\]
For a nonempty paired set $\mathcal I$ comparing a baseline $0$ with
a supported or revised system $1$, the completion range for the
baseline-minus-revised error contrast is
\begin{equation}
\left[
\frac1{|\mathcal I|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
(\underline\ell_{i,0}-\overline\ell_{i,1}),
\quad
\frac1{|\mathcal I|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
(\overline\ell_{i,0}-\underline\ell_{i,1})
\right].
\label{eq:solution-paired-assessment-range}
\end{equation}
Each endpoint follows by minimizing or maximizing the paired
difference over the unresolved binary losses. The same qualifications
about resolved judgments apply.

The same assessment summaries apply to each phase under its registered
gates and common executed-cell sets. Full-solution checking requires the
separately registered rubric, evidence, and resources, instantiated for
the pilot in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication}; canonical
scoring alone does not supply a derivation judgment.

\subsection{Accumulation, Order, and Adaptive Path Dependence}
\label{subsec:adaptive-path-dependence}

Section~\ref{subsubsec:reuse-complementarity} distinguishes identical
endpoint coverage from cumulative accuracy. Its order comparison holds
conditional mechanisms fixed. General adaptive development can change
the artifacts, controller, coverage, or continuation resources themselves.

One useful Shannon identity about accumulation survives, but only for a fixed random collection of artifacts in a declared counterfactual ensemble. To express that identity at FormulaReasoning's registered scoring resolution, let

\begin{equation}
\widetilde Z^\star
:=
c_{\widetilde Q^\star}(\widetilde Y^\star)
\end{equation}

be the finite canonical target for a future task, and let $G_1,\ldots,G_T$ be persistent support objects. For a permutation $\sigma$, write $G_{\sigma(1:t)}=(G_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,G_{\sigma(t)})$. The standard chain rule \citep[Section~2.5]{cover2006elements} gives

\begin{align}
I\!\left(
\widetilde Z^\star;
G_{\sigma(1:t)}
\,\middle|\,
\widetilde Q^\star,\Theta
\right)
&=
\sum_{r=1}^{t}
I\!\left(
\widetilde Z^\star;
G_{\sigma(r)}
\,\middle|\,
\widetilde Q^\star,\Theta,
G_{\sigma(1:r-1)}
\right).
\label{eq:artifact-information-chain-rule}
\end{align}

The cumulative mutual information is nondecreasing in $t$ because every increment is nonnegative. Individual increments may depend on the prefix through redundancy or complementarity. If every order exposes the same jointly distributed final tuple up to permutation, the endpoint mutual information is order-invariant.

That endpoint statement does not apply automatically to the present development process because its artifacts and other system revisions are created adaptively. If a counterfactual order analysis additionally specifies an updater $\mathfrak U_t$ for every admissible history and order, then for a hypothetical order $\sigma$ let $\mathcal H_t^\sigma$ denote the post-seal episode information made available to that updater after item $\sigma(t)$, including the permitted reference, canonical adjudication, diagnostic record, tests, and human decisions. One may then write schematically

\begin{align}
\Delta_t^\sigma
&=
\mathfrak U_t\!\left(
S_{t-1}^\sigma,
\mathcal H_t^\sigma
\right),
\notag\\
S_t^\sigma
&=
\operatorname{Freeze}\!\left(
S_{t-1}^\sigma\oplus\Delta_t^\sigma
\right).
\label{eq:adaptive-path}
\end{align}

Each realized state $S_t^\sigma$, together with its registered environment and budget, determines an admissible family $\Pi_{t,\sigma}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ and one implemented policy $\pi_{t,\sigma}^{\mathrm{impl}}$. Policies in that family induce their own histories and laws. Order dependence may therefore enter through the artifacts and code in the state, the admissible family, the environment, the laws induced by its members, the implemented policy, or the realization gap.

Admissible histories obey the prospectively registered feedback
restrictions, instantiated for the pilot in
Section~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory}. The changes
$\{\Delta_t^\sigma\}$ generally are not a permutation of fixed objects:
orders can elicit different diagnoses, motifs, code, tests, and release
decisions. Equal final store hashes therefore do not imply equal
complete states or induced laws.

The pilot records one path, not $\mathfrak U_t$ on alternative histories,
and identifies no order contrast. On its operational task law,
$Z=c_Q(Y)$ is determined by $Q$, so any jointly defined artifact packet
$G$ satisfies

\begin{equation}
I(Z;G\mid Q)=0.
\end{equation}

The computational mechanism in the stage model is compatible with this
zero-information identity; its induced KL interpretation is
Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-log-loss-kl}.

\subsection{Prospective Resource and Lifecycle Accounting}
\label{subsec:adaptive-resource-accounting}

The primitive ledger records physical consumption, including unsuccessful
construction, admission, runtime checking, repair, and external
assessment. The accounting boundary, scopes, reporting cutoff, and
reconciliation rules are fixed prospectively;
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-resource-ledger} and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledgers-manifest} specify the pilot's rules and schemas.
The definitions here connect such records to lifecycle comparisons.

Registration fixes a finite coordinate dimension $d_{\mathrm{res}}$,
an accounting seal before study-specific reference/evaluator work, and
a reporting cutoff $T_{\mathrm{report}}$. An instrumented physical event
$e$ after the seal has additive native resource vector
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r_e=
\left(
\begin{gathered}
n_e^{\mathrm{in,total}},n_e^{\mathrm{cached}},
n_e^{\mathrm{in,uncached}},n_e^{\mathrm{out}},n_e^{\mathrm{calls}},
s_e^{\mathrm{tool}},s_e^{\mathrm{compute}},\\[-1mm]
m_e^{\mathrm{human}},b_e^{\mathrm{read}},b_e^{\mathrm{written}},
b_e^{\mathrm{persist,+}},b_e^{\mathrm{persist,-}},
d_e^{\mathrm{charge}},\ldots
\end{gathered}
\right)\in\mathbb R_+^{d_{\mathrm{res}}}.
\label{eq:event-resource-vector}
\end{equation}
These coordinates retain provider-native token semantics, calls, tool
and compute use, human effort, byte flows, and charges. Incompatible
units remain separate; cached and uncached components are not added
again to their total. Charges are observed or reconstructed, never both.
For an event set $\mathcal E'$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R(\mathcal E'):=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E'}\mathbf r_e.
\label{eq:event-resource-total}
\end{equation}
Latency, elapsed time, and peak storage are reconstructed separately.
Incomplete coordinates are reported as known subtotals with unavailable
counts, not complete totals. A scheduled action creates no event by
itself; physically executed prelaunch work is nevertheless charged.

Let $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt}}$, $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{eval}}$,
$\mathcal E_{\mathrm{deploy}}$, and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{over}}$ be
the disjoint adaptation, evaluation-only, frozen-deployment, and
study-overhead event sets whose registered dispatch/start time
$t_e\leq T_{\mathrm{report}}$. Each physical event has one immutable
scope, giving
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_{\mathrm{study}}^{\mathrm{phys}}
=\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt}})
+\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{eval}})
+\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{deploy}})
+\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{over}}).
\label{eq:physical-study-resources}
\end{equation}
Every in-boundary cohort event is counted once. Later reconciliation
preserves cohort membership; later events enter dated supplements.
Failed or rejected work remains charged. Contamination adds an
analytical-use attribution without rewriting scope or duplicating
physical consumption. Unreported provider-internal work remains outside
the declared instrumentation boundary.

For $N\geq1$ actually logged deployments of frozen $S_t$, let
$\mathcal E_{\mathrm{deploy},t}^{(N)}$ include those uses and their
assigned setup, hosting, maintenance, storage, and teardown, with shared
events included once. Let $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt},\leq t}$ contain
adaptation completed before $S_t$ froze. The prospective operational
lifecycle vector and its per-use average are
\begin{align}
\mathbf R_{\mathrm{pros\text{-}life},t}(N)
&=\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt},\leq t})
+\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{deploy},t}^{(N)}),
\label{eq:prospective-lifecycle-resources}\\
\overline{\mathbf R}_{\mathrm{pros\text{-}life},t}(N)
&=\frac{\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt},\leq t})}{N}
+\frac{\mathbf R(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{deploy},t}^{(N)})}{N}.
\label{eq:average-prospective-lifecycle-resources}
\end{align}
Evaluation and overhead remain separately visible in the physical study
total; terminal evaluations are not relabelled as deployment. Forecasts
use explicitly projected vectors. Full historical lifecycle resources
would additionally include the resources used to reach $S_0$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_{\mathrm{full\text{-}life},t}(N)
=\mathbf R_{\mathrm{hist}}+\mathbf R_{\mathrm{pros\text{-}life},t}(N).
\label{eq:full-lifecycle-resources}
\end{equation}
Unrecorded historical coordinates remain unknown, not zero.

A dated valuation $\lambda$ may translate selected native coordinates
into common value units:
\begin{equation}
C^\lambda(\mathcal E')
:=\operatorname{Val}_\lambda(\mathbf R(\mathcal E')).
\label{eq:resource-valuation-map}
\end{equation}
For linear pricing,
$\operatorname{Val}_\lambda(\mathbf r)=\mathbf p_\lambda^\top\mathbf r$;
nonlinear pricing needs the corresponding map. Native quantities, API
charges, and imputed human costs remain separately available.

Counterfactual policy attribution is a distinct construction. With
$\mathcal E$ the union of the physical scope sets, define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_p^{\mathrm{attr}}
:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}w_{p,e}\mathbf r_e,
\qquad w_{p,e}\in\{0,1\},
\label{eq:policy-attributed-resources}
\end{equation}
where $w_{p,e}=1$ means that policy $p$ would require event $e$.
A shared certificate can appear in several attributed totals while
being counted once physically. For unexecuted policies these indicators
are assumptions, and different states could require different events;
such totals remain projections. Write
\begin{equation}
C_t^\lambda:=C^\lambda(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{adapt},\leq t})
\label{eq:cumulative-valued-adaptation-cost}
\end{equation}
for valued cumulative adaptation cost.

The theory supplies no universal information--cost relation. For
$\widetilde Z=c_{\widetilde Q}(\widetilde Y)$ and a counterfactual
packet $\widetilde E_C$ available at resource level $C$, no bound such as
\begin{equation}
I(\widetilde Z;\widetilde E_C\mid\widetilde Q,\Theta)
\leq\Phi_\lambda(C)
\label{eq:unassumed-cost-information-bridge}
\end{equation}
is assumed. The next calculation instead combines a justified accuracy
gain with explicit cost and valuation premises. Observed loss--resource
points are one trajectory, not an estimated production frontier.

\subsubsection{Conditional lifecycle value of reuse}
\label{subsubsec:reuse-lifecycle-value}

Fix two releases from the idealized reuse comparison, evaluated under
its common task law, runtime budget, and registered interaction
conditions. Write
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sol}}
:=\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_{t+1}
-\operatorname{Acc}^{\mathrm{sol}}_t
\geq g,
\qquad g:=\alpha r b\gamma>0,
\label{eq:lifecycle-accuracy-premise}
\end{equation}
when the strict-gain conditions in
Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-quantitative-gain} hold. The same calculation
below applies to any independently justified uniform lower bound $g$
on the expected per-question gain.

Consider $N\geq1$ future questions evaluated under those comparison
conditions. Let $I_{u,n}^{\mathrm{sol}}$ indicate full-solution success
for release $u\in\{t,t+1\}$ on question $n$, and let
\begin{equation}
D_N:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\left(I_{t+1,n}^{\mathrm{sol}}-I_{t,n}^{\mathrm{sol}}\right).
\label{eq:lifecycle-additional-solutions}
\end{equation}
This is the signed difference in the number of fully correct solutions
under a joint comparison with the specified marginals. Linearity of
expectation gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[D_N]=N\Delta_t^{\mathrm{sol}}\geq Ng.
\label{eq:lifecycle-expected-gain}
\end{equation}
Independence across questions or between releases is unnecessary for
this expectation identity. The required marginal success probabilities
must nevertheless remain those of the declared comparison; a changing
task mix or execution regime requires corresponding indexed gains.

Fix a dated valuation scenario $\lambda$ and a comparator with the same
cost boundary. Let $\Delta C_N^\lambda$ be the integrable incremental valued
lifecycle cost of using the revised release for this cohort. Costs are
valued for each alternative before taking their difference, so a
nonlinear valuation need not be applied to a resource-vector difference.
Include incremental construction and admission work, unsuccessful
development, retrieval, instantiation, execution, runtime checking, and
maintenance within the declared boundary. Shared physical work is
counted once; historical common costs cancel only when they are
actually common to the specified alternatives.

Suppose finite constants $F\geq0$ and $c_{\mathrm{use}}\in\mathbb R$
satisfy the scenario's expected incremental cost bound
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\Delta C_N^\lambda]
\leq F+Nc_{\mathrm{use}}.
\label{eq:lifecycle-incremental-cost-premise}
\end{equation}
Here $F$ bounds incremental upfront work and $c_{\mathrm{use}}$ bounds
expected incremental recurring cost per question over the declared
horizon. The latter can be negative when saved baseline work exceeds
added store costs. Nonlinear or time-dependent maintenance costs must
be included in a valid horizon-specific bound or retained explicitly;
the affine cost premise is not implied by the ledger.

Let $v\geq0$ be the declared value of one additional fully correct
solution, in the same units as valued cost. Under this linear value
scenario, define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{NetValue}_N^\lambda
:=vD_N-\Delta C_N^\lambda.
\label{eq:lifecycle-net-value-definition}
\end{equation}
Combining Equations~\eqref{eq:lifecycle-expected-gain}
and~\eqref{eq:lifecycle-incremental-cost-premise} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\operatorname{NetValue}_N^\lambda]
\geq N\bigl(vg-c_{\mathrm{use}}\bigr)-F.
\label{eq:lifecycle-net-value-bound}
\end{equation}
If $vg>c_{\mathrm{use}}$, a sufficient expected break-even condition is
\begin{equation}
N\geq\left\lceil\frac{F}{vg-c_{\mathrm{use}}}\right\rceil,
\qquad N\in\{1,2,\ldots\},
\label{eq:lifecycle-break-even}
\end{equation}
provided the gain and cost premises hold through that horizon. This
certifies nonnegative expected incremental value under the scenario.
It is a sufficient count, not the optimal count or a probability
guarantee. A positive margin is needed to recover a positive upfront
cost through this bound. In the boundary case $F=0$ and
$vg=c_{\mathrm{use}}$, the bound is already zero for every $N\geq1$.
When the lower bound remains negative, it cannot certify break-even,
but it does not establish an actual loss; the true gain may exceed $g$
or the true cost may be below its bound.

For an arithmetic illustration, use the hypothetical gain
$g=0.013718$ from the four-stage example and posit $F=100$ cost units,
$v=10$ cost units per additional fully correct solution, and
$c_{\mathrm{use}}=0.02$ cost units per question. Then
$vg-c_{\mathrm{use}}=0.11718$, so $N=854$ suffices under these
assumptions. None of these values is an estimate from the pilot.

For an order $\sigma$ generated by a specified updater, let
$\Delta_{\sigma,n}^{\mathrm{sol}}$ be the expected accuracy gain over a
declared baseline at use $n$, and let $\Delta C_\sigma^\lambda(N)$
include incremental lifecycle costs through that horizon. Then

\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\operatorname{NetValue}_\sigma^\lambda(N)]
=v\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta_{\sigma,n}^{\mathrm{sol}}
-\mathbb E[\Delta C_\sigma^\lambda(N)].
\label{eq:order-cumulative-net-value}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:reuse-order-cumulative-accuracy} supplies the accuracy
part under the stage model. In Table~\ref{tab:reuse-admission-order-example},
equal total costs make the expected value advantage of $A,B,C$ equal to
$3v$ over the 300 uses. Other costs, admission durations, or task mixes
must enter the comparison explicitly. Equal endpoint accuracy alone
does not determine cumulative value.

The gain, cost bounds, valuation, and future-use conditions are scenario
premises. Resource records alone do not establish them or identify the
incremental contribution of admission certification.

\subsection{Limits of Interpretation}
\label{subsec:identification-pilot}

The evidence framework is organized by the claim being made, not by an
implication from availability through use to effect and value. The released
trajectory makes executions and revisions auditable. Its limits follow
from which quantities and counterfactual comparisons remain unobserved;
in particular, it does not by itself identify a positive latent
representation residual or the contributions of components changed together.

For the representation question, suppose the latent representation
$\Theta$ is unobserved and no capacity or structural restriction excludes
augmenting it with the source $U$. Any compatible observable law can
then also be reproduced using $\widetilde\Theta=(\Theta,U)$, with every
system and audit kernel ignoring the added coordinate. This preserves
both the observed trajectory law and the source-generated condition
$\widetilde\Theta\perp\widetilde Q\mid U$. Yet the finite canonical target
$\widetilde Z=c_{\widetilde Q}(\widetilde f(U,\widetilde Q))$ is determined
by $(\widetilde Q,U)$, so
\[
H(\widetilde Z\mid\widetilde Q,\widetilde\Theta)=0,
\qquad
I(\widetilde Z;\widetilde E\mid\widetilde Q,\widetilde\Theta)=0.
\]
Observed canonical errors or gains after exposing support therefore
cannot establish a positive representation residual without further
restrictions. A justified rate, capacity, or structural premise could
block this construction; the observed trajectory alone does not
supply that premise.

For component attribution, suppose a release changes a set
$\mathcal C_t$ of at least two components. Even exact risks under a
common evaluation law for $S_{t-1}$ and
$S_t=S_{t-1}\oplus\Delta_{t,\mathcal C_t}$ would not generally identify
the individual contributions. When relevant partial-update systems
$S_{t-1}\oplus\Delta_{t,C'}$, with
$\varnothing\subsetneq C'\subsetneq\mathcal C_t$, are unevaluated, two
response models can agree on every evaluated system while assigning
different risks to those missing states. Their component effects and
interactions can therefore differ despite identical observations.
Additivity alone does not resolve this: one total bundle contrast can
be compatible with several allocations of additive component effects.
Identification requires sufficiently informative interventions or
additional assumptions that determine the otherwise unobserved contrasts.

Publishing prompts, controlled-artifact diffs, test results, and audit
traces makes the realized path inspectable and its controlled artifacts
reconstructable. It does not supply outcomes for unevaluated systems or
guarantee identical behavior from a stochastic or externally changing
provider. The finite trajectory also does not establish a population
causal effect of the complete revision bundle.

The framework separates conditional theory from observed evidence.
Representation converses need an independently justified source ensemble
and rate premise. Calibration needs its registered policy families and
closures. The reuse and complementarity calculations need stable stage
kernels and feasible coverage comparisons. The lifecycle calculation
additionally needs future-use, valuation, and cost premises. None is
established merely by a successful trace or a larger store.

The pilot documents realized applications, retained derivations,
failures, revisions, and prospective costs. Exposed-item replays measure
fit; the proposed $D=8$, $H=4$ protocol has four planned paired endpoint
units. These records can expose particular failures of the proposed mechanism and
provide circumstantial evidence for useful reuse. They do not establish
population superiority, an optimal admission order, or the distinct
effect of certification. The complete empirical scope is stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-scope}.

Together, the architecture, conditional analyses, and prospective protocol
connect scoped procedural support to the evidence required for claims about
its use, effect, and lifecycle value. The mathematical results specify
comparisons in which useful reuse follows from explicit premises; the
records permit inspection of actual applications without upgrading them
to those comparisons. This synthesis is broader than the particular
feasibility study and does not depend on that study validating every layer.

A shared procedural commons would additionally require evidence of
cross-user applicability, sound governance, manageable correlated
failures, and sufficient reuse to cover continuing assurance and
maintenance. The conditional calculations identify quantities to study;
the protocol makes one development path and its costs auditable.

\section{The Courtroom Loop}
\label{sec:the-courtroom-loop}

This section expands the orientation in
Section~\ref{sec:courtroom-orientation} into the operational architecture,
following the conditional theory in Section~\ref{sec:theory}.

The \emph{Courtroom loop} is a versioned assurance architecture for
constructing, revising, and reusing external procedural support. Its
\emph{inner loop} executes one frozen question-specific policy and seals
its terminal action and retained derivation. Its \emph{outer loop}
examines authorized post-seal evidence, proposes and tests revisions,
admits or rejects reusable objects, and publishes or carries forward the
next immutable release. The store can grow across questions while
remaining fixed during each scored execution.

Release manifests, sealed execution records, external adjudication, and
resource ledgers document availability, attempted use, assessed outcomes,
and costs without establishing a revision's effect or lifecycle value.
Admission certification assesses reusable claims within their declared
scope; runtime validation checks particular applications. Each release
declares which optional validation, repair, and escalation capabilities
are active.

\subsection{Running FormulaReasoning Example}
\label{subsec:courtroom-example}

In a hypothetical mass calculation using $m=\rho V$, invalid conversions
that multiply density by ten and divide volume by ten cancel numerically
while leaving the retained derivation invalid, so a canonical pass alone
does not establish full-solution validity.

\subsection{Versioned System State, Manifests, and Isolation}
\label{subsec:courtroom-state}

For a registered development horizon $D\geq1$, let $S_0$ be the starting
release and $S_t$ the complete registered client-visible release after
development episode $t\in\{1,\ldots,D\}$, with active motif and graph store
\[
M_t:=\operatorname{mem}(S_t).
\]
A release fixes the runtime implementation and configuration, active store
with its contracts and evidence, and permitted tools, resources, and
access. Release and execution manifests identify the versioned contents,
changes, execution regime, and applicable internal and outer budgets.
Provenance links do not grant runtime access to restricted artifacts.

The fixed evaluation contract is separate from changeable runtime
components. Runtime parsers and validators can change through releases
while conforming to that contract and remaining blind to hidden
references during execution. Each execution starts from a read-only
release clone; question-local state cannot modify the release or enter
another runtime session directly. A seal commits the record and its
configuration; later adjudication or correction creates a linked
append-only record. Evaluation details are in
Sections~\ref{subsec:pilot-question-sets}--\ref{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication};
manifest, session, cache, and sealing rules are in
Appendix~\ref{app:execution-replay}.

\subsection{Inner Loop: Question-Specific Use}
\label{subsec:courtroom-inner-loop}

Fix $S_{t-1}$ and development question $Q_t$. The following seven
functions may recur within the frozen stopping rules. They are
architectural functions, not the common semantic-stage decomposition
required by the reuse model.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Identify and decompose.}
Declare the target, relevant quantities, unresolved dependencies, and
local subproblems; propose semantic roles and bindings.

\item \textbf{Retrieve.}
Expose active motifs and relations through a versioned index, recording
the candidates and versions. Exposure does not establish applicability.

\item \textbf{Select, bind, and compose.}
Select support, state applicability conditions, bind question evidence or
derived values to its roles, and record composition dependencies.
Mechanical checks assess the registered structural requirements without
necessarily establishing semantic binding. The frozen policy routes to
support or fallback before execution; it need not detect that the
baseline would fail.

\item \textbf{Execute and record.}
Perform permitted operations and record their inputs, outputs, versions,
and checks, using the common answer and derivation interface for both
supported and fallback routes.

\item \textbf{Seal the pre-gate proposal.}
Seal the proposed disposition, answer, retained derivation, and supporting
trace before optional runtime validation. This does not open the external
adjudication or development gate.

\item \textbf{Validate and route.}
An active validator receives a read-only proposal view and recommends
release, bounded repair, deferral, or escalation through the frozen route.
Repairs preserve the original proposal, use only permitted evidence, and
remain within resource limits. No route receives the hidden reference.

\item \textbf{Seal the final action.}
Seal the terminal answer or no-answer disposition, retained derivation,
linked validation and repair records, and resource account before
external adjudication or revision.
\end{enumerate}

Validation, repair, fallback, and record construction all consume
execution resources. A formally legal but semantically incorrect action
can remain undetected.

\subsection{Working Graphs, Retained Derivations, and Adjudication}
\label{subsec:courtroom-derivations}

The working graph records hypotheses, candidate bindings, attempted
transformations, rejected actions, and superseded values. The submitted
solution selects its retained claims and their transitive dependencies;
the audit trace also preserves discarded work and its costs. Neither
graph membership nor a correct final number admits a claim to the
persistent store.

Repair must recompute or revalidate affected surviving dependencies under
the corrected inputs; changing a validity flag is insufficient.
Superseded claims remain identifiable, and the terminal record fixes
which versions support the answer. Withdrawn exploratory mistakes need
not invalidate a repaired solution, whereas an invalid retained
dependency remains a defect.

Canonical correctness and full-solution validity are distinct endpoints
under Equations~\eqref{eq:theory-canonical-acceptance}
and~\eqref{eq:theory-full-solution-loss}. Empirical adjudicators assess
mathematical validity from sealed evidence under a frozen rubric; their
judgments are fallible and can remain unresolved. Runtime validation can
influence the current attempt through its frozen route, but external
adjudication cannot repair or replace the submission.
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication} specifies assessments and
Appendix~\ref{app:audit-microscopes} specifies trace coding.

\subsection{Outer Loop: Audit, Revision, Admission, and Release}
\label{subsec:courtroom-outer-loop}

Authorized post-seal evidence can inform diagnosis, candidate
construction, scoped admission checks, and tests. Candidates are
accepted, rejected, or left unresolved with evidence, reasons, and
costs. A retained defect can motivate revision even when the final number
passes, but the diagnosis remains a hypothesis: a generalized procedure
and each proposed relation require their own admission evidence.

The \emph{release controller} alone publishes persistent state after
checking verdicts, tests, permissions, anti-leakage controls, and required
records; other components propose changes or evidence without direct
write authority. If no revision is accepted, record a no-change release
$S_t=S_{t-1}$. Revisions must respect the immutable outer caps and study
invariants. A co-bundled admission-policy revision cannot lower a
candidate's standard retroactively.

Reference access does not authorize runtime memorization of the
motivating item. Releases cannot contain item identifiers, literal or
paraphrased questions, reference answers, source-instance keys, or
equivalent lookups. Reference-derived fixtures remain in the isolated
test partition; reusable proposals retain scope and provenance.
The pilot's embargo, launch, adaptation, and replay rules are specified in
Sections~\ref{subsec:pilot-development-episode}
and~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory}.

\subsection{Assurance-Bearing Procedural Store}
\label{subsec:courtroom-store}

The active store $M_t$ contains typed, versioned motifs and relations.
Each motif carries a payload, semantic input/output roles, applicability
conditions and limitations, provenance, assurance evidence and its scope,
tests including observed failures, and an immutable admission and
revision history. Relations express dependencies, applicability,
composition, conflicts, or supersession with their own scope and
evidence. A composition claim must establish when one procedure's output
meets another's input contract; matching names or units alone is
insufficient.

Ordinary retrieval exposes only active admitted versions. Revocation or
rollback creates a new release while preserving history, so empirical
active stores need not be nested. Four assurance channels remain
distinct:
\begin{description}
\item[Mechanical checks.]
Deterministic checks establish only the specified structural, type, unit,
provenance, precondition, or postcondition properties they cover.

\item[Admission certification.]
A registered combination of proof, recomputation, tests, provenance
inspection, and uncertainty assessment supports reusable claims within
their declared scope. An
empirical acceptance verdict does not prove the universal soundness
assumed by the idealized reuse model.

\item[Runtime instance validation.]
A frozen validator assesses a particular interpretation, applicability,
binding, composition, or execution; its recommendation acts only through
the registered bounded route.

\item[Escalation.]
A bounded channel requests permitted evidence or review without bypassing
sealing, admission, or release control. The primary first-contact study
permits no live operator assistance.
\end{description}
These channels can share models, tools, assumptions, and blind spots;
their errors are not assumed independent. Failed and inconclusive checks
remain recorded and charged.

\subsection{Resource Ledger and Audit Trail}
\label{subsec:courtroom-resources}

The ledger links executions, proposals, tests, verdicts, and releases to
native resource events and active human effort without overwriting
earlier records. Shared physical work counts once; retries, failures,
rejected proposals, abandoned revisions, and unused admitted objects
retain their costs. Latency and peak storage remain distinct from
additive consumption. Unrecorded historical work remains unknown, not
zero. Observed expenditure does not establish future gain, cost bounds,
or lifecycle value. Phase accounting and detailed schemas are specified
in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-resource-ledger} and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledgers-manifest};
Section~\ref{subsubsec:reuse-lifecycle-value} states the separate
premises for lifecycle comparisons.

\subsection{Connection to the Layered Theory}
\label{subsec:courtroom-theory}

Scoped admission, application checks, semantic interfaces, and resource
accounting correspond to premises of the reuse model in
Section~\ref{subsec:verified-procedural-growth}; component presence does
not establish those premises. A larger index can impair selection,
a prompt change can alter fallback reliability, and validation can
displace later computation. The seven architectural functions do not
automatically supply the model's common stage decomposition or stable
conditional kernels. The study records outcomes, uses, and costs along
one path; it does not estimate family optima or verify the model's uniform
premises.

\subsection{Study Objective and Registration Boundary}

The study records numerical and assessed full-solution outcomes, procedural

The two-stage registration in Section~\ref{subsec:ledger-registration}
seals all prespecified rules, $S_0$, permissions and resource bounds before

prospective item is revealed. Reference inspection cannot change an evaluator
constant, rule, seed, permission or cap. Until the commitment exists, this is
a design to be registered. Historical work in $S_0$ is disclosed as observed, partly observed or unknown;
missing historical costs are not zero.

A reference custodian is technically and procedurally isolated from
development, runtime, retrieval, revision tests, and confirmatory execution.
Locked forms and reconciliation rules govern reference audit and eligibility.
Unreleased questions, targets, programs, annotations, eligibility decisions,

and disclosure.

Pre-execution conformance checks use registered synthetic or otherwise
permitted non-study examples for the numerical evaluator and retained
solution interface. The latter includes valid alternative derivations,
binding and unit mistakes, cancelling errors, unsupported answers, stale
dependencies, valid repairs, ambiguous evidence, and assessment-cap
exhaustion. A canonicalizer or required-solution-interface defect, or an
insufficient eligible pool, stops
the study before the first question is revealed. Repair requires a dated

Registration constrains human information, authority, budgets, stopping and

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Question Sets, Dependence-Family Separation, and Reference-Isolated Adjudication}

\label{subsec:pilot-question-sets}

FormulaReasoning provides formula-structured numerical tasks and an external
formula database \citep{li2026formulareasoning}. Registration identifies the
exact dataset release, repository revision, selected split, and file hashes.
The registered question sets are

\begin{align}
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{dev}}
&:=
\bigl((Q_t,Y_t)\bigr)_{t=1}^{D},
\label{eq:pilot-development-stream}\\
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{term}}
&:=
\bigl\{(Q_h^{\mathrm{term}},Y_h^{\mathrm{term}}):h=1,\ldots,H\bigr\}.
\label{eq:pilot-terminal-panel}
\end{align}

For every candidate entering reference audit, the record keeps the published benchmark value $Y_i^{\mathrm{pub}}$ separate from any verified pre-canonical target. An item is eligible only if the frozen procedure successfully produces and certifies an exact typed computational target $Y_i^{\mathrm{ver}}$; for eligible items, set $Y_i:=Y_i^{\mathrm{ver}}$. The published record is a reconciliation datum, not a source of padded digits. The pool-flow report lists every disagreement among the verified target, the published answer, and the released repository metric. Six significant digits is a study-defined computational-resolution convention that treats displayed problem constants as exact task constants; it is not a claim about physical measurement precision or the benchmark authors' intended significant figures.

The eligible FormulaReasoning pool is grouped before sampling by underlying source problem, generated direction or variant, and direct template sibling. An exclusion census searches every available historical notebook, run artifact, cache, prompt, microscope log, test fixture, and motif/store provenance record. Every question whose prior use in prompt design, code development, motif construction, certification trials, regression testing, or item-level microscopes is known to the team or identified by the registered census is excluded together with its direct source, generated, and template siblings. Candidate-pool counts and exclusions are reported at each stage, together with the residual possibility of undocumented historical exposure. The eight development questions and four terminal questions are drawn from twelve distinct registered dependence-family clusters. This construction removes the declared direct relationships; it does not prove statistical independence because broader formula, template, or latent source conventions may still be shared, and it does not establish that a hosted model did not encounter an item during pretraining. Identifiers and order are hidden from the human operator and runtime system until the registered controller releases them. Here $Q_i$ denotes the complete benchmark input, including all evidence supplied with the question.

The development questions are prospectively fresh at first contact, but they cease to be held out as soon as their question, answer, or trace is used in revision. The terminal panel is \emph{prospectively untouched}: its identities and contents remain inaccessible to the human--API development process throughout the registered experiment. Only after $S_8$ and the adaptation ledger have been frozen and the complete final-development replay batch has been sealed may the reference-stripped terminal items be released to the isolated terminal-execution controller. Neither terminal inputs nor terminal outcomes may return to the development process or trigger another release. Reusable motif-family overlap is permitted and logged because procedural transfer is part of the object of study; reuse of the same source instance or a direct sibling is prohibited leakage.

The canonicalization and adjudication contract is frozen before reference inspection and execution. Let $u(q)$ be the frozen metadata projection containing only the registered target quantity kind, dimension, canonical unit, bare-number and missing-unit rules, and percentage--ratio scale. Its vocabulary is finite. The projection is obtained either by deterministic extraction from named public question fields or by reference-blind annotation finalized on a locked form before the annotator may inspect any hidden reference value, reference program, canonical target, adjudication field, or system output; provenance and the frozen conflict-resolution rule are retained. The finalized item-level $u(q)$ record is included in the reference-stripped runtime item and exposed identically to every evaluated release and to any registered terminal wrapper using the finite-canonical construction. The same solver-relevant output schema, unit vocabulary, and scale conventions are exposed to every evaluated release. The scorer uses $c_q=\bar c_{u(q)}$ and may not execute the problem's formulas, inspect a reference value or program, or choose an item-specific precision, exponent interval, or plausibility range. Any explicit task-rounding directive is frozen separately as part of the reference function and applied before canonicalization. Let $\mathcal O$ be the common terminal action space, and let

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{ans}:\mathcal O
\longrightarrow
\widehat{\mathcal Y}_{\varnothing}
\end{equation}

return the complete released raw answer object carried by the terminal solution record $o$, with $\operatorname{ans}(o)=\varnothing$ for a no-answer, deferral, cap-exhausted, or launched-branch infrastructure-failure disposition. The complete solution record may contain many intermediate numbers, but $\operatorname{ans}$ reads only the registered terminal-answer fields and may not promote an intermediate value to the final answer. Multiple numerical candidates, ambiguous parses, or extra answer-bearing fields remain represented in the raw object and are malformed under $c_q$, which maps them to $\bot$; no parser may select a favorable last number. Recall that the registered canonicalizer satisfies $c_q(\varnothing)=\bot$. For each eligible item $i$, define

\begin{equation}
Z_i:=c_{Q_i}(Y_i),
\qquad
Z_i\neq\bot,
\label{eq:pilot-canonical-target}
\end{equation}

where applying $c_{Q_i}$ to the verified reference uses the jointly measurable reference renderer registered in Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The exact canonical numerical acceptance flag and loss are

\begin{align}
\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(o)
&:=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
c_{Q_i}\!\left(\operatorname{ans}(o)\right)=Z_i
\right\},
\label{eq:pilot-adjudication-predicate}
\\
\ell_i^{\mathrm{can}}(o)
&:=
1-\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(o)
=
\ell^{\mathrm{can}}(Q_i,Y_i,o).
\label{eq:pilot-item-loss}
\end{align}

The raw answer and reference renderer use the same typed schema and exact
decoder, percentage--ratio conversion, compatible-unit conversion, scale
normalization, and finite-grid code. The contract fixes quantity kind,
dimension, canonical unit, absolute-temperature versus interval semantics,
affine conversions, exact conversion factors, bare-number and missing-unit
rules, and unit grammar. Distinct labels are unequal with no subsequent
tolerance. Answer-release coverage uses
$I_i^{\mathrm{ans}}(o):=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{ans}(o)\neq\varnothing\}$.
A malformed, ambiguous, nonfinite, dimensionally incompatible, or out-of-range
supplied answer is answered but incorrect; a no-answer, deferral,
cap-exhausted, or handled launched-failure disposition is unanswered and
maps to $\bot$. On eligible analysis-valid executions both have loss one.
Coverage, accepted-answer
risk, dispositions, and resources are reported separately from numerical
loss; any composite utility is secondary and prospectively specified.

Before prospective execution, every reference record must be recomputed and validated with exact rational or decimal arithmetic, or with certified interval arithmetic. Any explicit instruction such as ``round to the nearest integer'' belongs to the reference function before canonicalization and does not change the global precision. The renderer retains guard digits; it does not render to six digits and then quantize a second time. The formula, bound values, quantity kind, dimensions, units, and published answer are independently checked. A published answer that is coarser than six digits is not padded or silently replaced: the recomputation must be compatible with its implied rounding cell, or the item is declared ineligible under the frozen preallocation rule.

The sole primary precision in this prospective protocol is

\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{FR}}=6
\end{equation}

significant decimal digits, not decimal places. The registered normalized-exponent bounds satisfy $e_{\min},e_{\max}\in\mathbb Z$ and $e_{\min}\leq e_{\max}$. After exact typed normalization, a nonzero result is expressed in normalized base-10 scientific form, rounded once by round-to-nearest, ties-to-even, renormalized after any significand carry, and only then checked against the global finite exponent interval $[e_{\min},e_{\max}]$. Binary floating-point rounding is not used. A certified reference interval must lie wholly within one six-digit cell or establish an exact tie; otherwise the item is ineligible before allocation. Every signed-zero form maps to one zero label, while a nonzero value whose rounded-and-renormalized exponent lies outside the interval maps to $\bot$ rather than being clipped or rounded to zero. Specializing Equation~\eqref{eq:theory-canonical-alphabet-size} to $p_{\mathrm{FR}}=6$, with $N_e=e_{\max}-e_{\min}+1$, the full signed grid has

\begin{equation}
K=\lvert\mathcal Z_{\bot}\rvert=2+18\cdot10^5N_e,
\label{eq:pilot-canonical-alphabet-size}
\end{equation}

including normalized zero and $\bot$. The exponent bounds are global operational-contract choices made before
reference inspection from a publicly specified finite decimal representation,
not selected target values or item-specific ranges. The execution manifest
reports their numerical values, associated candidate exclusions, $K$, $\log_2(K-1)$, and the conditional
$\Psi_K$ curve. Without an estimate of $A_{s,B}^{\mathrm{can}}$, no observed
point or empirical floor is inferred from that curve. The conformance suite
covers answer cardinality, equivalent units, percentage--ratio and affine
temperature forms, bare and missing units, trailing zeros, rounding and exact
ties, significand carry, exponent boundaries, signed zero, and malformed,
ambiguous, nonfinite, and out-of-range values. A preregistered wider-bound
sensitivity reports verdict changes.

Only after the full confirmatory sealing point has been reached does the isolated adjudication process compute or reveal preregistered secondary sensitivity summaries at five and seven significant digits and reproduce the released FormulaReasoning baseline's strict $1\%$ relative-error implementation. These secondary adjudications and all of their derivatives remain unavailable to the outer development process and confirmatory-execution controllers before that point. Any repository-metric edge case, including a zero denominator, is reported as the released implementation handles it and is not silently repaired post hoc. These secondary scores never alter the six-digit primary verdict, and every item whose verdict changes across rules is listed. Historical outputs scored only under an earlier $5\%$ rule are labeled noncomparable and are not imported into a primary denominator.

Runtime and adjudication records are physically separated. Each model-visible
item contains the complete question and supplied evidence, public $u(q)$,
safe nonidentifying metadata, and an opaque cell identifier. Dataset, source,
variant, template, allocation, future-phase, reference, grading, and target
identifiers or fields remain in custodied ledgers. No within-question solver,
retriever, binder, executor, validator, repair, escalation, or pre-seal
certification channel receives adjudication records. Authorized developmental
feedback must pass through the release process. Batch scoring, shadow scoring,
and external assessment obey Section~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory}.
The reference custodian remains output-blind and cannot exercise
output-specific scoring discretion; an unresolved output parse maps to $\bot$.

Target-loss eligibility and exclusion are output-blind: they may not depend on a system output, correctness verdict, release, or branch. Wherever operationally possible, the isolated reference custodian applies the frozen rule without access to system outcomes. An item is never silently replaced after results are observed. A later-discovered contamination is retained in the public as-run record, assigned a deviation code, and excluded from any analysis whose registration requires uncontaminated status. A later discovery that an item is ill posed, that its reference maps to $\bot$, or that an item- or reference-level canonical contract is inapplicable may justify exclusion only when established independently of every solver output under the frozen rule. The record is retained without replacement, and the effect on every planned, launched, analysis-valid, target-loss-eligible, primary-included, and paired denominator is reported; scheduled and planned counts remain fixed, and the exclusion count is reported separately. A solver-output parse, grammar, ambiguity, or range edge case maps to $\bot$ under the frozen scorer and cannot retroactively make the item ineligible; a scorer-software defect is handled as a protocol deviation and sensitivity analysis rather than an output-conditioned exclusion. Because one terminal item has high leverage, release-specific late-exclusion sensitivities code each affected cell as loss zero and loss one. For a paired contrast, the reported lower and upper sensitivities are the minimum and maximum over the Cartesian product of the four assignments $(0,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$ for every excluded terminal pair. If a cell is both unlaunched and later found item-ineligible, item ineligibility takes precedence: the cell remains outside both the primary and pessimistic noncompletion denominators and enters only the registered late-exclusion sensitivity. None of these sensitivities is relabelled as the registered primary analysis.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Retained Solutions and Full-Solution Adjudication}
\label{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication}

The mathematical endpoint is
$\ell^{\mathrm{sol}}(q,y,o)$ from
Equation~\ref{eq:theory-full-solution-loss}: the canonical answer
must be correct and the retained derivation must be valid. The study
assesses this endpoint using a frozen evidence contract and rubric.
An empirical assessment is not infallible access to
$\operatorname{Valid}(q,o)$.

\paragraph{The sealed solution record.}

Every launched policy uses the same terminal evidence contract, fixed at
the accounting seal and disclosed to every release. The sealed solution
identifies the target, givens, assumptions and unit/sign conventions;
retained claims, semantic bindings, inputs and justifications; dependencies
to the answer, including used object/relation versions, scope and checks;
executed expressions/code and results supporting tool-derived claims; and
the final retained state, distinguished from discarded or repaired work.
Supporting certificates and records must be identifiable, not inferred from
a release name or admission verdict. The answer needs a supported path to
the givens and registered mathematical/physical primitives.

This is an explicit submitted solution and observable evidence, not a demand
for a provider's hidden internal reasoning. Exact values, approximations and
intervals have distinct meanings; approximations need justification at the
claimed resolution.

Discarded exploratory errors need not invalidate the final solution. Repairs
must replace or justify affected claims and recompute or revalidate surviving
dependents; changed flags, stale descendants and cancelling mistakes do not
suffice. Construction, selection, rendering and checking consume runtime
resources. Later assessors cannot supply missing work or select a more
favorable graph after reference access.

Failure dispositions may retain partial or empty derivations with reasons;
canonical failure already settles full-solution failure. Catastrophic
incompleteness without a terminal action has no fabricated solution or
assessment (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-retry-policy}). By contrast, missing
or unassessable evidence on an existing terminal submission does not erase
its numerical execution record.

\paragraph{Frozen rubric and assessment procedure.}

The accounting seal fixes assessor roles/qualifications or automated
configurations, evidence, tools, reconciliation and record requirements,
and common per-record cap $B_{\mathrm{assess}}$ across versions and phases.
Checking is charged to adaptation or evaluation without enlarging runtime
budgets or authorizing corrective solver calls. The assessor is separate
from runtime validation, admission and the microscope, and cannot change
a submission or output-blind eligibility.

The rubric checks interpretation/assumptions, formula scope, binding,
dimensions/units/signs, arithmetic or justified approximation, dependency and
repair integrity, and support for the answer. Alternative valid derivations
are accepted without matching the reference's wording or order.

Each read-only pseudonymized packet contains the question, public metadata,
submitted derivation and permitted evidence. Branch, release, phase,
comparative outcomes and canonical verdict are masked where unnecessary;
objects use neutral identifiers linked to custodied hashes. Record recognizable
content and other blinding limits; do not supply a counterpart solution as
a template. Role overlap with operator/model/reference functions is disclosed,
not treated as independent error control.

The first pass records checked evidence and invalid/unresolved dependencies
before joining the canonical verdict. Further reference-assisted checking
requires its registered procedure and release gate. Preserve initial and
reconciled judgments, reasons, responsible identities/configurations and
resources; neither operator nor microscope can override them to make an
episode successful.

For a sealed record $o$, the final derivation assessment is
\begin{equation}
d_i(o)\in
\{\mathrm{valid},\mathrm{invalid},\mathrm{unassessable}\}.
\label{eq:pilot-derivation-assessment}
\end{equation}
Here \emph{valid} means that the assessor has checked the required
retained claims and dependencies and recorded supporting evidence.
\emph{Invalid} requires an identified retained false claim, invalid
inference, or violated applicability or dependency condition, located
in the record. \emph{Unassessable} covers missing evidence, unresolved
semantic ambiguity or disagreement, unavailable necessary checks, and
assessment-cap exhaustion without a resolved verdict. A known invalid
retained step is sufficient for an invalid judgment even if other
parts remain unresolved.

Tool success, validator/admission endorsement, reference-number agreement
or unsupported LLM approval alone cannot establish validity. Where meaningful,
locate the earliest invalid retained dependencies and affected descendants;
several minimal defects may coexist. These diagnostics do not add partial
credit. Seal the assessment after permitted checks; unresolved disagreement
stays unassessable. Dated corrections preserve earlier verdicts and their
reported effects. All checking and reconciliation is charged.

\paragraph{Combining the two correctness readouts.}

Each report declares its cutoff and records the assessment process state
as not started, in progress, completed, or a failed attempt, including any
gate still awaited. Define $\widetilde d_i(o)$ as the reporting value:
invalid takes precedence whenever a supported retained defect is already
established under the registered rubric and access gates. Otherwise use
the supported completed judgment $d_i(o)$ when available, and report
pending while assessment work remains permitted by the original gates,
cap, and retry rules. Failed attempts preserve their reasons and costs;
if no supported completed judgment is available and no permitted work
remains, record unassessable unless invalidity is established. Cap
exhaustion and assessment-procedure violations retain
their registered dispositions rather than being relabelled pending.
Pending is an availability marker, not a completed unassessable judgment.
Only completed checking of all required claims can support valid.
Preserve findings, timestamps, gates, and later linked corrections.

Define the assessed full-solution status at that cutoff by
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{status}^{\mathrm{sol}}_i(o)
:=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{pass},
&\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(o)=1,\
\widetilde d_i(o)=\mathrm{valid},\\
\mathrm{fail},
&\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(o)=0
\ \text{or}\ \widetilde d_i(o)=\mathrm{invalid},\\
\mathrm{unresolved},
&\operatorname{ok}^{\mathrm{can}}_i(o)=1,\
\widetilde d_i(o)\in\{\mathrm{unassessable},\mathrm{pending}\}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:pilot-solution-status}
\end{equation}
Missing or unresolved derivation evidence does not make an item ineligible,
remove it from the canonical denominator, or turn it into an unlaunched cell.

The unresolved-assessment and paired-contrast ranges in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:full-solution-assessments} are reported
conditionally on the resolved judgments; they do not correct for
mistaken valid or invalid judgments.

\paragraph{Timing and feedback authority.}

Adaptive first-contact assessment requires a sealed terminal solution
record and may begin only after both scheduled branch records have
sealed and the adaptive branch has launched.
Its sealed assessment may inform the authorized post-seal development
process. For an official same-item replay in episodes $t<8$, the
assessment may inform later episodes only after the replay has sealed;
it cannot reopen the release decision for episode $t$.
Episode-8 replay assessment is evaluation-only after adaptation
closure.

Full-solution adjudication of the shadow, final-development batch,
and terminal batch is deferred until the full confirmatory sealing
point, or the registered abort-sealing certificate. This stronger
gate also covers human review, model-assisted checking, and every
derived assessment field. It does not change the separately specified
permission for isolated mechanical canonical scoring after a complete
confirmatory batch has sealed. No full-solution feedback can influence
a confirmatory execution. All assessments use the same frozen rubric,
with their actual gate, evidence access, and checking resources logged.

\subsection{Frozen Releases and the Starting Shadow}

\label{subsec:pilot-frozen-releases}

The complete release after episode $t$ is $S_t$, with a content-addressed
manifest and development budget $B_t$. The requested base model, provider,
decoding configuration and provider-retry policy are invariant through $S_8$.
Each attempt uses a read-only release clone. Runtime components, the separate
frozen evaluation manifest, execution observations and comparability are
specified in Section~\ref{subsec:execution-manifests}. The evaluation manifest
also fixes item assignment and $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ under
Section~\ref{subsec:ledger-registration}. ``Frozen'' is client-visible,
not a guarantee against hidden provider drift.

For each development item $Q_t$, two first-contact branches are scheduled before the reference is revealed. A launched adaptive cell executes under $(S_{t-1},B_{t-1})$, while a launched shadow cell executes under $(S_0,B_0)$. Here and below, $\operatorname{Run}(S,Q;\xi)$ also depends on the applicable branch- and phase-specific execution manifest, interaction environment, budget, cache, and reset policy; suppressing those indices does not make $S$ alone determine the execution or its law. Let $\mathsf{Rec}_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}$ and $\mathsf{Rec}_t^0$ denote the sealed scheduled-branch records. For a launched branch that durably seals a terminal action, the corresponding record and action satisfy

\begin{align}
\mathsf{Rec}_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}
&:=
\operatorname{Seal}\!\left(
\operatorname{Run}(S_{t-1},Q_t;\xi_t^{\mathrm{adapt}})
\right),
\label{eq:pilot-adaptive-first-contact}\\
\mathsf{Rec}_t^{0}
&:=
\operatorname{Seal}\!\left(
\operatorname{Run}(S_0,Q_t;\xi_t^{0})
\right),
\label{eq:pilot-shadow-first-contact}\\
\widehat O_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}
&:=\operatorname{term}(\mathsf{Rec}_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}),
\qquad
\widehat O_t^0
:=\operatorname{term}(\mathsf{Rec}_t^0).
\label{eq:pilot-first-contact-actions}
\end{align}

The variables $\xi_t^{\mathrm{adapt}}$ and $\xi_t^0$ collect observable or
controllable randomness and provider metadata; request identifiers are not
seeds. The map $\operatorname{term}$ extracts the sealed terminal solution
record and its immutable evidence links. Every scheduled cell receives an
execution or status record, but an unlaunched or catastrophically incomplete
cell has no fabricated terminal action or loss. Launch and failure rules are
defined in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-retry-policy}.

Development pairing is a whole-system comparison: the adaptive budget
$B_{t-1}$ may differ from the shadow's $B_0$. The separate matched
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ applies only to final-development and terminal evaluation.
Branch caps and realized consumption accompany outcomes. At $t=1$, both
branches use $S_0$ in two isolated realizations.

The shadow is evaluation instrumentation and supplies no developmental
feedback. Its outcome-bearing records remain embargoed until the
\emph{confirmatory sealing point}: $S_8$ and the adaptation ledger are frozen
and all sixteen final-development and terminal records have sealed.
Sections~\ref{subsec:execution-development}--\ref{subsec:execution-isolation}
specify fixed branch slots, quotas, provider-plane separation, computation
and disclosure gates, and the alternative irrevocable abort-sealing
certificate. A premature shadow disclosure loses the isolated-shadow
interpretation under Section~\ref{subsec:ledger-deviations}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Sequential Development Episode}

\label{subsec:pilot-development-episode}

For $t=1,\ldots,D$, the controller follows this sequence.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Reveal, run, and seal first contact.}
Release only the current reference-stripped runtime item to the scheduled
adaptive $S_{t-1}$ and shadow $S_0$ branches. Each uses fresh local state and
its frozen policy without human intervention. The terminal answer, retained
derivation, and supporting evidence seal together.

\item \textbf{Adjudicate and diagnose through the adaptive gate.}
After both scheduled records seal, the reference may enter the authorized
development channel only if the adaptive branch launched. An existing
terminal action receives mechanical canonical scoring and separate
derivation assessment, with the canonical verdict masked during the first
assessment pass. The operator and bounded microscope may then inspect only
the permitted, actually available artifacts. Catastrophic incompleteness
follows the deviation path without reconstructed output or assessment.
Nonlaunch permits no item-driven diagnosis or revision and leads to a
no-change release or the registered study stop. The shadow remains hidden.

\item \textbf{Propose and test reusable revisions.}
The operator may change permitted runtime prompts, controller code, schemas,
parsers, tools, retrieval, validators, executors, motifs, relations, tests,
or development budgets. The study invariants in
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-frozen-releases} and all sealing rules remain fixed.
A proposal may be motivated by the current question but may not branch on
its identifier, embed its answer, hide a literal or paraphrased question
as a runtime exemplar, encode a source-instance lookup, or introduce an
unreported item-specific exception. Reference-derived current-item fixtures
stay in an isolated test partition unavailable to runtime and retrieval.
Reusable rules retain scope and provenance. All tests and external accesses
obey the frozen corpus, synthetic-generation, repository, and egress rules;
unrevealed candidates and answer sources may not be searched.

\item \textbf{Decide every candidate.}
Record Accept, Reject, Defer, or Ambiguous, with reasons, tests, and costs.
Only accepted candidates enter the release. Motif and relation candidates
use the admission policy active at the start of the episode. A co-bundled
policy revision takes effect only after those decisions and cannot certify
its own candidates retroactively. Numerical success, assessed full-solution
success, or a runtime-validator pass does not automatically admit working
nodes or relations. Every reusable claim needs its own scoped admission
evidence; invalid or unresolved intermediate material remains audit evidence
unless a separately checked repair or resolution supports a candidate.

\item \textbf{Freeze the next release.}
For accepted bundle $\Delta_t^{\mathrm{acc}}$, set
\begin{equation}
S_t=\operatorname{Freeze}\!\left(S_{t-1}\oplus\Delta_t^{\mathrm{acc}}\right).
\label{eq:pilot-version-transition}
\end{equation}
Seal the full diff, component hashes, tests, rationale, and resource links.
If no change is accepted, record $S_t=S_{t-1}$. A nonlaunched adaptive episode
opens no item-driven candidate and follows this no-change path unless a
registered stop applies.

\item \textbf{Replay after a changed release.}
If $S_t\neq S_{t-1}$, schedule exactly one official same-item replay under
$(S_t,B_t)$; otherwise mark it structurally inapplicable. Seal its terminal
or status record before any replay adjudication. It cannot reopen the
episode's release decision or authorize another replay. For $t<8$, authorized
findings may inform later episodes. Episode 8 first freezes $S_8$, closes
the adaptation ledger, and revokes adaptation write authority; its replay
is then evaluation-only. Its record must seal before final-development
evaluation begins. Section~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory} supplies
the exact gates.
\end{enumerate}

Register immutable caps on calls, native tokens, charges, tools/compute,
elapsed time and active human effort. Each cap is registered as
episode-limiting, adaptation-stream-limiting, cell-nonlaunching, or globally
stopping; its treatment is fixed before outcomes. Release budgets $B_t$ stay
within this envelope. Exhausting the adaptation allocation stops revisions
and yields later no-change releases while protecting the reserved evaluation
allocation, unless a global stop applies. The field-by-field matched outer
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ uses frozen endpoint-configuration hashes, leaves internal
routing/stopping intact and permits different realized consumption.

Replay fit and numerical/full-solution transitions have the descriptive
interpretation in Section~\ref{subsec:audit-interpretation}, not identified
causal repair or fresh transfer.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Inference, Retry, and Branch-Stopping Rules}

\label{subsec:pilot-retry-policy}

The manifest fixes a phase-independent launch boundary. A scheduled cell becomes launched at the earliest of (i) durable handoff of $Q$ or any question-bearing field to a branch-visible component and (ii) commencement of any branch-specific operation of the frozen within-question policy, including a registered local operation. The controller atomically appends the launch event at or before that handoff or operation. An unlaunched cell therefore means that the question remained confined to the isolated schedule controller and was never visible to the solver, retriever, tools, branch-local infrastructure, human operator, or microscope. Exact-response reuse is permitted only in the separately labelled secondary deployment-cache condition and never constitutes a primary scheduled cell. A failure before the launch boundary produces an explicit unexecuted record; after that boundary, a failure cannot be reclassified as nonlaunch. Any physically executed preflight, control, or infrastructure events are logged separately. If an adaptive first-contact cell does not launch, the episode records no target loss and permits no question-, output-, or adjudication-driven diagnosis or revision; absent a separately registered study-stop rule, it freezes a no-change release.

Every released system fixes its own inference budget and terminal policy within the immutable study caps. In the proposed pilot implementation, the language model proposes one local action per call. A formally invalid, unparsable, or schema-invalid returned action consumes one semantic action attempt. At most two action attempts are permitted for a step, and the manifest also fixes finite question-level maxima for total semantic actions, model calls, tool calls, and runtime. Reaching any maximum stops that branch with the registered no-answer, cap-exhausted, or infrastructure disposition. A semantically poor but formally legal action is not intercepted merely because an auditor would prefer another reasoning path. It proceeds under the frozen system until a terminal proposal or another registered stopping condition is reached.

Provider retries are distinct from semantic action retries. Retry eligibility is content-independent and limited to preregistered transport or service states for which no usable response was returned. An eligible retry repeats the identical request hash; every physical call is logged and charged. A timeout is recorded as an execution of unknown remote completion status and is charged whenever observed or billed. The first eligible usable response is the response used: the controller may not choose among returned responses on the basis of their content. The manifest fixes eligible status codes, maximum provider attempts, backoff, timeout, and exhaustion disposition.

Repair, deferral, and escalation inside a runtime attempt are available only if they are already encoded in the released system. Their prompts, evidence interfaces, call limits, deterministic rechecks, and fallbacks are frozen in that version. No active branch may expand into an unbounded sequence of corrective calls. Once a scheduled branch has launched, exhaustion of its frozen runtime budget or a provider/infrastructure failure handled by its retry rule ordinarily produces a controller-sealed registered terminal disposition, maps to $\bot$, and receives canonical and full-solution loss one when the execution is otherwise analysis-valid and the item is target-loss eligible. If a catastrophic post-launch failure prevents even the preregistered watchdog from durably sealing that disposition, the record is launched-but-incomplete and analysis-invalid, with null terminal-action and loss fields; the controller does not fabricate an output. Any later response from the failed or timed-out request is quarantined and may neither replace the sealed record nor enter a later-visible cache. By contrast, a study-level cap or infrastructure failure that prevents the branch from launching yields an unexecuted scheduled cell: it is recorded as missing and as protocol noncompletion, not as an answer produced by the scheduled release. The mandatory pessimistic eligible planned-cell feasibility summary assigns such an otherwise target-loss-eligible cell loss one while preserving its nonoutput status; it is not substituted for the primary analysis-valid executed-cell canonical-loss summary.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Scope of Inference}
\label{subsec:pilot-scope}

The study documents one $S_0\to S_8$ development path. It reports first-contact
outcomes, exposed-item fit, four prospectively untouched endpoint pairs,
procedural-use evidence, unresolved judgments, and resources. Its summaries
are descriptive, without population $p$-values, confidence intervals, or
claims of population superiority. Single hosted-model realizations do not
estimate decoding variability. Pairing fixes the realized item but does not
randomize versions, remove provider drift, or identify component-wise or
whole-bundle causal effects.

The reuse model motivates checking applicability, binding, retained validity,
fallback and continuation resources. A baseline error is allowed by that
model, and regression alone locates no failed premise. This one path tests
neither complementarity nor admission-order effects and does not establish
uniform population conditions.

Because $Z=c_Q(Y)$ is determined by the complete question, useful support
need not add conditional Shannon information. The pilot estimates no
population risks, policy-family optima/accessibility residuals, stage
probabilities, predictive or induced-solution log loss. Finite numerical
representatives establish neither same-budget closure nor coverage of valid
full derivations; observed errors are not family-optimal floors. Assessment
ranges do not correct mistaken resolved judgments.

Resource records document observed consumption within the declared
boundary, not recovery of unknown history. Lifecycle-value scenarios
additionally require justified gain, future-use, cost, and valuation
premises; they do not demonstrate cost-effectiveness. A shared knowledge
commons would also need valid cross-context reuse sufficient for continuing
assurance, governance and maintenance. Controlled order, assurance-policy
and stage interventions remain follow-ups requiring their own frozen
platforms, registries, environments and budgets.

checks, freeze $S_8$ and the \emph{adaptation ledger}: the version/revision
ledger and every adaptation-scope execution, resource, candidate, test,
verdict, and human-action record through episode 8. Revoke all adaptation
write authority irrevocably. The overall execution and resource ledgers
remain append-only for subsequent evaluation events.

Only then may the episode-8 replay run as evaluation-only instrumentation,
unavailable to former adaptation principals before authorized disclosure.
Its terminal or status record---including structural inapplicability,
nonlaunch, or catastrophic incompleteness---must seal before the
final-development batch begins; otherwise apply the stop or abort rule.

The confirmatory sealing point requires all three conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item $S_8$ and the adaptation ledger are frozen;
    \item all eight final $S_8$ development-replay records are sealed;
    \item all eight terminal version--item records from applying
    $S_0$ and $S_8$ to the four terminal questions are sealed.
\end{enumerate}
Explicit unexecuted and launched-but-incomplete status records count
toward closure of the scheduled record set when durably sealed under
the registered deviation rules. They supply no solver output and do
not enter the primary analysis-valid executed-cell denominators.
The separate eligible planned-cell noncompletion coding remains
governed by Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes}.

All eight final-development records seal before any target-dependent
final-development field is loaded or canonical score computed. The terminal
controller starts only at the fixed gate after the maximum reserved
final-development window and only if the batch has sealed; otherwise the
registered stop or abort rule applies. All eight terminal records likewise
seal before terminal target-dependent fields or canonical scoring. Isolated
mechanical scoring is then permitted inside each completed batch's archive.
No outcome-bearing content or derivative leaves either archive until all
sixteen records seal. Final-development content, provider-plane signals,
and duration-dependent gate messages remain unavailable to the terminal
controller; terminal questions and results remain unavailable to the
final-development controller.

Under the completed protocol, full-solution assessment and trace coding of
shadow, final-development, and terminal submissions begin only after the full
confirmatory sealing point. This includes human review, model-assisted
checking, and derived assessment/coding fields, not just their disclosure.
Shadow canonical scoring and summaries also wait until this point. An abort
follows the partial-record rule below. No final-development or terminal
outcome may trigger another release. If an outcome-blind mapping of terminal
requirements to motif types is used, complete it in the isolated reference
archive after execution seals and before opening any terminal output or
adjudication for analysis or assessment, as required by
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes}.

If a global stop makes the full sealing point unreachable, an irrevocable
\emph{abort-sealing certificate} is required before any partial archive is
disclosed, assessed, or coded. Revoke all adaptation write authority, close
the adaptation ledger, seal remaining cancelled or unexecuted status records
where technically possible, and commit all partial archives and access logs.
Same-experiment replacement and rerun are prohibited. The certificate permits
inspection of the partial record but does not complete the confirmatory
endpoint. A pre-episode-8 stop cannot relabel the last release $S_8$; any later
evaluation needs separate registration and an early-stop descriptive label.

\subsection{Fresh-State and Cache Invariant}
\label{subsec:execution-isolation}

Every question--version execution begins from fresh question-local
state and a fresh client-side provider session, with no study-controlled
conversational state carried across cells. Primary confirmatory
executions disable client-side answer-bearing exact-response reuse
across branches and phases. No cache visible to a later question
may contain an evaluation-derived answer, canonical label or score,
action trace, retained derivation, validator output, repair record,
microscope diagnosis, or solution-assessment field. Permitted
generalized procedures enter persistent state only through the
registered admission and release process.

Separate provider projects and credentials enforce the adaptive, shadow,
final-development, and terminal archive boundaries. No answer-bearing thread,
hosted vector store, mutable study-controlled workspace, dashboard,
request-history view, observability stream, alert, quota view, or
outcome-dependent billing signal may be shared across embargoed roles.
External assessors and their tools obey the same restrictions. Before an
embargo lifts, only registered content-independent gate and integrity
messages may leave the archive; checking cannot create an indirect channel
into an active controller.

Provider-native prompt or input caching is retained and recorded
through native usage fields; it is not assumed to replay an answer
or to be namespace-controllable. Deliberate reuse of an earlier
answer may be reported only as a separately labelled secondary
deployment-cache condition, with the reused response identified by
its original request and response commitments. It is not a new
confirmatory execution and is excluded from the primary confirmatory
matrices. Any permissible engineering cache is read-only,
content-addressed, non-answer-bearing, and keyed at least by release
hash, item, branch or run, execution phase, request hash, and cache
policy. Adaptive and shadow namespaces remain disjoint, including
the two $t=1$ executions of $S_0$.

Sealed directories are append-only. Missing artifacts, hash
mismatches, unauthorized access, and prohibited disclosures follow
the deviation rules in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-integrity}; the
affected record is preserved. No sealed artifact is silently
regenerated under its original run identifier.

\subsection{Release Manifests and Replay Comparability}
\label{subsec:execution-manifests}

Each content-addressed release manifest identifies its parent and accepted
diff; candidate provenance, admission decisions and tests; requested
model/provider, decoding and retry configuration and returned-identifier
acceptance rule; runtime component versions; active scoped store objects,
canonical store hash, retrieval-index and source-store hashes, and interface
hash; development budget and stopping limits; common evidence/evaluation
contracts; and release time, rationale, deviations and resource links.

Store serialization uses stable identifier order. Added, replaced, revoked
and unused objects remain traceable; store, index, interface, prompt,
fallback and budget changes remain distinguishable. Stores need not be
nested, and size is not useful coverage. Manifest links preserve provenance
without granting runtime access to restricted evidence or reference-derived
fixtures.

The separate evaluation manifest fixes the reference and canonicalization
procedures, numerical endpoint, solution contract, assessment rubric,
assessor roles/configurations, allowed evidence and tools, common checking
cap, reconciliation, blinding, disclosure and unresolved-case rules.
Runtime releases cannot change it. Per-record findings and actual checking
resources are later observations.

A replay is client-configuration-equivalent only when its frozen
release hash, model-visible artifacts, runtime budget, evidence
interface, cache policy, and requested provider configuration match.
Canonical-score comparability additionally requires the same frozen
evaluation-manifest hash. Full-solution-assessment comparability
requires the same evidence contract, rubric, checking cap, and
adjudication rules, with actual evidence access and assessor
configurations recorded. Missing evidence or unresolved judgments
remain visible rather than being dropped to manufacture comparability.

Official same-item replay under $B_t$ and final-development replay under
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ need not be configuration-equivalent. Matching the
client configuration does not imply equal stochastic realizations or fixed
hidden provider weights, routing, and serving kernels.

Actual returned model and request identifiers, timestamps, usage, and
controllable-seed status belong to execution records. Report disagreements
with their configuration and timing rather than silently attributing them to a store change. Exact cached
responses remain labelled reuse and never count as new primary draws.

\section{Trace-Supported Failure and Derivation Coding}
\label{app:audit-microscopes}

This appendix distinguishes three post-seal functions. The
\emph{development microscope} proposes explanations and possible
revisions. \emph{Trace coding} classifies observable events and
documented defects. \emph{Full-solution assessment} applies the
frozen derivation rubric to the submitted solution under
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication}. Their records are
linked, but a microscope diagnosis is not an adjudicated validity
verdict and a trace code does not replace either correctness
readout. Role overlap is disclosed with the applicable access,
independence, and resource restrictions.

\subsection{Microscope Authority and Disclosure Gates}
\label{subsec:audit-authority}

The microscope is a bounded post-seal development instrument. It may inspect
only permitted adaptive artifacts after both first-contact records seal,
the adaptive branch has launched, and the authorized gate opens. Nonlaunch
releases no question, output, trace, reference, or adjudication field to it.
Catastrophic incompleteness follows the registered failure/abort procedure
without fabricated evidence or waived disclosure rules.

The manifest freezes the microscope's model/provider identifiers, prompts,
tools, permitted inputs, output schema, call/token and tool/runtime limits,
and write authority. It cannot alter a sealed submission, its source release,
or the evaluation contract, or write directly to the store. Calls, human
interventions, tests, and reconciliation are logged and charged. Explanations
it constructs remain post-seal artifacts.

The gates in Section~\ref{subsec:execution-confirmatory} also govern microscope
access, human/model review, and trace coding. Future development items,
terminal material, and outcome-bearing shadow artifacts remain inaccessible
before their authorized release. Authorized replay findings for $t<8$ may
inform later episodes only; episode-8 replay is evaluation-only after
adaptation closure. Shadow and confirmatory assessment and coding wait for
all sixteen confirmatory seals or the irrevocable abort certificate.
Post-embargo findings cannot trigger another release or replace a submission.

\subsection{Numerical Outcomes, Derivation Judgments, and Audit Codes}
\label{subsec:audit-endpoints}

Numerical outcomes use the canonical target and loss in
Equations~\ref{eq:pilot-canonical-target}--\ref{eq:pilot-item-loss}, with the
no-action and $\bot$ rules of Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-retry-policy}.
Derivation judgments and combined statuses use
Equations~\ref{eq:pilot-derivation-assessment}--\ref{eq:pilot-solution-status}.
Missing evidence is not invalidity, and no detected defect is not a validity
verdict.

Coding distinguishes retained claims from discarded, superseded and repaired
work. It locates defects and affected dependencies without adding partial
credit. A claimed repair requires the sealed replacement or justification
and recomputation or revalidation of surviving dependents, as required by
the solution contract.

Trace findings and microscope hypotheses do not overwrite scores,
eligibility or derivation judgments. A discrepancy enters the registered
checking and reconciliation procedure, preserving earlier records. Work
used for a full-solution judgment counts against $B_{\mathrm{assess}}$ even
when also called trace coding; a different label cannot enlarge the cap.
Corrections are dated supplements.

\subsection{Frozen Trace-Supported Taxonomy}
\label{subsec:audit-taxonomy}

The codebook separates substantive defects from dispositions,
outcome transitions, and evidence limitations. Its substantive
categories are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item solver target- or subgoal-declaration error;
    \item motif retrieval or selection error;
    \item graph composition or formula-order error;
    \item variable-role or semantic-binding error;
    \item applicability or precondition error;
    \item unit, scale, precision, schema, or structured-specification
    error;
    \item deterministic-execution, raw-answer rendering, or
    answer-packaging error;
    \item runtime-validator or routing error, when that channel is
    present in the frozen release;
    \item an identified false retained claim, invalid inference,
    contradictory retained state, or broken dependency; and
    \item repair-integrity error, including a surviving dependency
    on a superseded or invalid value without the required correction
    or revalidation.
\end{enumerate}

Each substantive code requires a located event or claim and the
evidence supporting the classification. Codes do not assert an
internal mental state, latent knowledge deficit, or missing
information in model weights. Failure to retrieve a useful packet
is not automatically a retrieval error: the code must identify the
relevant trace-supported availability, matching, or selection
condition that was violated. An absent justification is coded as
an evidence limitation unless checking establishes a substantive
defect.

The form separately records:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item repair attempts, fallback, deferral, escalation, cap
    exhaustion, and terminal dispositions;
    \item designated replay transitions in canonical status and
    assessed full-solution status, including their direction;
    \item reference, canonicalizer, adjudication, eligibility, or
    ill-posed-item issues, with the responsible review record;
    \item infrastructure failures, nonlaunch, incomplete execution,
    and protocol deviations; and
    \item missing evidence, unassessable dependencies, unresolved
    disagreements, and canonical failures without a supported
    substantive classification.
\end{itemize}
These fields are not substantive defect codes.

Multiple substantive codes may apply. Each carries its event or
dependency identifier and retained, discarded, superseded, or
repaired status. For execution-level indexing, the primary defect
code is the earliest supported substantive code under the frozen
event ordering and tie rule; all other supported codes are secondary.
For summaries of the submitted solution, a separate primary
\emph{retained} defect is selected only from defects still supporting
that solution. These are annotation conventions, not identified
causal orderings. If no substantive category is supported for a
canonical failure, it remains unclassified.

\subsection{Coding Procedure, Coverage, and Disagreement}
\label{subsec:audit-coding-procedure}

The accounting seal freezes the codebook, coding form, trace-order
and tie rules, coder roles, permitted evidence and tools, checking
limits, independence requirements, reconciliation procedure, and
any overlap with the operator, assessor, or reference custodian.
For each auditable record, two coders independently make initial
substantive assignments on the permitted evidence before exchanging
judgments. Their identities or model configurations, prior access,
evidence packets, initial codes, disagreements, reconciliation,
and unresolved fields are retained.

Where possible, the first evidence review masks branch and release
identity, comparative outcomes, and microscope diagnoses. Necessary
unmasking and prior exposure are recorded. If a coder also performs
derivation assessment, that assessment obeys its separate rubric,
budget, and first-pass rule before the canonical verdict is joined.
Microscope proposals remain labelled hypotheses; a coder must
identify supporting evidence before adopting a substantive code.
Unresolved coder disagreement remains visible and cannot be settled
by choosing the label that makes a revision look successful.

Coding is attempted for every launched terminal action, including
canonically correct, canonically incorrect, target-ineligible, and
analysis-invalid records for which evidence survives, and for every
protocol deviation. The coding ledger records scheduled
cells, available actions and traces, completed coding, and missing
or insufficient evidence. These audit-completion counts are distinct
from answer-release coverage and from useful procedural coverage
$a_{i,t}(q)$ in the idealized model of
Section~\ref{subsec:verified-procedural-growth}.
Nonlaunch and catastrophic incompleteness
are audited through their available control records without
inventing a derivation. Numerical status is not imputed when the
reference or eligibility contract does not support it.

The audit covers adaptive first contacts, official replays, final-development,
terminal and post-embargo shadow records, their designated transitions, and
protocol deviations, under Section~\ref{subsec:audit-authority}. Missing or
unauditable derivation evidence does not remove an otherwise eligible
execution from either correctness denominator. A trace-auditable subset is
only a descriptive coding subset; report actual category denominators and
release-specific observability.

Evaluation-issue codes do not decide loss or inclusion: authorized roles
apply the frozen eligibility and deviation rules. Initial and reconciled
codes seal separately from execution evidence; corrections are dated
supplements. All coding and checking work is charged, with shared events
counted once.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsection{Interpreting Reuse and Replay Findings}
\label{subsec:audit-interpretation}

The reuse audit links procedure availability, retrieval and ordering,
route selection, scope, binding, checks, execution, and retention.
It distinguishes selection of a stored procedure or the baseline
route before step execution from any subsequent registered repair
or fallback. For each link it distinguishes an observed event, an
evidence-supported judgment, an unresolved question, and a proposed
explanation. Claims about relevant admitted support
on terminal items use the registered outcome-blind relevance mapping
in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes}; procedural-use claims link to
the sealed traces under Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-reuse-evidence}.
A larger store or a successful final number alone does not establish
improved useful coverage.

An incorrectly bound stored procedure or violated precondition, after an
assessed valid prefix, can supply evidence against correct use in that
invocation. Neither a
replay score change nor absence of a coded defect establishes which model
premise held or failed.

Record numerical and assessed full-solution transitions separately between
the designated sealed actions. Unresolved-to-pass is not confirmed error
correction. Under stochastic
execution, these transitions alone identify neither repair, forgetting,
interference, transfer nor a causal revision effect.

A located defect, proposed remedy and later assessed valid use can provide
descriptive evidence consistent with procedural reuse. The audit does not
reconstruct the unsupported continuation at the same state or estimate stage probabilities
and Equation~\ref{eq:reuse-quantitative-gain} from trace flags.

Microscope-motivated revisions follow the proposal, isolation, admission,
testing, and costing rules in Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-development-episode}.

\subsection{Resource-Event Ledger}
\label{subsec:ledger-resources}

One row represents one instrumented, physically executed atomic event at
the finest registered grain for which native usage is reported or
measured. Composite workflows link their child events without duplicating
their resource vectors. Each event at or after the
prospective accounting seal receives exactly one immutable scope:
\begin{equation}
\texttt{physical\_scope}
\in\{\texttt{adapt},\texttt{eval},\texttt{deploy},\texttt{over}\}.
\end{equation}

The scope rule assigns:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \texttt{adapt}: adaptive first contact and authorized reference release,
scoring and assessment available to development, diagnosis, human revision
work, candidate construction, admission/release tests, and official replays
whose results can influence a later registered episode;
\item \texttt{eval}: embargoed shadow execution, replays that cannot inform a
later release (including episode 8), final-development and terminal runs,
and their scoring, assessment, and coding;
\item \texttt{deploy}: actual uses of a fixed release outside adaptation and
evaluation, with setup, index loading, runtime hosting, operational maintenance
or governance, storage, and teardown assigned to that deployment cohort;
\item \texttt{over}: in-boundary registration, reference/evaluator and rubric
construction, conformance tests, manifest production, audit logging, archive
maintenance, integrity checks, and analysis not attached to another scope.
\end{itemize}
Solution construction within an execution is runtime work; post-seal checking
is assigned according to its authorized use. Shared physical events appear
once. Protocol design and other work before the accounting seal remain
historical, with unknown or partly observed quantities left unknown; all
in-boundary work after that seal is prospectively charged.

Failed revisions, rejected/deferred/ambiguous candidates, unsuccessful tests,
exhausted retries, and admitted artifacts never used remain charged when
their work occurs. A mechanically ineligible candidate may stop before
substantive certification; an eligible candidate whose certificate is
consulted incurs that cost regardless of verdict. Outcome-dependent resource
fields obey their archive's embargo. Physical scope never changes because evidence is subsequently reused
or improperly disclosed; the separate analytical-use attribution below
records that use and any downstream contamination.

\subsection{Registration Manifest}
\label{subsec:ledger-registration}

Registration has two content-hashed stages. Before reference audit, the
accounting seal freezes $S_0$, the candidate pool, rules and seeds for grouping,
eligibility, exclusion, reserves, selection, allocation, and order; reference
and canonicalization procedures; evidence, assessment, and coding contracts;
schemas, permissions, accounting, and immutable outer caps. Reserved evaluation
resources and $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ are fixed then. Development $B_t$ can change
only through releases within those bounds.

Before the first prospective item is revealed, the execution manifest
reaffirms those fields and $S_0$ by hash without alteration. It adds only the
eligible pool, reference records, canonical-contract hash, selected items,
allocation, and order produced by the frozen procedures and locked forms.
Reference inspection can affect these outputs only through their recorded
judgment and reconciliation fields, not discretionary new rules or seeds.

The timestamped manifest is archived through an immutable third-party deposit
or an independently custodied signed and encrypted commitment. Manifest and
decryption material remain controlled until scheduled disclosure, when the
deposited bytes, signature, timestamp, and disclosed manifest must reconcile.
Public commitments to low-entropy fields such as item identities or order
use a keyed construction or high-entropy secret salt withheld through the
embargo; an unsalted hash does not conceal them.

The combined registration record incorporates the full requirements and
required fields at the following locations. All prespecified choices are
fixed at the accounting seal; only the realized outputs allowed above are added later.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Items and references:}
dataset/exposure census, grouping, pool flow, eligibility, reserves, selection,
allocation/order algorithms and seeds, realized sets and isolated reference
audit (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-question-sets}).
\item \textbf{Releases, authority and budgets:}
$S_0$, history, invariants, revision/admission timing and permissions;
immutable caps, exhaustion/global stops, reserved evaluation allocation and
field-by-field $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$ with endpoint configuration/mode hashes
(Sections~\ref{subsec:pilot-frozen-releases},
\ref{subsec:pilot-development-episode} and~\ref{subsec:execution-manifests}).
\item \textbf{Numerical contract:}
references, public metadata, scorer/renderer, conformance, actual exponent
bounds, six-digit loss, $K$, $\log_2(K-1)$, conditional $\Psi_K$ curve and
all registered sensitivities (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-question-sets}
and the renderer requirements below).
\item \textbf{Solution and assessment:}
evidence/schema, rubric, roles/configurations, tools, $B_{\mathrm{assess}}$,
blinding, checking, reconciliation, cutoff/process states, unresolved judgments
and corrections (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-solution-adjudication}).
\item \textbf{Execution and isolation:}
launch, limits/retries/failures, quarantine, fresh state/cache, branch balance,
fixed slots/quotas/padded gates, provider/archive separation, immutable evidence,
episode-8 closure, confirmatory/abort gates, access and comparability
(Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-retry-policy} and Appendix~\ref{app:execution-replay}).
\item \textbf{Development and audit instruments:}
permitted corpora, synthetic/revision tests, repository/network egress and
logging (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-development-episode}); microscope,
codebook, forms, two independent initial assignments, ordering, evidence,
budgets, role overlap and reconciliation (Appendix~\ref{app:audit-microscopes}).
\item \textbf{Outcomes and inclusion:}
both readouts, common executed/paired sets, coverage, all planned summaries,
unresolved/paired ranges, mandatory pessimistic eligible planned-cell numerical
summary, any full-solution completion range and outcome-blind relevance mapping
(Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-outcomes}); finite deviation map and consistency
checks (Sections~\ref{subsec:ledger-deviations}
and~\ref{subsec:ledger-consistency}); inferential limits
(Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-scope}).
\item \textbf{Records and physical accounting:}
concrete schemas, versioning, missingness, visibility and required ledger
information (Tables~\ref{tab:version-revision-ledger}--\ref{tab:trace-coding-ledger});
instrumentation, atomic events, native units, scopes, completeness, charges,
cohort time/cutoff, reconciliation and all conditions for any optional
valuations, policy attributions or lifecycle scenarios
(Section~\ref{subsec:ledger-resources} and Table~\ref{tab:resource-event-ledger}).
\end{enumerate}
The frozen forms, algorithms, schemas, rubric, numerical parameter values,
caps and configuration artifacts must be supplied by registration before
execution, not left as unresolved implementation choices.
The manifest also provides the jointly measurable representative
renderer $\iota(q,z)=\overline\iota(u(q),z)$ with
$\iota(q,\bot)=\varnothing$ and
$c_q(\iota(q,z))=z$ for every registered $q$ and label $z$.
Construction uses only public $u(q)$ and the requested label, never a
reference, hidden target field, substantive solver, or item-specific range.
The renderer is conformance-tested against this registered contract.
The construction supplies canonical numerical representatives; it does
not establish same-budget confidence completion, predictive-family
membership, or preservation of a source policy's preterminal experiment
for every policy and $0<\delta<1$.

The reference audit uses isolated roles, locked forms, blinding,
reconciliation, minimum eligible-cluster and reserve-exhaustion rules.
Pre-execution conformance failures and successor registration follow
Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-registration}; they do not reset the original
accounting boundary.

\subsection{Cross-Ledger Consistency Requirements}
\label{subsec:ledger-consistency}

Before publication, the registered checks enforce these relations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
\item Nonlaunch and catastrophic incompleteness have no terminal action,
ordinary loss, or formal full-solution assessment. A sealed no-answer is an
action with a false answer-presence flag. Structurally inapplicable replays do not
inflate scheduled or missing-cell counts.
\item External scoring, assessment, coding, and corrections link to the
earlier execution commitment without rewriting its retained graph.
Availability and access permission remain separate. Provenance links
grant no additional access, and authorized abort disclosure follows
the registered partial-record procedure.
\item Full-solution status follows the registered mapping. Canonical
failure or an established retained defect settles failure, even if other
checking is pending. Canonical pass with unassessable derivation or pending
assessment remains unresolved; its process state and cutoff remain visible.
Absence of a trace defect does not establish validity.
\item Both readouts retain the common cell sets and item-level eligibility.
Unresolved derivations do not create exclusions. Summaries identify their
record versions, counts, and unavailable outcomes.
\item Each physical event has one immutable scope and cutoff cohort,
and one active value per coordinate or charge in an as-of total.
Repeated references and superseded records cannot duplicate consumption.
\item Proposals, releases, assessments, coding passes, and deviations
have provenance and resource links. Failed, rejected, unused, and
reconciliation work remains charged when physically performed.

\item Optional lifecycle-value scenarios identify their comparator,
cost boundary, valuation, reuse horizon, parameter provenance, and
premises. Derived results reconcile to the stated inputs; assumed
future uses create no physical events and never alter observed
resource totals.
\end{itemize}
